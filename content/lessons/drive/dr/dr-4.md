## Objectives

- Understand the legal status of the **Highway Code**: **MUST** vs **should**.
- Know the **hierarchy of road users** and rules **H1–H3** (2022 update).
- Apply the most important basic rules from day one.

## Explanation

### MUST vs should (link with GR5!)

- Rules with **MUST / MUST NOT** are **legal requirements** – breaking them is an **offence** (the rule cites the law).
- Rules with **should / should not / do / do not** are **advice**. Not following them is not an offence in itself, but it **can be used in evidence** in court (e.g. to show careless driving).

:::es
**MUST / MUST NOT** = **obligación legal** (incumplir = infracción). **should / do / do not** = **consejo**; no es delito por sí solo, pero **puede usarse como prueba** (p. ej. de conducción descuidada).
:::

### The hierarchy of road users (2022)

Road users who can do the **greatest harm** have the **greatest responsibility** to reduce danger to others. In order of vulnerability: **pedestrians** (especially children, older and disabled people) → **cyclists** → **horse riders** → **motorcyclists** → **cars/taxis** → **vans/minibuses** → **large passenger vehicles and HGVs**.

- **Rule H1** – the hierarchy and responsibility principle.
- **Rule H2** – at a **junction**, drivers, motorcyclists, horse riders and cyclists should **give way to pedestrians crossing or waiting to cross** a road you're **turning into or from**; and give way to pedestrians on **zebra crossings** and people **waiting** to cross them.
- **Rule H3** – don't **cut across** cyclists, horse riders or horse-drawn vehicles **going ahead** when you're **turning** into or out of a junction or changing lane.

:::es
**Jerarquía (2022)**: quien puede causar **más daño** tiene **más responsabilidad**. De más vulnerable a menos: peatones → ciclistas → jinetes → motoristas → coches → furgonetas → autobuses y camiones.
- **H2**: al girar en un cruce, **cede el paso a los peatones** que cruzan o esperan para cruzar la calle a la que entras o de la que sales.
- **H3**: no cortes el paso a ciclistas que siguen recto cuando giras.
:::

### Basic rules to know now

- **Seat belts**: you **MUST** wear one (with limited exemptions); the driver is responsible for **children under 14**.
- **Mobile phones**: you **MUST NOT** hold and use a phone while driving (see DR9).
- **Speed limits**: **MUST NOT** exceed them (DR8).
- **Traffic lights**: **MUST** stop at red; **amber** alone means stop unless it's unsafe to do so (no flashing amber "go" like in some Spanish junctions, except at **pelican crossings**).
- **Pedestrian crossings**: **zebra** (give way to people on it), **pelican/puffin** (lights), **toucan** (pedestrians and cyclists).
- **Headlights**: dipped headlights when visibility is seriously reduced.

:::es
Normas básicas: **cinturón** obligatorio (el conductor responde de los **menores de 14**); **móvil** en la mano prohibido; **límites** de velocidad; **semáforo** rojo = parar, **ámbar** = parar salvo que no sea seguro; **pasos de peatones**: *zebra*, *pelican*, *puffin*, *toucan*.
:::

## Scenarios

1. You turn left into a side road and a pedestrian is waiting to cross it. → **Give way** to them (rule H2).
2. You turn left and a cyclist is going straight on, on your left. → **Don't cut across** them; wait (rule H3).
3. Amber light just as you arrive and it's safe to stop. → **Stop**.

## Common mistakes (drivers from Spain)

- Not expecting to give way to pedestrians at side roads (H2 is newer and stronger than many drivers think).
- Treating "should" rules as unimportant – they can be used as **evidence**.

## Summary

1. MUST = law; should = advice that can be used in evidence.
2. Hierarchy: greatest harm → greatest responsibility.
3. H2: give way to pedestrians crossing/waiting at junctions.
4. H3: don't cut across cyclists going ahead.
5. Seat belts, phones, speed, lights: legal requirements.
