## Objectives

- Read UK **road markings** for waiting, parking and loading.
- Understand **white lines** on the road: centre lines, hazard lines, double white lines.
- Recognise markings near **crossings, junctions and bus stops**.

## Explanation

### Yellow lines (along the edge of the road)

| Marking | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Double yellow lines** | **No waiting at any time** (unless a sign shows seasonal restrictions) |
| **Single yellow line** | No waiting during the **times shown on nearby signs** |
| **Yellow "blips" on the kerb** | **Loading restrictions** (times on signs) – one blip / two blips |
| **Red lines** (red routes, mostly in London) | **No stopping** (double red: at any time) |

"Waiting" restrictions usually still allow **picking up/setting down passengers** briefly; **loading** may be allowed unless kerb blips say otherwise (⚠ VERIFICAR excepciones y blue badge rules).

:::es
- **Doble línea amarilla**: prohibido esperar **en todo momento**.
- **Línea amarilla simple**: prohibido esperar en el **horario** indicado en señales cercanas.
- **Marcas cortas en el bordillo**: restricciones de **carga/descarga**.
- **Líneas rojas** (sobre todo Londres): prohibido **parar**.
:::

### White lines (along the road)

- **Short broken centre line** – normal; you may cross to overtake if safe.
- **Hazard warning line** – **longer dashes, shorter gaps**: danger ahead (bend, junction) – don't cross unless you can see the road is clear.
- **Double white lines, solid nearest to you** – you **MUST NOT** cross or straddle (with limited exceptions, e.g. to pass a stationary vehicle or a cyclist/horse moving at 10 mph or less).
- **Double white lines, broken nearest to you** – you may cross to overtake if safe and you can finish before the solid line.
- **Edge lines** and **hatched areas** (diagonal stripes): avoid driving on hatched areas; if bordered by a **solid** line you **must not** enter except in an emergency.

:::es
- **Línea discontinua corta**: normal.
- **Línea de peligro** (trazos más largos): peligro, no cruces salvo que veas despejado.
- **Doble línea blanca con continua de tu lado**: **prohibido** cruzarla (salvo excepciones).
- **Cebreados**: evítalos; si están rodeados de línea **continua**, prohibido entrar salvo emergencia.
:::

### Other markings

- **Zig-zags** at crossings (white) and outside schools (**yellow**, "SCHOOL KEEP CLEAR"): **no stopping/parking**.
- **Bus stop** markings and **bus lanes** (solid white line, "BUS LANE", times on signs).
- **Box junction** (yellow criss-cross) – DR3.
- **"SLOW"**, **"KEEP CLEAR"**, arrows for lanes.
- **Parking bays**: **disabled** (Blue Badge), **permit holders**, **pay and display**, **loading only**.

## Scenarios

1. You want to stop for 5 minutes on a **single yellow line** at 10:00; the sign says "Mon–Sat 8am–6pm". → **Not allowed** (unless an exception applies).
2. You see **long dashes** in the centre line approaching a bend. → **Hazard line**: don't overtake.

## Common mistakes (drivers from Spain)

- Assuming "just a minute" is fine on double yellows.
- Not reading the **time plate** for single yellow lines.
- Parking on yellow **zig-zags** outside schools at pick-up time.

## Summary

1. Double yellow = no waiting at any time; single yellow = times on sign.
2. Kerb blips = loading restrictions; red lines = no stopping.
3. Hazard line = longer dashes = danger.
4. Double white with solid nearest you = MUST NOT cross.
5. Zig-zags (white and yellow): no stopping.
