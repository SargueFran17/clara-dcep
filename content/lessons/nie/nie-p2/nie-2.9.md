## Objectives

- Know the statutory **definition of domestic abuse** (Domestic Abuse Act 2021, ss. 1–3) and **"personally connected"**.
- Understand the offence of **controlling or coercive behaviour** (Serious Crime Act 2015, s. 76).
- Know the main **protective tools**: DVPN/DVPO (and DAPN/DAPO), **DVDS (Clare's Law)**, **DASH** and **MARAC**, and good investigative practice (evidence-led prosecutions).

## Explanation

### 1. The definition (DAA 2021, s. 1)

Behaviour of A towards B is **domestic abuse** if:
- **A and B are each aged 16 or over** and are **personally connected** to each other, **and**
- the behaviour is **abusive**: **physical or sexual abuse**; **violent or threatening behaviour**; **controlling or coercive behaviour**; **economic abuse**; **psychological, emotional or other abuse**.

It can be a **single incident** or a **course of conduct**. **Economic abuse** = behaviour that substantially affects B's ability to **acquire, use or maintain money or other property**, or **obtain goods or services**.

**Personally connected** (s. 2): they are or have been **married** / **civil partners**; have **agreed to marry** / enter a civil partnership; are or have been in an **intimate personal relationship**; each has or had a **parental relationship** in relation to the same child; or are **relatives**.

**Children** (s. 3): a child who **sees, hears or experiences the effects** of domestic abuse and is related to A or B is a **victim** in their own right.

:::es
**Violencia doméstica (DAA 2021 s. 1)**: A y B **mayores de 16** y **personalmente vinculados**, y conducta **abusiva**: abuso **físico o sexual**, conducta **violenta o amenazante**, **controladora o coercitiva**, **abuso económico**, abuso **psicológico/emocional**. Puede ser **un solo hecho**. **Vinculados (s. 2)**: matrimonio/pareja civil (actual o pasada), compromiso, **relación íntima**, ser progenitores del **mismo hijo**, o **familiares**. **Menores (s. 3)** que ven, oyen o sufren los efectos = **víctimas**.
:::

### 2. Controlling or coercive behaviour (SCA 2015, s. 76)

An offence where A:
- **repeatedly or continuously** engages in behaviour towards B that is **controlling or coercive**;
- at the time they are **personally connected** (since the 2021 amendments this includes **ex-partners** and family members **whether or not they live together** ⚠);
- the behaviour has a **serious effect** on B: it causes B to **fear, on at least two occasions, that violence will be used**, **or** it causes **serious alarm or distress** which has a **substantial adverse effect on B's usual day-to-day activities**;
- A **knows or ought to know** it will have a serious effect.

**Defence**: A believed he was acting in B's **best interests** and the behaviour was **reasonable** in all the circumstances – **not available** where B was caused to **fear violence**. **Either-way**; maximum **5 years** (⚠).

Examples: monitoring phone/location, controlling money, isolating from friends and family, deciding what B wears/eats, threats, humiliation.

:::es
**Conducta controladora o coercitiva (SCA 2015 s. 76)**: de forma **repetida o continua**, con una persona **vinculada** (ahora también **ex-parejas** y familiares aunque no convivan ⚠), con **efecto grave**: **miedo a la violencia en al menos dos ocasiones** o **alarma o angustia grave** que afecta **sustancialmente a su vida diaria**; y A **sabe o debería saber** que tendrá ese efecto. **Defensa**: creía actuar en su **interés** y era **razonable** – **no** si causó miedo a la violencia. Ejemplos: vigilar el móvil, controlar el dinero, aislar, decidir la ropa, humillar.
:::

### 3. Protective tools

- **Domestic Violence Protection Notice (DVPN)** (Crime and Security Act 2010, ss. 24–33): authorised by an officer of at least **superintendent** rank; bans the perpetrator from contacting the victim / returning home; an application for a **Domestic Violence Protection Order (DVPO)** must be heard by **magistrates** within **48 hours**; a DVPO lasts **14 to 28 days** (⚠ VERIFICAR).
- **Domestic Abuse Protection Notice/Order (DAPN/DAPO)** – introduced by the DAA 2021 to **replace** DVPN/DVPO (and some other orders), with longer durations and positive requirements; being **rolled out** (⚠ VERIFICAR estado en 2027).
- **Domestic Violence Disclosure Scheme (Clare's Law)** – "**right to ask**" (a person can ask about a partner's history of abuse) and "**right to know**" (police can proactively disclose).
- **DASH** risk assessment → **MARAC** for **high-risk** victims; **IDVA** support.
- Other options: **restraining orders**, **non-molestation orders** (breach is an offence), stalking protection orders.

:::es
- **DVPN** (CSA 2010): la autoriza un **superintendente**; prohíbe contacto/volver a casa; el **juzgado** debe ver la **DVPO** en **48 h**; dura **14–28 días** (⚠).
- **DAPN/DAPO** (DAA 2021): las sustituyen, con más duración y requisitos positivos (implantación por fases ⚠).
- **Clare's Law (DVDS)**: **derecho a preguntar** y **derecho a saber** sobre el historial violento de la pareja.
- **DASH** → **MARAC** (alto riesgo) e **IDVA**; además **órdenes de alejamiento** y **non-molestation orders**.
:::

### 4. Investigation – good practice

- **Positive action**: secure safety, deal with offenders, safeguard children.
- **Evidence-led prosecutions** – build the case so it doesn't depend only on the victim's statement: **body-worn video**, **999 recordings**, **res gestae** statements, photos of injuries, neighbours, medical records, digital evidence.
- Consider **non-fatal strangulation** (2.7), **stalking/harassment** (2.8), **sexual offences**, **criminal damage**, **threats to kill**.
- Victims may **retract**: understand the dynamics of abuse; don't blame.

:::es
**Buenas prácticas**: actuar con **firmeza** (seguridad, detener, proteger a los menores) y **procesamientos basados en pruebas** que no dependan solo de la víctima: **cámara corporal**, **grabación del 999**, *res gestae*, fotos, vecinos, informes médicos, pruebas digitales. Considera otros delitos (estrangulamiento, acoso, sexuales…). Las víctimas pueden **retractarse**: entiende la dinámica, **sin culpar**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The money.** A husband takes his wife's salary, gives her £10 a week and checks every receipt; she has stopped seeing friends and is constantly anxious. → **Economic abuse**; potentially **s. 76** (serious alarm/distress affecting day-to-day activities).
2. **The ex.** A man who split from his partner a year ago keeps tracking her phone and turning up at her work, making her fear violence. → Since 2021 **s. 76** can apply to ex-partners (⚠); also consider **stalking**.
3. **The 999 call.** A victim calls 999 screaming "he's got a knife!", then refuses to give a statement. → **Evidence-led prosecution**: the 999 call (possible **res gestae**), BWV, injuries.
4. **New partner.** A woman asks police if her new partner has a history of violence. → **Clare's Law – right to ask**.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Both parties must be **16+** for the statutory definition (children are victims under s. 3).
- **Personally connected** includes **relatives** and people with a **parental relationship** to the same child.
- s. 76: **repeated or continuous** + **serious effect** (fear of violence **at least twice**, or serious alarm/distress with **substantial adverse effect**) + **knows or ought to know**.
- Best-interests defence **not** available if fear of violence was caused.
- DVPN authorised by a **superintendent**; DVPO hearing within **48 hours**.

## Summary

1. DAA 2021: 16+, personally connected, abusive behaviour (5 types incl. economic); children who see/hear are victims.
2. s. 76 SCA 2015: controlling/coercive behaviour, repeated/continuous, serious effect, knows/ought to know.
3. DVPN (superintendent) → DVPO (magistrates within 48 h, 14–28 days); DAPN/DAPO replacing them (⚠).
4. Clare's Law (right to ask / right to know); DASH → MARAC; IDVA.
5. Evidence-led prosecutions: BWV, 999, res gestae, injuries, digital evidence.
