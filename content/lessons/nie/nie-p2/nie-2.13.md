## Objectives

- Know the **Public Order Act 1986** offences: **riot** (s. 1), **violent disorder** (s. 2), **affray** (s. 3), **fear or provocation of violence** (s. 4), **intentional harassment, alarm or distress** (s. 4A) and **harassment, alarm or distress** (s. 5).
- Know the **mental element** (s. 6) and the **dwelling** exception.
- Know powers over **processions and assemblies** (ss. 11–14), **breach of the peace** and newer protest offences (Public Order Act 2023).

## Explanation

### 1. Riot, violent disorder, affray

| Offence | Number | Conduct | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Riot** (s. 1) | **12 or more** present together | **use or threaten** unlawful violence for a **common purpose**; conduct (taken together) would cause a **person of reasonable firmness present at the scene** to fear for their personal safety; each person **using** violence for the common purpose is guilty | 10 yrs; **DPP consent** |
| **Violent disorder** (s. 2) | **3 or more** present together | use or threaten unlawful violence; same "reasonable firmness" test | 5 yrs |
| **Affray** (s. 3) | **one** person is enough | **uses or threatens** unlawful violence **towards another**; reasonable firmness test; **a threat cannot be made by words alone** | 3 yrs |

- For all three: **no person of reasonable firmness need actually be, or be likely to be, present** at the scene; they may be committed in **private as well as in public**.
- Affray: there must be a **person towards whom** violence is used/threatened (*I v DPP* – carrying petrol bombs, not brandished, not affray).

:::es
**Disturbios (s. 1)**: **12 o más**, violencia con **propósito común**, una persona de **firmeza razonable** temería por su seguridad; 10 años; consentimiento del **DPP**. **Desorden violento (s. 2)**: **3 o más**; 5 años. **Riña (affray, s. 3)**: **una** persona usa o amenaza violencia **hacia otra**; la amenaza **no** puede ser **solo con palabras**; 3 años. En los tres **no hace falta** que haya alguien presente y pueden ocurrir en **privado**.
:::

### 2. Sections 4, 4A and 5

- **s. 4 – fear or provocation of violence**: uses towards another **threatening, abusive or insulting** words or behaviour (or distributes/displays such material), **intending** to cause that person to **believe immediate unlawful violence** will be used against him or another, or to **provoke** immediate violence, or whereby that person is **likely** to believe/provoked. Max 6 months (either-way when racially aggravated ⚠).
- **s. 4A – intentional harassment, alarm or distress**: **threatening, abusive or insulting** words/behaviour/display, **with intent** to cause HAD, and **causing** a person HAD. Max 6 months. Defence: inside a dwelling and no reason to believe it'd be heard/seen outside; conduct was reasonable.
- **s. 5 – harassment, alarm or distress**: **threatening or abusive** words or behaviour (**"insulting" removed in 2013**), or disorderly behaviour, **within the hearing or sight of a person likely to be caused** HAD. **Fine only**. No one needs to actually be caused HAD (but a person must be there to hear/see – a police officer can be that person, *DPP v Orum*). Defences: no reason to believe a person within hearing/sight would be caused HAD; inside a dwelling; **reasonable** conduct.
- **Dwelling exception** (ss. 4, 4A, 5): no offence where the words/behaviour are used **inside a dwelling** and the other person is **also inside that or another dwelling**.
- **Mental element (s. 6)**: for ss. 4A/5, D **intends** his words/behaviour to be threatening, abusive (or insulting) or **is aware** that they may be; for riot/violent disorder/affray, intends to use/threaten violence or is aware it may be violent. **Voluntary intoxication** is no excuse.

:::es
**s. 4**: palabras/conducta **amenazantes, abusivas o insultantes** con intención de hacer creer que habrá **violencia inmediata** o provocarla. **s. 4A**: con **intención** de causar acoso, alarma o angustia (HAD) **y** causándolo. **s. 5**: **amenazantes o abusivas** (ya **no "insultantes"**) o conducta desordenada **a la vista u oído** de alguien que **probablemente** sufra HAD; solo **multa**; un policía puede ser esa persona. **Excepción de vivienda**: si ambos están **dentro de viviendas**. La embriaguez no excusa.
:::

### 3. Processions and assemblies (POA 1986)

- **s. 11**: **advance written notice** of a public procession (to demonstrate support/opposition, publicise a cause or mark an event) at least **6 clear days** before, unless not reasonably practicable. Organiser's offence if not given or route/time changed.
- **s. 12 / s. 14**: the **senior police officer** may impose **conditions** on a **procession** (s. 12) or **public assembly** (s. 14 – 2 or more persons in a public place wholly/partly open air) to prevent serious public disorder, serious damage to property, serious disruption to the life of the community, intimidation, or (since 2022) **noise** causing serious disruption/significant impact ⚠.
- **s. 13**: a **ban** on processions (chief officer applies to the council with Home Secretary's consent; in London the Commissioner).
- **Trespassory assemblies** (s. 14A).

### 4. Breach of the peace

**Common law** power: arrest (and enter premises) where a breach of the peace is **committed in the officer's presence**, or the officer **reasonably believes** one will be committed **in the immediate future**, or one has been committed and it's reasonably believed a renewal is threatened. A breach of the peace = **harm actually done or likely to be done to a person (or in his presence to his property)**, or a person in fear of such harm (*R v Howell* 1981). It's **not an offence** – the person can be **bound over**.

### 5. Newer protest offences (⚠)

**Public Order Act 2023**: **locking on** (s. 1) and **going equipped** to lock on (s. 2); **causing serious disruption by tunnelling**; **obstructing major transport works**; **interference with key national infrastructure** (s. 7); **Serious Disruption Prevention Orders**; extended **stop and search** powers for protest-related offences (s. 10–11, inspector/superintendent authorisations ⚠). **PCSCA 2022**: statutory offence of **intentionally or recklessly causing public nuisance** (s. 78).

:::es
**Manifestaciones**: **s. 11** aviso por escrito **6 días** antes; **s. 12/14** el mando policial puede poner **condiciones** (desorden grave, daños graves, alteración grave, intimidación, **ruido** desde 2022); **s. 13** prohibición. **Quebrantamiento de la paz** (*common law*): detener si ocurre o es inminente; no es delito (compromiso de buena conducta). **POA 2023**: delitos de **encadenarse** (*locking on*), túneles, infraestructuras clave; **molestia pública** (PCSCA 2022 s. 78).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The pub brawl.** Four men fight in a pub; customers flee. → **Violent disorder** (3+); each also possibly **affray**.
2. **The bottle.** D waves a broken bottle at V in the street. → **Affray** (threat towards another, not words alone) + possibly s. 4.
3. **Shouting at police.** Drunk D shouts abuse and swears at officers in the street at 2 a.m.; residents nearby. → **s. 5** (officer can be the person likely to be caused HAD; consider whether officers were actually likely to be distressed).
4. **The window.** D shouts threats from his flat window at his neighbour in her flat. → **Dwelling exception** – both inside dwellings → not ss. 4/4A/5 (consider harassment/threats offences).
5. **The march.** Organisers give notice 2 days before. → **s. 11** – unless not reasonably practicable.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Riot **12**, violent disorder **3**, affray **1** (towards another, not words alone).
- No person of reasonable firmness needs to be present.
- s. 5: **threatening or abusive** only (not insulting); fine only.
- s. 4A requires **intent and actual** HAD; s. 5 only **likely**.
- Dwelling exception: **both** inside dwellings.

## Summary

1. Riot (12, DPP consent, 10 yrs), violent disorder (3, 5 yrs), affray (1, 3 yrs).
2. s. 4 fear/provocation; s. 4A intentional HAD; s. 5 HAD (threatening/abusive, fine).
3. s. 6 mental element; dwelling exception; intoxication no excuse.
4. Processions/assemblies ss. 11–14; breach of the peace.
5. POA 2023 protest offences; public nuisance (PCSCA 2022).
