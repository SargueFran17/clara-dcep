## Objectives

- Know **harassment** (s. 2) and **putting people in fear of violence** (s. 4) under the **Protection from Harassment Act 1997**, and **stalking** (ss. 2A and 4A).
- Know **course of conduct**, the **defences**, **restraining orders** and **stalking protection orders**.
- Know **harassment of a person in their home** (CJPA 2001, s. 42A), **stirring up hatred** (Public Order Act 1986, Parts 3 and 3A) and **communications offences**.

## Explanation

### 1. Harassment (PHA 1997, ss. 1–2)

A person must not pursue a **course of conduct** which **amounts to harassment** of another and which he **knows or ought to know** amounts to harassment (s. 1). The test of "ought to know": a **reasonable person in possession of the same information** would think it amounted to harassment.

- **Course of conduct** (s. 7): conduct on **at least two occasions** in relation to a single person (or, for harassment of two or more people – s. 1(1A) – conduct on at least one occasion in relation to each, intending to persuade them not to do something they're entitled to do, or to do something they're not obliged to do).
- **Harassment** includes **alarming** a person or causing **distress**; conduct includes **speech**.
- **s. 2**: summary, max **6 months**.
- **Defences** (s. 1(3)): pursued **for the purpose of preventing or detecting crime**; under any **enactment or rule of law**; or **reasonable** in the particular circumstances.

### 2. Putting people in fear of violence (s. 4)

A course of conduct causes another to **fear, on at least two occasions, that violence will be used against them**, and D knows or ought to know it will. Either-way, max **10 years**. Defences: preventing/detecting crime, enactment, or **reasonable for the protection of himself or another or of property**.

:::es
**Acoso (PHA 1997 s. 1–2)**: **curso de conducta** (al menos **2 ocasiones**) que constituye acoso (alarma, angustia; incluye palabras) y que sabe **o debería saber** (persona razonable con la misma información). Sumario, **6 meses**. Defensas: **prevenir/detectar delitos**, **habilitación legal** o **razonable**. **s. 4**: hacer **temer violencia al menos dos veces**; **10 años**.
:::

### 3. Stalking (ss. 2A and 4A)

- **s. 2A**: a course of conduct amounting to **harassment** where the acts **are associated with stalking**, e.g. **following**, **contacting/attempting to contact** by any means, **publishing** statements or material relating to the person, **monitoring** their use of the internet, email or other electronic communication, **loitering** in any place, **interfering with property**, **watching or spying**. Summary, 6 months (51 weeks ⚠).
- **s. 4A**: stalking which causes B to **fear on at least two occasions that violence will be used**, **or** causes **serious alarm or distress** that has a **substantial adverse effect on B's usual day-to-day activities**. Max **10 years**.
- Police powers of **entry and search** (warrant, s. 2B) for s. 2A.
- **Stalking Protection Orders** (Stalking Protection Act 2019): a **chief officer of police** applies to the **magistrates' court**; the order can prohibit and require things; breach is an offence (max 5 yrs ⚠). An **interim** SPO is possible.

:::es
**Stalking (s. 2A)**: acoso con actos típicos – **seguir**, **contactar**, **publicar** sobre la persona, **vigilar** su uso de internet, **merodear**, **interferir** con sus cosas, **espiar**. **s. 4A**: con **miedo a la violencia (2 veces)** o **alarma grave** que afecta **sustancialmente** su vida diaria; **10 años**. **Stalking Protection Order**: la pide el **jefe de policía** al juzgado de magistrados.
:::

### 4. Restraining orders and civil remedies

- **Restraining order on conviction** (Sentencing Act 2020, s. 360) for **any** offence, to protect the victim(s) from conduct amounting to harassment or causing fear of violence.
- **Restraining order on acquittal** (PHA 1997, s. 5A) where necessary to protect a person from harassment.
- Breach without reasonable excuse = offence (max 5 yrs).
- **Civil injunction** (PHA s. 3); breach of the injunction is an offence (s. 3(6)).

### 5. Harassment of a person in their home (CJPA 2001, s. 42A)

D is **present outside or in the vicinity of any premises used as a dwelling**, for the purpose of **representing to or persuading** the resident (or another) that they **should not do** something they're entitled to do, or **should do** something they're not obliged to do, **intending** to harass/alarm/distress (or knowing/ought to know it's likely), and it does or is likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress. Summary (51 weeks ⚠). (Police can also **direct** people to leave the vicinity – s. 42.)

:::es
**Órdenes de alejamiento** al condenar (cualquier delito) o **al absolver** (s. 5A); incumplirlas es delito. **s. 42A CJPA 2001**: acosar a alguien **frente a su casa** para presionarle a hacer o no hacer algo; la policía puede ordenar que se vayan (s. 42).
:::

### 6. Stirring up hatred (Public Order Act 1986)

- **Racial hatred** (Part 3, ss. 18–23): using **threatening, abusive or insulting** words or behaviour, or displaying such written material, **intending** to stir up racial hatred **or** where, having regard to all the circumstances, racial hatred is **likely** to be stirred up. Max **7 years**.
- **Religious hatred and hatred on grounds of sexual orientation** (Part 3A, s. 29A–29N): only **threatening** words/behaviour and **intention** to stir up hatred are covered; there's a **freedom of expression** protection (criticism of religions, discussion of sexual conduct…). Max 7 years.
- **Attorney General's consent** is needed to prosecute.

### 7. Communications offences (⚠)

- **Communications Act 2003, s. 127**: sending by means of a **public electronic communications network** a message that is **grossly offensive** or of an indecent, obscene or **menacing** character.
- **Online Safety Act 2023**: **false communications** (s. 179) and **threatening communications** (s. 181) offences replaced parts of the Malicious Communications Act 1988 and s. 127(2) ⚠.

:::es
**Incitar al odio (POA 1986)**: **racial** – palabras **amenazantes, abusivas o insultantes** con **intención** o **probabilidad** de incitar al odio; **religioso/orientación sexual** – solo **amenazantes** y con **intención**; consentimiento del **Attorney General**; máx. **7 años**. **Comunicaciones**: s. 127 CA 2003 (mensajes **gravemente ofensivos** o **amenazantes**), y los nuevos delitos de la **Online Safety Act 2023** (comunicaciones falsas o amenazantes) ⚠.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The ex-boyfriend.** Turns up at her work twice, messages her friends, tracks her Instagram. → **Stalking (s. 2A)**; if serious alarm with substantial effect on her life → **s. 4A**.
2. **Two threats.** Neighbour threatens to "batter" V on two separate days; V is scared. → **s. 4** (fear of violence on two occasions).
3. **The protest at home.** Protesters outside a scientist's house to make her stop her research. → **s. 42A CJPA 2001**; direction under **s. 42**.
4. **The leaflets.** Leaflets with abusive racist content distributed in a town centre. → **Stirring up racial hatred** (AG consent).
5. **Acquitted but risky.** D acquitted of harassment, but evidence shows a risk. → **Restraining order on acquittal (s. 5A)**.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Course of conduct = **at least two occasions**.
- s. 4: fear of violence **on at least two occasions**.
- s. 4A: **fear of violence (2 occasions)** **or** **serious alarm/distress with substantial adverse effect**.
- Religious/sexual orientation hatred: **threatening** only + **intent**; racial: threatening, abusive **or insulting** + intent **or likely**.
- SPO: applied for by the **chief officer of police**.

## Summary

1. PHA 1997 s. 2 harassment (course of conduct, knows/ought to know); s. 4 fear of violence (10 yrs).
2. Stalking ss. 2A/4A; examples; SPOs (Stalking Protection Act 2019).
