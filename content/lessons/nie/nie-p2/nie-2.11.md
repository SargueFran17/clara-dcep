## Objectives

- Know **false imprisonment** and **kidnapping** (common law) and **hostage-taking** (Taking of Hostages Act 1982).
- Know the **Modern Slavery Act 2015**: **slavery, servitude, forced or compulsory labour** (s. 1), **human trafficking** (s. 2), **exploitation** (s. 3), the **s. 45 defence**, the **duty to notify** and the **National Referral Mechanism**.

## Explanation

### 1. False imprisonment (common law)

The **unlawful and intentional (or reckless) restraint of a person's freedom of movement** from a particular place. No minimum time; no need for force or a locked room – **restraint** can be by threats or by locking someone in a car. Max **life**.

### 2. Kidnapping (common law)

The **taking or carrying away of one person by another**, **by force or by fraud**, **without the consent** of the person taken and **without lawful excuse**. Max **life**.

- **Fraud** can vitiate consent – e.g. offering a lift then driving elsewhere (*R v Cort* 2003: women got on a "bus" believing it was a real service).
- A **parent** can kidnap their own child (*R v D* 1984) – but for a child under 16 the **Child Abduction Act 1984** is usually used; **DPP consent** needed to prosecute a parent for kidnapping a child under 16 ⚠.
- The carrying away need only be **a short distance**.

:::es
**Detención ilegal (common law)**: **restringir intencionadamente** (o con imprudencia) la **libertad de movimiento** de alguien; no hace falta fuerza ni tiempo mínimo. **Secuestro (common law)**: **llevarse** a una persona **por la fuerza o con engaño**, sin su consentimiento ni excusa legal (*Cort*: falso autobús). Un **padre** puede secuestrar a su hijo (*R v D*). Ambos: **perpetua**.
:::

### 3. Hostage-taking (Taking of Hostages Act 1982, s. 1)

A person (**whatever their nationality**, wherever in the world) who **detains** any other person (the hostage) and, in order to **compel a State, an international governmental organisation or a person** to do or abstain from doing any act, **threatens to kill, injure or continue to detain** the hostage. Max **life**. **Attorney General's consent** required.

### 4. Modern slavery (MSA 2015)

**s. 1 – Slavery, servitude and forced or compulsory labour**: holding another person in **slavery or servitude** (knowing or ought to know), or requiring a person to perform **forced or compulsory labour**. The court may consider **all the circumstances**, including personal circumstances (age, family, mental/physical illness) and any work done. **Consent** of the person does **not** preclude a finding. Max **life**.

**s. 2 – Human trafficking**: **arranging or facilitating the travel** of another person **with a view to them being exploited**. Travel = arriving in, entering, departing from **or travelling within any country** (incl. within the UK). **Consent to the travel is irrelevant** (adult or child). Max **life**.

**s. 3 – Exploitation**: slavery/servitude/forced labour; **sexual exploitation**; **removal of organs**; **securing services by force, threats or deception**; securing services from **children or vulnerable persons** (chosen because of youth, illness, disability or family relationship).

**s. 4**: committing any offence (e.g. kidnapping, false imprisonment) **with intent** to commit s. 2 – max **10 years**.

### 5. Protection of victims

- **s. 45 defence**: an **adult** victim who was **compelled** to commit an offence as a direct consequence of being a victim of slavery/exploitation, where a reasonable person in the same situation would have had **no realistic alternative**; a **child** (under 18) who committed it as a direct consequence of being a victim and a reasonable person of the same age would have done it. **Does not apply** to many **serious offences** (Schedule 4 – e.g. murder, rape, robbery ⚠).
- **Duty to notify** (s. 52): **public authorities**, including **police**, must notify the **Home Office** when they have reasonable grounds to believe a person may be a victim (adults' consent needed for a referral to the NRM; for children, always refer).
- **National Referral Mechanism (NRM)**: framework for identifying and supporting victims – a **reasonable grounds** decision then a **conclusive grounds** decision.
- **Slavery and Trafficking Prevention Orders** (on conviction) and **Risk Orders** (no conviction needed – chief officer applies) ⚠.

:::es
**Toma de rehenes (1982)**: retener a alguien y **amenazar con matarlo, herirlo o seguir reteniéndolo** para **obligar** a un Estado, organización o persona; cualquier nacionalidad; consentimiento del **AG**; perpetua. **Esclavitud moderna (MSA 2015)**: **s. 1** esclavitud, servidumbre, trabajo forzoso (el **consentimiento** de la víctima no impide el delito); **s. 2 trata** = **organizar o facilitar el viaje** (también **dentro del Reino Unido**) para **explotar** a alguien; el consentimiento es **irrelevante**; perpetua. **s. 45**: defensa para víctimas **obligadas** a delinquir (no para delitos graves). **Deber de notificar** al Home Office; **NRM** (decisión de motivos razonables y luego concluyente).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **Locked in the car.** D locks his partner in his car for 20 minutes during an argument. → **False imprisonment** (restraint, no minimum time).
2. **The fake taxi.** D poses as a taxi driver and drives a drunk woman to a remote place. → **Kidnapping** by fraud (+ consider sexual offences).
3. **Car wash.** Workers live on site, documents taken, "debts" to pay off, paid nothing. → **s. 1 MSA** (forced labour/servitude); duty to notify; NRM.
4. **County lines.** A 15-year-old from London is moved to Hampshire to sell drugs. → **s. 2 trafficking** (travel within the UK; child's consent irrelevant); the child may have a **s. 45** defence.
5. **The embassy.** Gunman detains staff and demands the release of prisoners. → **Hostage-taking** (AG consent).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Kidnapping needs **taking/carrying away**; false imprisonment just **restraint**.
- Fraud can vitiate consent (*Cort*).
- Trafficking: **travel within the UK** counts; **consent irrelevant**.
- s. 45 defence: adult – compelled + no realistic alternative; child – direct consequence; **not** for Schedule 4 offences.
- Hostage-taking: **AG's consent**.

## Summary

1. False imprisonment: unlawful restraint of freedom of movement (life).
2. Kidnapping: taking away by force or fraud without consent (life); parents can kidnap.
3. Hostage-taking 1982 (life; AG consent).
4. MSA 2015: s. 1 slavery/forced labour; s. 2 trafficking; s. 3 exploitation; s. 4 preparatory.
5. s. 45 defence; duty to notify; NRM; STPOs/STROs.
