## Objectives

- Know the **Computer Misuse Act 1990** offences: **unauthorised access** (s. 1), with **intent to commit further offences** (s. 2), **unauthorised acts to impair** (s. 3), acts causing **serious damage** (s. 3ZA) and **articles** (s. 3A).
- Understand **authorisation** (insiders exceeding their access).
- Know the principles for handling **digital evidence**, **encryption keys** (RIPA 2000, s. 49) and the reporting landscape (**Action Fraud**, **ROCU**, **NCA**).

## Explanation

### 1. Unauthorised access (s. 1)

A **causes a computer to perform any function** with **intent to secure access** to any program or data held in any computer (or to enable such access), the access is **unauthorised**, and A **knows** that at the time. Max **2 years** (⚠).

- The intent **need not be directed at any particular** program, data or computer.
- **Unauthorised**: A is not entitled to control access of the kind in question and doesn't have consent from someone who is. An **employee** who accesses data **beyond the limits of their authority** commits s. 1 (*R v Bow Street Magistrates, ex p Allison* 2000, HL). (*DPP v Bignell* – officers using the PNC for private purposes but within their access level – was not s. 1; consider misconduct/Data Protection Act.)
- Typing a guessed password, using someone else's login, "hacking" into an account.

### 2. With intent to commit further offences (s. 2)

s. 1 committed with intent to commit, or facilitate the commission of, an offence carrying **5 years or more** (e.g. fraud, theft) – by A or another, now or later. The further offence need not be possible. Max **5 years**.

### 3. Unauthorised acts with intent to impair (s. 3)

Any **unauthorised act** in relation to a computer, knowing it's unauthorised, **intending** (or **reckless** as to) **impairing** the operation of any computer, preventing/hindering access to programs or data, impairing the operation of programs or reliability of data. Examples: **malware**, **ransomware**, **DDoS** attacks, deleting files. Max **10 years**.

- **s. 3ZA**: unauthorised act causing, or creating a significant risk of, **serious damage** to **human welfare, the environment, the economy or national security** – max **14 years**, or **life** if damage to human welfare (loss of life, illness, injury) or national security.

### 4. Articles (s. 3A)

**Making, adapting, supplying or offering to supply** any article intending it to be used (or believing it's likely to be used) to commit s. 1, 3 or 3ZA; or **obtaining** any article intending to use it / supply it. Includes programs or data (e.g. **malware kits**, password crackers). Max **2 years** (⚠).

:::es
**Computer Misuse Act 1990**: **s. 1** acceso **no autorizado** (hacer que el ordenador ejecute una función para acceder a datos, sabiendo que no está autorizado; un **empleado** que se sale de su autorización también – *Allison*); **s. 2** con intención de cometer **otro delito** (≥ 5 años, p. ej. fraude); **s. 3** actos no autorizados para **perjudicar** (malware, ransomware, DDoS; intención o imprudencia; **10 años**); **s. 3ZA** daño **grave** (14 años o perpetua); **s. 3A** crear, suministrar u obtener **herramientas** (programas) para estos delitos.
:::

### 5. Digital evidence and investigation

- **Principles** (ACPO/NPCC Good Practice Guide):
  1. No action should **change data** that may later be relied on in court.
  2. Where access to original data is necessary, the person must be **competent** and able to explain their actions.
  3. An **audit trail** of all processes must be created and preserved.
  4. The **officer in charge** has overall responsibility for compliance.
- **Device seizure**: if on, consider not switching off (volatile data, encryption) – get **specialist advice**; isolate phones from networks (Faraday bag); record the state of the device; preserve chain of custody.
- **Encryption keys**: **RIPA 2000, s. 49** notice requiring disclosure of a key or the data in intelligible form; failing to comply is an offence (s. 53) – max **2 years**, **5 years** in national security or child indecency cases.
- **Interception** of communications in the course of transmission without lawful authority is an offence (Investigatory Powers Act 2016, s. 3).
- **Reporting**: **Action Fraud** / NFIB (national fraud and cyber reporting – being replaced ⚠), **Regional Organised Crime Units (ROCU)** cyber teams, **NCA National Cyber Crime Unit**.

:::es
**Prueba digital (principios NPCC)**: 1) no **alterar** los datos; 2) si hay que acceder al original, una persona **competente** que pueda explicarlo; 3) **registro** de todo lo hecho; 4) el **responsable** de la investigación responde. No apagues dispositivos sin asesoramiento; **bolsa de Faraday** para móviles. **RIPA s. 49**: exigir la **clave** de cifrado; negarse es delito (2 años; 5 si seguridad nacional o imágenes de menores). **Interceptar** comunicaciones sin autorización es delito (IPA 2016). **Action Fraud**, **ROCU**, **NCA**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The nosy employee.** A bank clerk looks up her ex's account details though her job doesn't allow it. → **s. 1** (exceeds authority – *Allison*).
2. **The password.** D guesses his partner's email password and reads her messages. → **s. 1**; if to blackmail her → **s. 2**.
3. **Ransomware.** A gang encrypts a hospital's files and demands bitcoin. → **s. 3** / **s. 3ZA** (human welfare), blackmail, fraud.
4. **Selling malware.** D sells a remote access trojan online. → **s. 3A** (supplying articles).
5. **Locked phone.** Suspect refuses to give his PIN. → **s. 49 RIPA** notice; refusal = s. 53 offence.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- s. 1 doesn't need intent to access a **particular** computer or data.
- s. 2: the further offence must carry **≥ 5 years** (or be fixed by law).
- s. 3 can be **reckless**; s. 1 needs knowledge.
- Employees can commit s. 1 by exceeding their **limits** of authority.
- Digital evidence: **don't change data**; competence; audit trail; OIC responsible.

## Summary

1. s. 1 unauthorised access (2 yrs ⚠); s. 2 with intent (5 yrs); s. 3 impair (10 yrs); s. 3ZA serious damage (14/life); s. 3A articles.
2. Authorisation: exceeding limits counts (Allison).
3. Digital evidence principles; seizure of devices; RIPA s. 49 keys; IPA 2016 interception.
4. Action Fraud/NFIB, ROCU, NCA.
