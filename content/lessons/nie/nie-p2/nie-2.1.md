## Objectives

- Know the elements of **murder** and the rules on **causation**.
- Know the **partial defences**: **loss of control**, **diminished responsibility**, **suicide pact** – and **infanticide**.
- Distinguish **unlawful act** and **gross negligence** manslaughter; know **corporate manslaughter**, **causing or allowing the death of a child or vulnerable adult**, **threats to kill** and **soliciting murder**.

## Explanation

### 1. Murder (common law)

The **unlawful killing** of a **human being** (a person "in being" – born alive and with an existence independent of the mother; a foetus is not) **under the King's peace** (not, e.g., an enemy in the course of war) with **malice aforethought** – an **intention to kill or to cause grievous bodily harm**.

- Sentence: **mandatory life imprisonment**.
- **Oblique intent** (*R v Woollin* 1998): the jury may find intention if death or serious injury was a **virtual certainty** as a result of D's actions and D **appreciated** that.
- **Year and a day rule** abolished (Law Reform (Year and a Day Rule) Act 1996): prosecution needs the **Attorney General's consent** where the injury was sustained **more than 3 years** before death, or D has already been convicted of an offence connected with the death ⚠.
- Murder by a **British citizen abroad** can be tried here.

:::es
**Asesinato (common law)**: **matar ilegalmente** a un **ser humano** nacido vivo, bajo la paz del Rey, con **"malice aforethought"** = **intención de matar o de causar lesiones graves (GBH)**. **Cadena perpetua obligatoria**. **Intención oblicua** (*Woollin*): muerte/lesión grave **prácticamente segura** y el acusado lo sabía. Regla del "año y un día" abolida: si la lesión fue hace **más de 3 años**, hace falta el consentimiento del **Attorney General**.
:::

### 2. Causation

- **Factual**: "but for" D's act, would the victim have died? (*R v White* 1910 – poison but died of heart attack: not D's cause).
- **Legal**: D's act must be an **operating and substantial** (more than minimal) cause; need not be the only cause.
- **Thin skull rule**: take the victim as you find them – including beliefs (*R v Blaue* 1975 – Jehovah's Witness refused a blood transfusion).
- **Medical treatment** breaks the chain only if **so independent and potent** that the original wound is merely the setting (*R v Cheshire* 1991; *R v Smith* 1959).
- **Victim's escape**: if foreseeable (not "daft") it doesn't break the chain (*R v Roberts*).
- **Third-party acts**: police returning fire when D used a human shield – D still liable (*R v Pagett* 1983).

:::es
**Causalidad**: de hecho ("de no ser por") y jurídica (causa **operativa y sustancial**, no necesariamente la única). **Cráneo fino** (*Blaue*): la víctima tal como es. El **tratamiento médico** solo rompe la cadena si es tan independiente y potente que la herida es solo el contexto (*Cheshire*). *Pagett*: escudo humano – sigue siendo responsable.
:::

### 3. Partial defences (reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter)

**Loss of control** (Coroners and Justice Act 2009, ss. 54–55):
1. D's acts resulted from a **loss of self-control** (need not be sudden);
2. which had a **qualifying trigger**: **fear of serious violence** from V against D or another, **and/or** things done or said that constituted **circumstances of an extremely grave character** and caused D to have a **justifiable sense of being seriously wronged**;
3. a person of D's **sex and age**, with a **normal degree of tolerance and self-restraint** and in D's circumstances, might have reacted in the same or a similar way.

Not available if D acted in a **considered desire for revenge**; **sexual infidelity** on its own is **disregarded** as a trigger; a trigger D **incited** to provide an excuse doesn't count.

**Diminished responsibility** (Homicide Act 1957, s. 2 as amended): an **abnormality of mental functioning** that **arose from a recognised medical condition**, **substantially impaired** D's ability to **understand the nature of his conduct**, **form a rational judgment**, or **exercise self-control**, and **provides an explanation** for the killing. **Burden on the defence** (balance of probabilities).

**Suicide pact** (s. 4 Homicide Act 1957): D kills in pursuance of a suicide pact, having a settled intention of dying himself.

**Infanticide** (Infanticide Act 1938): a **woman** causes the death of her **child under 12 months**, when the **balance of her mind was disturbed** because of not having fully recovered from giving birth or lactation.

:::es
**Defensas parciales (→ homicidio voluntario)**: **pérdida de control** (CJA 2009): pérdida de autocontrol + **detonante** (miedo a violencia grave o algo **extremadamente grave** que le hizo sentirse **gravemente agraviado**) + una persona de su sexo y edad con tolerancia normal podría haber reaccionado igual; **no** por venganza meditada; la **infidelidad** sola no cuenta. **Responsabilidad disminuida**: anomalía del funcionamiento mental por **condición médica reconocida** que **reduce sustancialmente** su capacidad (comprender, juicio racional, autocontrol); la prueba la aporta la defensa. **Pacto suicida**. **Infanticidio**: madre, hijo **menor de 12 meses**, mente alterada por el parto/lactancia.
:::

### 4. Involuntary manslaughter (max life)

- **Unlawful act (constructive) manslaughter**: an **unlawful act** (a criminal offence – not an omission), which is **dangerous** – a **sober and reasonable person** would inevitably recognise it carried **some risk of some harm** (*R v Church* 1966) – and which **causes death**. D need not foresee any harm.
- **Gross negligence manslaughter** (*R v Adomako* 1994): a **duty of care** owed to V, **breach** of that duty, a **serious and obvious risk of death**, the breach **caused** death, and it was so bad as to be **gross** (criminal) in the jury's view.
- **Corporate manslaughter** (Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007): an organisation whose activities are **managed or organised** (by **senior management** as a substantial element) in a way that **causes death** and amounts to a **gross breach** of a relevant duty of care. Penalty: **unlimited fine**, publicity and remedial orders.

### 5. Other offences

- **Causing or allowing the death (or serious physical harm) of a child or vulnerable adult** (Domestic Violence, Crime and Victims Act 2004, s. 5): V dies/suffers serious harm from an unlawful act of a **member of the same household** who had **frequent contact**; there was a **significant risk of serious physical harm**; D was that person **or** was or ought to have been aware of the risk and **failed to take reasonable steps** to protect V. Solves "which of the two parents did it?" cases.
- **Threats to kill** (OAPA 1861, s. 16): without lawful excuse, making a threat to another **intending** that the other **would fear it would be carried out** – max **10 years**.
- **Soliciting murder** (OAPA 1861, s. 4) – max **life**.

:::es
**Homicidio involuntario**: **por acto ilícito peligroso** (*Church*: una persona sensata vería **algún riesgo de algún daño**) o **por negligencia grave** (*Adomako*: deber de cuidado, incumplimiento, riesgo de muerte, causa, **grave**). **Homicidio corporativo** (2007): multa ilimitada. **Causar o permitir la muerte** de un menor o adulto vulnerable (DVCVA 2004 s. 5): miembros del **mismo hogar**. **Amenazas de muerte** (OAPA s. 16): intención de que el otro **tema** que se cumpla; **10 años**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The punch.** One punch, V falls, hits head, dies. D didn't intend serious harm. → **Unlawful act manslaughter** (assault, dangerous by Church test).
2. **The refusal.** V stabbed, refuses a blood transfusion for religious reasons and dies. → D still liable – **thin skull** (*Blaue*).
3. **The baby.** An infant dies from non-accidental injuries; both parents deny it. → **s. 5 DVCVA 2004** (causing or allowing).
4. **The text.** "I'm going to kill you" sent to make V afraid. → **Threats to kill (s. 16)**.
5. **Years of abuse.** D kills her abusive partner after a threat of serious violence. → **Loss of control** (fear of serious violence) and/or **diminished responsibility** – for the jury.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Malice aforethought = intent to kill **or** intent to cause **GBH**.
- Loss of control: **need not be sudden**; **revenge** and **sexual infidelity alone** excluded.
- Diminished responsibility: **recognised medical condition**; burden on **defence**.
- Infanticide: **woman**, child **under 12 months**.
- Unlawful act manslaughter needs a criminal **act** (not omission) – the danger test is **objective**.

## Summary

1. Murder: unlawful killing, person in being, King's peace, intent to kill/GBH – mandatory life.
2. Causation: but for + operating and substantial; thin skull; medical treatment rarely breaks chain.
3. Partial defences: loss of control, diminished responsibility, suicide pact; infanticide.
4. Manslaughter: unlawful act (Church) / gross negligence (Adomako); corporate manslaughter.
5. s. 5 DVCVA 2004; threats to kill (s. 16 OAPA, 10 yrs); soliciting murder.
