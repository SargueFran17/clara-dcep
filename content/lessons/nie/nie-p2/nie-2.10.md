## Objectives

- Know **police protection** (Children Act 1989, s. 46) and **emergency protection orders** (s. 44).
- Know **child cruelty** (Children and Young Persons Act 1933, s. 1) and **child abduction** (Child Abduction Act 1984, ss. 1–2).
- Understand the multi-agency safeguarding framework (s. 47 enquiries, strategy discussions, MASH) and specific risks (FGM, missing children, CSE).

## Explanation

### 1. Police protection (Children Act 1989, s. 46)

Where a **constable** has **reasonable cause to believe** that a **child** (under **18**) would otherwise be likely to suffer **significant harm**, he may:
- **remove** the child to **suitable accommodation** and keep him there; or
- take reasonable steps to ensure the child's **removal from a hospital** or other place where he is being accommodated **is prevented**.

Duties **as soon as reasonably practicable**: inform the **local authority**; inform the **child** (if able to understand) and take steps to discover their wishes; inform the **parents/persons with parental responsibility**; refer the case to the **designated officer** (an officer of at least **inspector** rank ⚠), who **inquires** into the case and may apply for an **EPO**.

- **Maximum 72 hours** in police protection.
- The police do **not** acquire parental responsibility, but must do what's reasonable to safeguard welfare.
- s. 46 gives **no power of entry** – use **PACE s. 17** (save life or limb) or an **EPO** with a warrant.

:::es
**Protección policial (CA 1989 s. 46)**: si un agente cree razonablemente que un **menor de 18** sufrirá **daño significativo**, puede **llevarlo a un lugar adecuado** o **impedir que lo saquen** del hospital. Avisar a **servicios sociales**, al **menor**, a los **padres** y al **oficial designado** (inspector ⚠). Máximo **72 horas**. **No** da poder de entrada (usar **s. 17 PACE**) ni patria potestad.
:::

### 2. Emergency Protection Order (s. 44)

Made by a **court** (on application by anyone, e.g. local authority, or the designated officer) where there's reasonable cause to believe the child is likely to suffer significant harm if not removed / if not kept where they are. Lasts up to **8 days**, extendable once by up to **7 days** ⚠. It can include a **warrant** for a constable to assist with entry and search (s. 48).

### 3. Child cruelty (CYPA 1933, s. 1)

A person **aged 16 or over** who has **responsibility** for a child **under 16** and **wilfully assaults, ill-treats** (physically or otherwise), **neglects, abandons or exposes** him, or causes or procures him to be so treated, in a manner **likely to cause unnecessary suffering or injury to health** (including **psychological** suffering or injury ⚠). Either-way, max **14 years** (raised from 10 in 2022 ⚠).

- **Wilfully** = deliberately or recklessly (*R v Sheppard* 1981 – e.g. failing to get medical help when aware of the risk).
- Neglect includes failing to provide **adequate food, clothing, medical aid or lodging**.
- A parent is presumed to have responsibility.

:::es
**Crueldad infantil (CYPA 1933 s. 1)**: persona de **16 o más** con responsabilidad sobre un **menor de 16** que **voluntariamente** lo agrede, maltrata (también psicológicamente ⚠), descuida, abandona o expone de forma que pueda causarle **sufrimiento innecesario o daño a la salud**. **Voluntariamente** = deliberadamente o con imprudencia (*Sheppard*). Máx. **14 años** ⚠.
:::

### 4. Child abduction (Child Abduction Act 1984)

- **s. 1 – person connected with the child** (parent, guardian, person with a child arrangements order for residence, special guardian, person with custody): **takes or sends** a child **under 16** **out of the UK** **without the appropriate consent**. Defences: e.g. a residence order holder taking the child out for **less than 1 month** (3 months for special guardians ⚠); belief that consent was given or would have been; took all reasonable steps to communicate. **DPP consent** needed to prosecute.
- **s. 2 – other persons**: **without lawful authority or reasonable excuse**, **takes or detains** a child **under 16** so as to **remove him from**, or **keep him out of**, the **lawful control** of any person having lawful control. Defence: belief that the child had attained 16 (and other matters ⚠).
- Max **7 years** for both. "Taking" includes **causing or inducing** the child to accompany D.

:::es
**Sustracción de menores (CAA 1984)**: **s. 1** una persona **vinculada** (padre, tutor…) **saca del Reino Unido** a un **menor de 16** sin el **consentimiento** necesario (excepciones: titular de orden de residencia por **menos de 1 mes**…); consentimiento del **DPP**. **s. 2** **otras personas** que **se llevan o retienen** a un menor de 16 **fuera del control legal** de quien lo tiene, sin autoridad ni excusa razonable. Máx. **7 años**.
:::

### 5. The safeguarding framework

- **Children Act 2004, s. 11**: police (and other agencies) must ensure their functions are discharged having regard to the need to **safeguard and promote the welfare of children**.
- **Working Together to Safeguard Children** (statutory guidance ⚠ 2023 version): **strategy discussion** (police, children's social care, health) when there's reasonable cause to suspect significant harm → **s. 47 enquiries** led by the **local authority**; police lead on the **criminal investigation**.
- **MASH** (Multi-Agency Safeguarding Hub) shares information.
- **Specific risks**: **FGM** (FGM Act 2003 – **mandatory reporting duty** for regulated professionals who discover FGM in a girl **under 18**; FGM Protection Orders); **missing children** (risk assessment, return home interviews); **child sexual and criminal exploitation** (county lines); **forced marriage** (Forced Marriage Protection Orders; marriage under 18 is now an offence ⚠).
- **Voice of the child**: speak to the child, record their views; **ABE** interviews for evidence.

:::es
**Marco de protección**: **CA 2004 s. 11** (deber de proteger el bienestar). ***Working Together***: **reunión de estrategia** (policía, servicios sociales, sanidad) → **investigación s. 47** dirigida por el **ayuntamiento**; la policía lleva la parte **penal**. **MASH**. Riesgos: **MGF** (obligación de denunciar para profesionales, menores de 18), **menores desaparecidos**, **explotación sexual y criminal** (county lines), **matrimonio forzado**. Escuchar al menor; entrevistas **ABE**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The flat.** Called to a flat: a 3-year-old alone, filthy, no food; parents unreachable. → **s. 46 police protection** (remove to suitable accommodation); entry under **s. 17 PACE** if needed; inform LA, designated officer.
2. **Hospital.** Parents try to take an injured baby from hospital against medical advice; suspicion of abuse. → **s. 46** – **prevent removal** from the hospital.
3. **The airport.** Mother tries to fly her 8-year-old to another country permanently without the father's consent. → **s. 1 Child Abduction Act**.
4. **The stranger.** A man persuades a 13-year-old to get into his car and drives off. → **s. 2** (taking includes inducing to accompany); consider sexual offences.
5. **Neglect.** Parents ignore a child's obvious serious illness for days. → **Child cruelty (s. 1 CYPA)** – wilful neglect (*Sheppard*).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Police protection: child = **under 18**; max **72 hours**; **no power of entry**.
- Designated officer: **inspector** ⚠.
- Child cruelty: offender **16+**, child **under 16**.
- Child abduction: child **under 16**; s. 1 (connected person, **out of the UK**) vs s. 2 (others, **lawful control**).
- s. 47 enquiries are led by the **local authority**.

## Summary

1. s. 46 CA 1989 police protection – significant harm, 72 hours, duties to inform, designated officer.
2. s. 44 EPO by a court (8 days + 7).
3. CYPA 1933 s. 1 child cruelty (16+ / under 16; wilfully; 14 yrs ⚠).
4. Child Abduction Act 1984 ss. 1–2 (under 16; 7 yrs).
5. Safeguarding framework: s. 11 CA 2004, strategy discussion, s. 47, MASH; FGM, missing, exploitation.
