## Objectives

- Know the **classes** (A, B, C) and examples of controlled drugs under the **Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 (MDA)**.
- Understand the main **offences**: production, **supply** (and being concerned), **possession**, **possession with intent to supply**, **cultivation of cannabis**, **s. 8 premises** offences.
- Know the **defences** (s. 5(4), s. 28) and the **police powers** (s. 23).

## Explanation

### 1. Classes (examples – ⚠ VERIFICAR)

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **A** | heroin, cocaine (incl. crack), MDMA (ecstasy), LSD, methadone, methamphetamine, magic mushrooms (psilocin) |
| **B** | cannabis, amphetamine (Class A if prepared for injection), ketamine (⚠), codeine, mephedrone, synthetic cannabinoids |
| **C** | benzodiazepines (e.g. diazepam), GHB/GBL, anabolic steroids, khat |

**Temporary class drug orders** (s. 2A) can control new substances quickly for up to 12 months (⚠ VERIFICAR). Many "legal highs" fall under the **Psychoactive Substances Act 2016**.

:::es
**Clases**: **A** (heroína, cocaína/crack, MDMA, LSD, metadona, metanfetamina, setas), **B** (cannabis, anfetamina –A si es inyectable–, ketamina ⚠, codeína, mefedrona), **C** (benzodiacepinas, GHB, esteroides, khat). **Órdenes temporales** (s. 2A) y **Psychoactive Substances Act 2016** para nuevas sustancias.
:::

### 2. Possession (s. 5(2)) – what it means

- **Possession** includes having something in your **custody or under your control**, even if someone else physically holds it (s. 37(3)).
- You must **know you have the thing** (the container/package), though you don't need to know it's a drug for the basic offence – that's where the **s. 28 defence** comes in (*Warner v MPC* (1969)).
- The quantity must be **enough to amount to something** – visible, tangible and measurable – not just microscopic traces (*R v Boyesen* (1982)).

:::es
**Posesión**: tenerlo **bajo tu custodia o control** (aunque lo lleve otro). Tienes que **saber que tienes la cosa** (el paquete) aunque no sepas que es droga (para eso está la **defensa del s. 28**) – *Warner*. La cantidad debe ser **visible y medible**, no restos microscópicos (*Boyesen*).
:::

### 3. Supply offences (s. 4(3)) and possession with intent (s. 5(3))

It is an offence to:
- (a) **supply** or **offer to supply** a controlled drug;
- (b) be **concerned in the supply** of a controlled drug by another;
- (c) be **concerned in the making of an offer** to supply by another.

**Supply** means passing to another for **their purposes** – no payment is needed (sharing can be supply). Returning drugs to the person who left them with you can be supply (*R v Maginnis* (1987)). An **offer** is complete when made – even if the defendant **never had** the drugs or the substance turns out to be **fake**. **"Being concerned"** covers wide involvement (lookouts, drivers, taking orders).

**Possession with intent to supply** (s. 5(3)): possession + intention to supply **to another** (evidence: quantity, deal bags, scales, cash, messages on phones).

:::es
**Suministro (s. 4(3))**: a) **suministrar u ofrecer**; b) **participar** en el suministro de otro; c) **participar en una oferta** de otro. **Suministrar** = pasarlo a otro **para sus fines**, sin necesidad de pago (compartir puede serlo); devolver la droga a quien te la dejó también (*Maginnis*). La **oferta** se consuma al hacerla, aunque no tengas la droga o sea **falsa**. **Posesión con intención de suministrar (s. 5(3))**: indicios = cantidad, bolsitas, básculas, dinero, mensajes.
:::

### 4. Production (s. 4(2)) and cultivation (s. 6)

- **Producing** a controlled drug (e.g. making crack from cocaine, manufacturing).
- **Cultivating any plant of the genus Cannabis** (s. 6) – growing, tending, watering.

### 5. Premises – s. 8

An **occupier**, or a person **concerned in the management** of premises, commits an offence if they **knowingly permit or suffer** on those premises:
- (a) **producing** or attempting to produce a controlled drug;
- (b) **supplying** or attempting/offering to supply a controlled drug;
- (c) **preparing opium** for smoking;
- (d) **smoking cannabis, cannabis resin or prepared opium**.

Note: **using** other drugs (e.g. injecting heroin) on the premises is **not** covered by s. 8(d) – only **smoking cannabis/opium**. "Occupier" can include someone with **sufficient control** (e.g. a student in a hall room – *R v Tao* (1977)).

:::es
**s. 8 (locales)**: el **ocupante** o quien **gestiona** el lugar que **a sabiendas permite**: a) **producción**; b) **suministro**; c) preparar **opio**; d) **fumar cannabis** u opio. **No** cubre **consumir** otras drogas (p. ej. inyectarse heroína). "Ocupante" incluye a quien tiene **control suficiente** (*Tao*: un estudiante en su habitación).
:::

### 6. Defences

- **s. 5(4)** (possession only): the defendant took possession (a) **to prevent another committing an offence** and, as soon as possible, **destroyed it or delivered it** to a person lawfully entitled (e.g. police); or (b) to **deliver it into lawful custody** and took all reasonable steps to do so as soon as possible.
- **s. 28**: it's a defence to prove **lack of knowledge** of a fact the prosecution must prove; and, about the substance, that the defendant **neither knew nor suspected nor had reason to suspect** it was a **controlled drug**. It is **no defence** that they thought it was a **different** controlled drug (s. 28(3)). The defendant proves it on the **balance of probabilities** (⚠ some debate – VERIFICAR).

:::es
- **s. 5(4)**: cogerla para **evitar un delito** y **destruirla o entregarla** cuanto antes, o para **entregarla a la autoridad**.
- **s. 28**: no sabía, ni sospechaba, ni tenía motivos para sospechar que era **droga controlada**. **No** sirve decir "creía que era **otra** droga".
:::

### 7. Police powers – s. 23

- **s. 23(2)**: if a constable has **reasonable grounds to suspect** a person is in **possession of a controlled drug**, they may **search** that person and **detain** them for the search; **search a vehicle/vessel**; and **seize** anything that appears to be evidence.
- **s. 23(3)**: a **justice of the peace** may issue a **search warrant** for premises.
- **s. 23(4)**: **intentionally obstructing** a person exercising these powers is an offence.
- Searches follow **PACE Code A** (GOWISELY, recording).

:::es
**s. 23(2)**: con **sospecha razonable** de posesión, puedes **registrar y retener** a la persona, **registrar vehículos** e **incautar** pruebas. **s. 23(3)**: **orden judicial** para registrar inmuebles. **s. 23(4)**: **obstruir** es delito. Se aplica el **Code A**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The text message.** A man texts "got 10 bags of white, £40 each" to a stranger. He has no drugs. → **Offer to supply** – complete when made.
2. **Sharing a joint.** At a party, LIA passes a joint she bought to her friend. → Can be **supply** (no payment needed).
3. **The landlord.** A landlord knows tenants **inject heroin** in the flat. → **Not s. 8(d)** (only smoking cannabis/opium) – but if he knows they **supply** there, s. 8(b).
4. **The found bag.** A mother finds cannabis in her son's room and takes it to flush it away straight away. → **s. 5(4)(a)** defence may apply.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Offer to supply**: no drugs needed; fake drugs still an offer of a controlled drug.
- **Supply** doesn't need payment.
- **s. 8(d)** = **smoking** cannabis/opium only.
- **s. 28**: "I thought it was cocaine, not heroin" is **no defence**.
- **s. 23** stop/search threshold: **reasonable grounds to suspect** possession.
- **Possession** needs knowledge of having the thing (*Warner*) and a usable quantity (*Boyesen*).

## Summary

1. Classes A, B, C (⚠ check list); TCDOs; Psychoactive Substances Act 2016.
2. Possession (custody/control, knowledge of the thing, measurable quantity).
3. Supply/offer/being concerned (s. 4(3)); intent to supply (s. 5(3)); production (s. 4(2)); cultivation (s. 6).
4. s. 8 premises: produce, supply, prepare opium, smoke cannabis/opium – knowingly permit/suffer.
5. Defences s. 5(4), s. 28; powers s. 23 (search on reasonable suspicion; warrants; obstruction).
