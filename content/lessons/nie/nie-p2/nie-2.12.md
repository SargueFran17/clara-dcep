## Objectives

- Know the main tools of the **Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014**: **civil injunctions**, **Criminal Behaviour Orders**, **dispersal powers**, **Community Protection Notices**, **Public Spaces Protection Orders** and **closure powers**.
- Know related police powers: **requiring name and address** (Police Reform Act 2002, s. 50) and **vehicles used anti-socially** (s. 59).

## Explanation

### 1. Civil injunction (ASBCPA 2014, Part 1)

- Court: **county court/High Court** (adults), **youth court** (10–17).
- Test (**civil standard** – balance of probabilities): the respondent (**10 or over**) has engaged or threatens to engage in **anti-social behaviour** – conduct that has caused, or is likely to cause, **harassment, alarm or distress** to any person, or (housing context) **nuisance or annoyance** – **and** it's **just and convenient** to grant it.
- Applicants: **police** (chief officer), **local authorities**, **housing providers**, TfL, Environment Agency, NHS Protect ⚠.
- Can contain **prohibitions and positive requirements**; a **power of arrest** can be attached if violence/significant risk of harm.
- Breach: **contempt of court** (adults), not a criminal offence.

### 2. Criminal Behaviour Order (s. 22)

- Made **on conviction** for any offence, on the **prosecution's application**.
- The court must be **satisfied beyond reasonable doubt** that the offender has engaged in behaviour causing or likely to cause **HAD** to any person, **and** consider that the order **will help prevent** such behaviour.
- Prohibitions and requirements; adults: at least **2 years** ⚠; under 18s: 1–3 years.
- **Breach** without reasonable excuse = **criminal offence**, max **5 years**.

:::es
**Requerimiento civil (Parte 1)**: el juez lo concede si, en **balance de probabilidades**, alguien (≥ **10 años**) ha causado o puede causar **acoso, alarma o angustia** y es **justo y conveniente**; lo piden **policía**, ayuntamientos, vivienda social…; incumplirlo = **desacato** (no delito). **Criminal Behaviour Order (s. 22)**: **tras una condena**, a petición de la **fiscalía**, más allá de toda duda razonable; incumplirlo es **delito** (máx. **5 años**).
:::

### 3. Dispersal powers (ss. 34–42)

- **Authorisation** (s. 34): an officer of at least **inspector** rank may authorise the use of the power in a specified **locality** for up to **48 hours**, if satisfied on reasonable grounds it may be necessary to remove or reduce the likelihood of members of the public being **harassed, alarmed or distressed**, or of **crime or disorder**. Must have particular regard to rights of freedom of expression and assembly.
- **Direction** (s. 35): a **constable in uniform** (or PCSO if designated) may direct a person (**10 or over**) to **leave** the locality and **not return** for up to **48 hours**; can require **surrender of items** that could be used for ASB; can **remove a person under 16** to their home or a place of safety.
- Must be given **in writing** where reasonably practicable; can't be used to stop people picketing or taking part in lawful processions.
- Failing to comply = offence (max 3 months/fine ⚠).

### 4. Community Protection Notice (s. 43)

- Issued by a **constable**, **PCSO** (if designated) or **local authority** to a person **16 or over** (or a body), if satisfied on reasonable grounds that their conduct is having a **detrimental effect, of a persistent or continuing nature, on the quality of life** of those in the locality, and is **unreasonable**.
- A **written warning** must be given first, with time to comply.
- Breach = offence (fine); **fixed penalty notice** possible; remedial works.

### 5. Public Spaces Protection Orders (s. 59)

Made by the **local authority** for a public place where activities have a **detrimental effect** on quality of life (e.g. drinking alcohol, dogs) – up to **3 years**, renewable. Police can enforce (e.g. FPNs, requiring surrender of alcohol).

:::es
**Dispersión**: un **inspector** autoriza la zona hasta **48 h**; un agente **uniformado** ordena a una persona (≥ **10 años**) que **se vaya y no vuelva** hasta **48 h**, puede retirar objetos y llevar a casa a **menores de 16**; por escrito; no contra piquetes ni manifestaciones legales. **CPN (s. 43)**: a personas ≥ **16** cuya conducta **persistente** e **irrazonable** perjudica la calidad de vida del barrio; antes **aviso por escrito**. **PSPO**: la hace el **ayuntamiento** para un espacio público (alcohol, perros) hasta **3 años**.
:::

### 6. Closure powers (ss. 76–93)

- **Closure notice** (s. 76): issued by a police officer of at least **inspector** rank (or LA) if satisfied on reasonable grounds that the use of premises has resulted, or is likely soon to result, in **nuisance** to members of the public or **disorder** near the premises; prohibits access for up to **24 hours** (or **48 hours** if authorised by a **superintendent** ⚠).
- **Closure order** (s. 80): the **magistrates' court** must hear the application **within 48 hours** of service of the notice; order up to **3 months**, extendable to a maximum of **6 months**.
- Offences: remaining on/entering closed premises without reasonable excuse.

### 7. Other police powers

- **Police Reform Act 2002, s. 50**: a constable in uniform who has reason to believe a person has been acting (or is acting) in an **anti-social manner** may require their **name and address**; failure/false details = offence.
- **s. 59 PRA 2002**: a constable in uniform who reasonably believes a **motor vehicle** is being used **carelessly/inconsiderately and off-road without authority** in a manner causing (or likely to cause) **alarm, distress or annoyance** may **stop** it, **seize and remove** it (normally after a **warning**), and **enter premises** (not a private dwelling) to do so.

:::es
**Cierre de locales**: **aviso** por un **inspector** (24 h; 48 h con **superintendente** ⚠) si hay **molestias o desorden**; el **juzgado** debe ver la **orden de cierre** en **48 h**; orden hasta **3 meses**, máximo **6**. **PRA 2002 s. 50**: pedir **nombre y dirección** por conducta antisocial. **s. 59**: **incautar vehículos** usados de forma que causen alarma o molestia (normalmente tras **advertencia**).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **Gang in the park.** Repeated intimidation of shoppers by a group on Friday nights. → **Inspector authorises dispersal** (≤ 48 h); officers give **s. 35 directions**; under-16s taken home.
2. **The noisy neighbour.** Persistent loud music every night affecting the street. → **CPN** (warning first).
3. **Drug den.** Flat causing constant nuisance and disorder. → **Closure notice** (inspector) → **closure order** from magistrates within 48 h.
4. **Off-road bikes.** Youths ride scramblers on a playing field, alarming families. → **s. 59 PRA** warning, then seizure.
5. **Prolific shoplifter.** Convicted, also abusive to staff. → Prosecution applies for a **CBO**.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Civil injunction: **civil** standard, breach = **contempt**; CBO: **on conviction**, breach = **offence**.
- Dispersal: **inspector** authorises up to **48 h**; direction by constable **in uniform**; age **10+**.
- CPN: age **16+**; **written warning** first.
- Closure: notice (inspector) → court within **48 h**; order max **6 months** in total.
- PSPO is made by the **local authority**.

## Summary

1. Civil injunctions (HAD/nuisance; 10+; just and convenient); CBOs (on conviction; breach offence).
2. Dispersal powers (inspector, 48 h; directions; under 16 home).
3. CPNs (16+, persistent, unreasonable, warning first); PSPOs (LA).
4. Closure notices and orders (48 h hearing; up to 6 months).
5. PRA 2002 s. 50 name and address; s. 59 vehicles.
