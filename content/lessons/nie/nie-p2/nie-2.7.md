## Objectives

- Build the **ladder of assaults**: common assault/battery → s. 47 ABH → s. 20 wounding/GBH → s. 18 with intent.
- Know the key **definitions** (assault, battery, ABH, wound, GBH, maliciously) and the classic **cases**.
- Understand **consent**, **reasonable punishment** and **self-defence**, plus other offences in this chapter (police/emergency workers, threats to kill, strangulation).

## Explanation

### 1. Assault and battery (common assault – Criminal Justice Act 1988, s. 39)

- **Assault** (technical assault): causing someone to **apprehend immediate unlawful force**. No touching needed. Words or even **silence** can be enough (*R v Ireland* (1997) – silent phone calls). Words can also **negate** an assault (*Tuberville v Savage* (1669): "If it were not assize-time, I would not take such language" – no assault).
- **Battery**: the **intentional or reckless application of unlawful force** to another. Any touching without consent can be enough; **no injury** needed. It can be **indirect** (*Haystead v CC Derbyshire* (2000): punching a woman so she dropped her baby = battery on the baby).
- **Mens rea**: **intention or (subjective) recklessness** as to causing apprehension / applying force.
- Both are **summary offences** charged under s. 39 CJA 1988.

:::es
- **Assault**: hacer que alguien **tema la aplicación inmediata de fuerza ilegítima** (sin tocar). Las palabras e incluso el **silencio** pueden bastar (*Ireland*: llamadas en silencio); las palabras también pueden **anularla** (*Tuberville v Savage*).
- **Battery**: **aplicar fuerza ilegítima** a otra persona, con intención o temeridad. **No hace falta lesión**; puede ser **indirecta** (*Haystead*: golpeó a una mujer y se le cayó el bebé).
- Son delitos **leves (summary)**, art. s. 39 CJA 1988.
:::

### 2. Assault occasioning actual bodily harm (OAPA 1861, s. 47)

- **Actus reus**: an **assault or battery** that **causes** (occasions) **actual bodily harm**.
- **ABH** = any hurt or injury **calculated to interfere with the health or comfort** of the victim, more than transient or trifling (*R v Miller* (1954); *R v Donovan* (1934)). Includes **psychiatric injury** (but not mere emotions like fear or panic – *R v Chan-Fook* (1994)) and even **cutting off someone's hair** (*DPP v Smith* (2006)).
- **Mens rea**: only the mens rea for the **assault or battery** – **no need to foresee any harm** (*R v Savage; DPP v Parmenter* (1991); *R v Roberts* (1971)).
- **Either-way**; maximum **5 years** (⚠ VERIFICAR).

:::es
- **s. 47 ABH**: una agresión (assault o battery) que **causa** un daño **real** (que afecta a la salud o al bienestar, más que pasajero o insignificante). Incluye **daño psiquiátrico** (no simples emociones) y **cortar el pelo** (*DPP v Smith* 2006).
- **Mens rea**: solo la de la agresión; **no** hace falta prever el daño (*Savage*, *Roberts*).
:::

### 3. Wounding or inflicting GBH (OAPA 1861, s. 20)

- Unlawfully and **maliciously** **wound** or **inflict grievous bodily harm**, with or without a weapon.
- **Wound** = breaking **both layers of the skin** (the dermis and epidermis); internal bleeding or a broken bone without a skin break is **not** a wound (*JCC v Eisenhower* (1984)). The **inner skin of the mouth/lip** counts.
- **GBH** = **really serious harm** (*DPP v Smith* (1961)); "serious harm" is also acceptable (*R v Saunders* (1985)). The jury looks at the **whole** of the injuries and the victim's characteristics (*R v Bollom* (2003)). Includes serious **psychiatric** injury (*R v Burstow* (1997)) and infecting someone with a serious disease (*R v Dica* (2004)).
- **Inflict** does **not** require an assault (*Burstow*).
- **Maliciously** = intention or recklessness as to **some harm** (not serious harm) – *Cunningham*; *Savage; Parmenter*.
- **Either-way**; maximum **5 years** (⚠ VERIFICAR).

:::es
- **s. 20**: herir (**wound**) o **causar GBH** de forma ilegítima y **maliciosa**.
- **Wound** = romper **las dos capas de la piel** (*Eisenhower*); hueso roto sin herida abierta **no** es "wound".
- **GBH** = daño **realmente grave** (*DPP v Smith* 1961), valorando el conjunto y a la víctima (*Bollom*); incluye daño **psiquiátrico grave** (*Burstow*).
- **Maliciously**: basta prever **algún daño**.
:::

### 4. Wounding or causing GBH with intent (OAPA 1861, s. 18)

- Unlawfully and maliciously **wound** or **cause GBH** **with intent** to do **GBH**, **or** with intent to **resist or prevent the lawful apprehension or detainer** of any person.
- **Specific intent** – intoxication may be relevant (1.2).
- If the intent is to resist arrest, the prosecution must show the defendant was at least **reckless** ("maliciously") as to causing some harm (⚠ VERIFICAR – *R v Morrison* (1989)).
- **Indictable only**; maximum **life imprisonment**.

:::es
- **s. 18**: herir o causar GBH **con intención** de causar GBH **o** de **resistirse a una detención legal**. Intención **específica**. Solo **Crown Court**; pena máxima **cadena perpetua**.
:::

### 5. Consent, reasonable punishment and self-defence

**Consent**
- A person can consent to **assault/battery** (everyday contact, sport within the rules).
- Generally **no consent to ABH or worse** (*R v Brown* (1993)), **except** recognised activities: properly conducted **sports**, **surgery**, **tattooing/piercing**, **rough horseplay** (*R v Jones* (1986)), and the risk of infection in **consensual sex** where the victim knew (*Dica*; *Konzani*).
- **Domestic Abuse Act 2021, s. 71**: consent to serious harm **for sexual gratification** is **no defence** (ABH or above).

**Reasonable punishment (Children Act 2004, s. 58)** – in **England**, the defence of reasonable punishment of a child is available **only** to **common assault/battery** – **not** to s. 47, s. 20, s. 18 or child cruelty. In **Wales** the defence was **abolished** in 2022 (⚠ VERIFICAR).

**Self-defence** – common law + **Criminal Justice and Immigration Act 2008, s. 76**: the force must be **reasonable in the circumstances as the defendant believed them to be** (honest belief, even if mistaken – unless due to voluntary intoxication). In **householder cases** force is not unreasonable just because it was **disproportionate**, but **grossly disproportionate** force is never reasonable.

:::es
- **Consentimiento**: vale para agresión simple; **no** para ABH o más (*Brown*), salvo **deporte reglado, cirugía, tatuajes/piercings, juegos bruscos** (*Jones*) y riesgo de contagio consentido. **DAA 2021 s. 71**: el consentimiento a un daño grave **por gratificación sexual** **no** es defensa.
- **Castigo razonable** (Children Act 2004 s. 58): en Inglaterra **solo** para la agresión simple; **no** para ABH o más. En **Gales** se **abolió** (2022 ⚠).
- **Legítima defensa** (CJIA 2008 s. 76): fuerza **razonable** según las circunstancias **que el acusado creía** (aunque se equivocara, salvo por embriaguez voluntaria). En **casos de vivienda**, la fuerza desproporcionada puede ser razonable, pero **nunca la groseramente desproporcionada**.
:::

### 6. Other offences in this chapter (overview)

- **Assault on emergency workers** (Assaults on Emergency Workers (Offences) Act 2018): common assault/battery against an emergency worker (police, fire, ambulance, prison officers, NHS staff…) **acting in the exercise of their functions** – higher maximum than common assault (⚠ VERIFICAR: 2 years). It is also an **aggravating factor** for other offences (s. 47, s. 20…).
- **Assaulting / resisting / wilfully obstructing a constable** in the **execution of their duty** (Police Act 1996, s. 89).
- **Assault with intent to resist arrest** (OAPA 1861, s. 38).
- **Threats to kill** (OAPA 1861, s. 16): making a threat, **without lawful excuse**, **intending** the person to **fear** it would be carried out (against them or a third person).
- **Non-fatal strangulation or suffocation** (Serious Crime Act 2015, s. 75A, added by the Domestic Abuse Act 2021): intentionally strangling or doing any other act affecting a person's ability to breathe – **no injury required**; consent is a defence only in limited cases (no serious harm caused and none intended/reckless) (⚠ VERIFICAR).

:::es
Otros delitos: **agresión a trabajadores de emergencias** (Ley 2018), **agresión/resistencia/obstrucción a un agente** en el ejercicio de sus funciones (Police Act 1996 s. 89), **agresión para resistirse a la detención** (OAPA s. 38), **amenazas de muerte** (OAPA s. 16: con intención de que la víctima **tema** que se cumplirá) y **estrangulamiento o asfixia no mortal** (SCA 2015 s. 75A): **no hace falta lesión**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The raised fist.** On a crowded bus, ANNA raises her fist at BEN across the aisle and says "you're next". BEN is frightened. → **Assault** (apprehension of immediate force). No touching needed.
2. **The haircut.** KYLE grabs his ex-partner and cuts off her ponytail. → **s. 47 ABH** (*DPP v Smith* 2006).
3. **The punch.** One punch splits the inside of LEE's lip and bleeds. → A **wound** (both layers of skin, including inner skin of the mouth) → **s. 20** if malicious.
4. **Escaping arrest.** A suspect stabs a constable in the arm to escape arrest. → **s. 18** (intent to resist lawful apprehension), plus emergency worker aggravation.
5. **Smacking.** A father in England slaps his 9-year-old leaving a red mark that lasts a few seconds. → If only **battery**, reasonable punishment **may** be argued; if it causes **ABH** (bruising), the defence is **not available**.

:::es
1. Puño levantado y amenaza → **assault**.
2. Cortar la coleta → **s. 47 ABH**.
3. Labio partido por dentro → **wound** → s. 20.
4. Apuñalar a un agente para escapar → **s. 18**.
5. Bofetada a un hijo (Inglaterra): si solo es battery, puede alegarse castigo razonable; si hay **ABH**, no.
:::

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **s. 47 mens rea**: you **don't** need foresight of harm – only intent/recklessness for the assault/battery.
- **s. 20 mens rea**: foresight of **some** harm, not serious harm.
- **Wound** = **both layers** of skin. A broken bone **without** a skin break = GBH but **not** a wound.
- **s. 20 "inflict"** vs **s. 18 "cause"** – both can be by indirect means; *Burstow* says inflict needs no assault.
- **Reasonable punishment**: only **common assault/battery** in England – never ABH.
- **Consent**: no consent to ABH+ (*Brown*) unless an exception applies; **s. 71 DAA 2021** for sexual gratification.
- **Self-defence**: judged on the facts **as the defendant believed them**; householder: disproportionate OK, **grossly** disproportionate not.
- **Threats to kill**: intention that the victim **fears** it will be carried out – not intention to kill.

## Summary

1. Assault = apprehension of immediate force; battery = application of unlawful force (s. 39 CJA 1988, summary).
2. s. 47 ABH: assault/battery + ABH caused; mens rea only for the assault/battery.
3. s. 20: wound (both layers) or inflict GBH (really serious harm), maliciously = foresee some harm.
4. s. 18: wound/cause GBH with intent to do GBH or to resist arrest – specific intent, life max.
5. Consent limits (*Brown*, exceptions, s. 71 DAA 2021); reasonable punishment only for battery (England); self-defence s. 76 CJIA 2008.
