## Objectives

- Know when an offence is **racially or religiously aggravated** (Crime and Disorder Act 1998, s. 28).
- Know **racial group** and **religious group**, **presumed membership** and **association**.
- Know the **specific aggravated offences** (ss. 29–32) and the **sentence uplift** for other offences (Sentencing Act 2020, s. 66).

## Explanation

### 1. The test (CDA 1998, s. 28)

An offence is racially or religiously aggravated if:
- **(a)** at the time of committing the offence, **or immediately before or after** doing so, the offender **demonstrates towards the victim hostility** based on the victim's **membership (or presumed membership)** of a racial or religious group; **or**
- **(b)** the offence is **motivated (wholly or partly)** by **hostility towards members of a racial or religious group** based on their membership of that group.

- **Membership** includes **association** with members of that group (e.g. the white partner of a Black man).
- **Presumed**: what matters is what the offender presumed – it's **irrelevant if he was wrong** (e.g. abusing a Sikh man as a "Muslim").
- It's **immaterial** that the hostility is **also based on any other factor**.
- Under (a), the **motive** doesn't matter – using racial abuse during the offence is enough (*DPP v Woods* 2002: it doesn't matter whether the victim was actually upset by the words). Abuse must be **towards the victim**.

:::es
**Agravante (CDA 1998 s. 28)**: (a) **al cometer el delito, o justo antes o después**, el autor **muestra hostilidad hacia la víctima** por su pertenencia (o **presunta** pertenencia) a un grupo racial o religioso; **o** (b) el delito está **motivado (total o parcialmente)** por hostilidad hacia ese grupo. Incluye la **asociación** con miembros del grupo; da igual que el autor **se equivoque**; da igual que haya **otros motivos**. En (a) no importa el motivo ni si la víctima se ofendió (*DPP v Woods*).
:::

### 2. Racial and religious groups

- **Racial group**: a group of persons defined by reference to **race, colour, nationality (including citizenship) or ethnic or national origins**. "**Bloody foreigners**" (*R v Rogers* 2007, HL) – a racial group defined by **not being British** counts. Ethnic groups include **Sikhs** and **Jews** (*Mandla v Dowell Lee* 1983), **Romany Gypsies** and **Irish Travellers**; **Rastafarians** have been held not to be (⚠).
- **Religious group**: a group defined by reference to **religious belief or lack of religious belief** (e.g. Muslims, Christians, atheists).

### 3. The aggravated offences (ss. 29–32) – higher maximums

| Section | Basic offences | Aggravated max (⚠) |
|---|---|---|
| s. 29 | **Assaults**: s. 20 wounding/GBH, s. 47 ABH, common assault | 7 yrs (s. 20/s. 47); 2 yrs (common assault) |
| s. 30 | **Criminal damage** (s. 1(1) CDA 1971) | 14 yrs |
| s. 31 | **Public order**: s. 4, s. 4A, s. 5 POA 1986 | 2 yrs (s. 4/4A); fine (s. 5) |
| s. 32 | **Harassment/stalking**: PHA ss. 2, 2A, 4, 4A | 2 yrs (ss. 2/2A); 14 yrs (ss. 4/4A) |

- If the jury is not satisfied about aggravation, they can convict of the **basic** offence.
- Racially aggravated criminal damage is **either-way regardless of value**.

### 4. Other offences – sentence uplift

For **any other** offence (e.g. robbery, s. 18 GBH, murder), the court **must treat** hostility as an **aggravating factor** and state so in open court (Sentencing Act 2020, s. 66) – based on **race, religion, disability, sexual orientation or transgender identity**.

:::es
**Grupo racial**: definido por **raza, color, nacionalidad (incluida ciudadanía) u origen étnico o nacional** (*Rogers*: "malditos extranjeros" = no británicos). **Sijs** y **judíos** son grupos **étnicos** (*Mandla*). **Grupo religioso**: por **creencia o falta de creencia** religiosa. **Delitos agravados (ss. 29–32)**: lesiones, daños (14 años), orden público (ss. 4, 4A, 5), acoso; penas mayores. Para **otros** delitos, el juez **aumenta la pena** (Sentencing Act 2020 s. 66: raza, religión, discapacidad, orientación sexual, identidad trans).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The taxi.** D assaults a taxi driver over a fare and, during the assault, shouts racial abuse. → **s. 29** (hostility **demonstrated at the time** – (a)), even if the motive was the fare.
2. **The wrong religion.** D attacks a Sikh man calling him a "terrorist Muslim". → Aggravated – **presumed** membership; mistake irrelevant.
3. **The partner.** D abuses a white woman because she's married to a Black man. → **Association** with members of a racial group.
4. **"Go back home."** D, in a queue, shouts "bloody foreigners" at Polish women and pushes one. → Racial group (non-British – *Rogers*); aggravated common assault.
5. **The robbery.** A robbery motivated by hostility towards gay men. → Not in ss. 29–32 → **sentence uplift** (s. 66).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- (a) demonstration: **at the time, immediately before or after**; (b) motivation: wholly **or partly**.
- **Presumed** membership counts; **association** counts; other factors immaterial.
- Only assaults (ss. 20, 47, common), criminal damage, public order (ss. 4, 4A, 5) and harassment/stalking have aggravated versions.
- Disability/sexual orientation/transgender → **sentence uplift**, not CDA aggravated offences.
- Jews and Sikhs = ethnic groups (racial); Muslims = religious group.

## Summary

1. s. 28 test: demonstration of hostility (at/immediately before or after) or motivation (wholly/partly).
2. Racial group (race, colour, nationality, ethnic/national origins); religious group (belief or lack of it); presumed membership; association.
3. Aggravated offences: s. 29 assaults, s. 30 criminal damage, s. 31 public order, s. 32 harassment.
4. Other offences: s. 66 Sentencing Act 2020 uplift (incl. disability, sexual orientation, transgender).
