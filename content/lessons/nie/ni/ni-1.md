## Objectives

- Know the **format** of the NIE: number of questions, time and pass mark.
- Understand **where the questions come from** and what is not allowed.
- Plan your **time per question** and a strategy for difficult questions.

## Explanation

The **National Investigators' Examination (NIE)** is the law exam that trainee detectives must pass as part of PIP level 2 (Professionalising Investigation Programme).

- **80 multiple-choice questions** in **2 hours** (120 minutes).
- Only **70** questions are **scored**. The other **10** are trial questions that do not count – and you do **not** know which ones they are.
- **Pass mark: 55.7 %**, which is **39 out of 70**.
- Every question comes **only** from **Blackstone's Police Investigators' Manual** for the exam year (for you: **2027 edition**).
- Using **AI during the exam is prohibited**.
- The syllabus has **43 chapters in 4 parts** (Appendix A of the handbook).

:::es
- **80 preguntas tipo test** en **2 horas**.
- Solo **70 puntúan**; 10 son de prueba y **no sabes cuáles** → responde todas como si contaran.
- **Aprobado: 55,7 % = 39 de 70.**
- Las preguntas salen **solo** del **Blackstone's Police Investigators' Manual del año** (el tuyo: **2027**).
- **Prohibido usar IA** durante el examen.
- **43 capítulos en 4 partes.**
:::

### Time per question

120 minutes ÷ 80 questions = **90 seconds per question**. A simple rule:

- At **30 minutes** you should be around question **20**.
- At **60 minutes**, around question **40**.
- At **90 minutes**, around question **60**.
- Keep **10–15 minutes** at the end to review flagged questions.

:::es
**90 segundos por pregunta.** Referencias: minuto 30 → pregunta 20; minuto 60 → 40; minuto 90 → 60. Guarda 10–15 minutos para revisar las marcadas. El modo examen de la app te dice por qué pregunta deberías ir.
:::

### How NIE questions are written

NIE questions are usually **scenario-based**: a short story with names (e.g. "DC SMITH arrests JONES…") and four options. Many options start with **"Yes / No, because…"** – both the answer **and the reason** must be correct. Read the **last line** first so you know what is being asked, then read the scenario looking for the **key facts** (age, time, place, what the person knew or believed, who authorised what).

:::es
Las preguntas suelen ser **escenarios** con nombres y 4 opciones. Muchas empiezan con **"Yes/No, because…"**: tienen que ser correctos **el sí/no y el motivo**. Truco: lee primero **la última frase** (qué te preguntan) y luego busca los **datos clave**: edades, horas, lugar, qué sabía o creía la persona, quién autorizó qué.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. At minute 45 you are on question 25. → You are **behind** (you should be near 30). Answer faster, flag doubtful questions and move on.
2. You meet a question about a chapter you never studied. → **Never leave it blank**: eliminate obviously wrong options and choose.

:::es
1. Minuto 45 y vas por la 25 → vas **atrasada** (deberías ir por la 30). Acelera y marca las dudosas.
2. Pregunta de algo que no estudiaste → **nunca en blanco**: descarta y elige.
:::

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Studying from websites or old manuals that **contradict** the 2027 Blackstone's. The manual always wins in the exam.
- Spending 5 minutes on one question.
- Leaving answers blank (there is no negative marking described in the handbook ⚠ VERIFICAR).
- Choosing "Yes" because the conclusion is right but the **reason** is wrong.

## Summary

1. 80 questions, 2 hours, 70 scored, 10 unscored (unknown).
2. Pass: 39/70 = 55.7 %.
3. Questions only from Blackstone's Investigators' Manual of the exam year (2027).
4. No AI in the exam.
5. 90 seconds per question; flag and move on.
