## Objectives

- Know **simple criminal damage** (Criminal Damage Act 1971, s. 1(1)), **aggravated** criminal damage (s. 1(2)) and **arson** (s. 1(3)).
- Apply the meanings of **damage**, **property**, **belonging to another** and **recklessness** (*R v G*).
- Know the **lawful excuse** defence (s. 5), **threats** (s. 2) and **possession with intent** (s. 3).

## Explanation

### 1. Simple criminal damage (s. 1(1))

A person who **without lawful excuse destroys or damages any property belonging to another**, **intending** to destroy or damage it **or being reckless** as to whether it would be destroyed or damaged.

- **Either-way**; max **10 years**. If the value of the damage is **£5,000 or less**, it is tried **summarily** (⚠ VERIFICAR pena).
- **Racially or religiously aggravated** criminal damage (Crime and Disorder Act 1998, s. 30) – max **14 years**.

### 2. Key definitions

- **Damage**: a question of fact and degree; includes **temporary** impairment of **value or usefulness** – e.g. graffiti even in water-soluble paint (*Hardman v CC Avon* 1986), trampling grass; **not** spitting on a coat that is easily wiped clean (⚠ *A (a juvenile) v R* 1978). Altering computer data is generally **not** criminal damage (Computer Misuse Act 1990, unless physical condition impaired).
- **Property** (s. 10(1)): property of a **tangible** nature, **real or personal**, including **money**, and **wild creatures tamed or ordinarily kept in captivity**; but **not** **mushrooms, flowers, fruit or foliage of plants growing wild**.
- **Belonging to another** (s. 10(2)): anyone with **custody or control**, a **proprietary right or interest**, or a **charge** on it. You can commit s. 1(1) on property you **co-own**.
- **Reckless** (*R v G* 2003, HL): A is aware of a **risk** that damage will occur (or that a circumstance exists) and, in the circumstances known to him, it is **unreasonable** to take the risk – a **subjective** test (children/no awareness = not reckless).

:::es
**Daños simples (s. 1(1))**: destruir o dañar **propiedad ajena**, **sin excusa legal**, con **intención o imprudencia**. Either-way, máx. **10 años** (≤ **£5,000** → juicio sumario ⚠). **Daño**: incluye pérdida **temporal** de valor o utilidad (grafitis). **Propiedad**: **tangible**, incluye dinero y animales salvajes **domesticados o en cautividad**; **no** setas, flores, frutos u hojas **silvestres**. **Ajena**: quien tiene **custodia/control**, derecho o carga. **Imprudencia (R v G)**: **subjetiva** – conocía el riesgo y era irrazonable correrlo.
:::

### 3. Lawful excuse (s. 5) – only for s. 1(1), s. 2 and s. 3 (not endangering life)

A has a lawful excuse if:
- (a) he **believed** that the person entitled to consent **had consented, or would have consented** if they had known the circumstances; **or**
- (b) he damaged the property **to protect property** (his own or another's) which he **believed was in immediate need of protection**, and that the **means** adopted were **reasonable** in all the circumstances.

The belief need only be **honestly held** (s. 5(3)) – even if caused by **intoxication** (*Jaggard v Dickinson* 1981). But for (b), whether the act was done "**in order to protect property**" is judged **objectively** (*R v Hill and Hall* 1989).

:::es
**Excusa legal (s. 5)**: (a) creía que el titular **había consentido o habría consentido**; o (b) dañó para **proteger bienes** que creía en **peligro inmediato** y con **medios razonables**. Basta una creencia **honesta**, aunque sea por **embriaguez** (*Jaggard*). **No** se aplica a los daños con peligro para la vida.
:::

### 4. Aggravated criminal damage (s. 1(2)) and arson (s. 1(3))

**s. 1(2)**: destroys or damages **any property** (**including his own**), intending or reckless as to the damage, **and intending** by the destruction/damage **to endanger the life of another** or **being reckless** as to whether life would be endangered. Max **life**. Indictable only.

- Life must be endangered **by the damage itself**, not by the act causing it (*R v Steer* 1987 – shooting through a window: danger came from the bullets, not the broken glass).
- Life need **not actually** be endangered.
- **s. 5 lawful excuse does not apply**.

**Arson (s. 1(3))**: an offence under s. 1 committed by **destroying or damaging property by fire** – max **life** (also aggravated arson).

### 5. Threats (s. 2) and possession (s. 3)

- **s. 2**: without lawful excuse, makes to another a **threat**, **intending** that the other **would fear it would be carried out**, to destroy or damage **property belonging to that other or a third person**, or **his own** property in a way likely to endanger the life of that other or a third person. Max **10 years**.
- **s. 3**: has **anything in his custody or under his control**, **intending** without lawful excuse to use it (or cause/permit another to use it) to destroy or damage property. Max **10 years**. (Conditional intent is enough.)

:::es
**Daños agravados (s. 1(2))**: **cualquier** propiedad (también **la propia**) con intención o imprudencia de **poner en peligro la vida** de otro; el peligro debe venir **del daño** (*Steer*); no hace falta que la vida corra peligro real; **perpetua**. **Incendio (s. 1(3))**: daños **por fuego**, perpetua. **s. 2 amenazas** de dañar bienes con intención de que el otro tema que se cumpla; **s. 3** tener algo con intención de usarlo para dañar; máx. **10 años**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **Graffiti.** Youths spray tags with chalk spray that washes off with effort. → Likely **damage** (temporary impairment; cost of cleaning).
2. **Own car.** A sets fire to his own car outside a block of flats. → Not s. 1(1) (own property), but **aggravated arson** if reckless as to endangering life.
3. **The neighbour's dog.** A breaks a fence to rescue his dog that's being attacked. → **s. 5(2)(b)** – protecting property (animals are property) in immediate need.
4. **Drunk mistake.** A, drunk, breaks into a house believing it's his friend's who would consent. → **s. 5(2)(a)**, honest belief – *Jaggard*.
5. **Spray paint in the bag.** Stopped at night with spray cans, intending to tag a train. → **s. 3** possession with intent.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- s. 1(1) needs property **belonging to another**; s. 1(2) can be **own** property.
- **Wild** mushrooms/flowers/fruit/foliage are **not** property for CDA (but picking them **for sale** can be theft – that's the Theft Act).
- Lawful excuse (s. 5) **not** available for endangering life.
- *R v G*: recklessness is **subjective**.
- *Steer*: danger must come from the **damage**.

## Summary

1. s. 1(1): damage to property of another, intent/recklessness, no lawful excuse – 10 yrs (≤ £5,000 summary).
2. Damage, property (tangible; not wild plants), belonging to another, R v G recklessness.
3. s. 5 lawful excuse: consent belief; protection of property (immediate need, reasonable means).
4. s. 1(2) aggravated (endanger life – life max) and s. 1(3) arson.
5. s. 2 threats; s. 3 possession with intent.
