> ⚠ **Comprueba con tu edición 2027.** La **Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (POCA)** se ha reformado varias veces (p. ej. Criminal Finances Act 2017, Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023). **Importes mínimos, plazos y rangos**, siempre del manual.

## Objectives

- Understand **criminal property** and the three main **money laundering offences** (POCA ss. 327, 328, 329).
- Know the **defences**, especially **authorised disclosure** (a SAR/DAML) and **adequate consideration**.
- Get an overview of **cash seizure and forfeiture**, **confiscation**, **restraint** and other civil powers.

## Explanation

### 1. Criminal property (s. 340)

Property is **criminal property** if:
- it **constitutes** a person's **benefit from criminal conduct** or **represents** such a benefit (in whole or part, directly or indirectly), **and**
- the alleged offender **knows or suspects** that it constitutes or represents such a benefit.

**Criminal conduct** = conduct that is an offence in the UK (or would be if it happened here). **Suspicion** is a **low** threshold: a possibility that is more than fanciful (⚠ VERIFICAR *R v Da Silva* (2006)).

:::es
**Bienes de origen delictivo (s. 340)**: los que **son o representan** el **beneficio de una conducta delictiva**, **y** el autor **sabe o sospecha** que lo son. **Sospecha** = umbral **bajo** (una posibilidad más que fantasiosa – *Da Silva* ⚠).
:::

### 2. The three principal money laundering offences

| Section | Conduct |
|---|---|
| **s. 327** | **concealing, disguising, converting, transferring** criminal property, or **removing** it from England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland |
| **s. 328** | entering into or becoming **concerned in an arrangement** which you know or suspect **facilitates** the acquisition, retention, use or control of criminal property **by or on behalf of another** |
| **s. 329** | **acquiring, using or having possession** of criminal property |

All are **either-way**; maximum **14 years** (⚠ VERIFICAR).

:::es
- **s. 327**: **ocultar, disfrazar, convertir, transferir** o **sacar** del país bienes delictivos.
- **s. 328**: participar en un **acuerdo** que **facilita** a otro adquirir, retener, usar o controlar bienes delictivos.
- **s. 329**: **adquirir, usar o poseer** bienes delictivos.
Mixtos; máx. 14 años (⚠).
:::

### 3. Defences

- **Authorised disclosure** (s. 338): the person makes a disclosure (a **Suspicious Activity Report – SAR**) to the **NCA** (UK Financial Intelligence Unit) and gets **appropriate consent** (a **DAML – Defence Against Money Laundering**) before acting, or makes it as soon as practicable after acting with a reasonable excuse (⚠ VERIFICAR plazos de consentimiento: notice/moratorium periods).
- **Adequate consideration** (s. 329(2)(c)): a person who acquires/uses/possesses property **for adequate consideration** (e.g. a shop selling goods at a fair price to a criminal) is **not** guilty under **s. 329** – but this does **not** apply if the consideration is goods/services that help the criminal conduct (⚠ VERIFICAR).
- **Reasonable excuse** for not making a disclosure; some **overseas** conduct exceptions; deposit-taking body threshold exceptions (⚠).

:::es
**Defensas**: **comunicación autorizada** (s. 338): informar a la **NCA** con un **SAR** y obtener **consentimiento (DAML)** antes de actuar. **Contraprestación adecuada** (s. 329): quien vende a precio justo a un delincuente no comete s. 329 (con excepciones). **Excusa razonable**.
:::

### 4. Regulated sector offences (overview)

Banks, accountants, estate agents, etc. (the **regulated sector**) commit offences if they **fail to disclose** knowledge or suspicion of money laundering (s. 330) and must not **tip off** (s. 333A) a suspect that a disclosure has been made (⚠ VERIFICAR qué incluye tu edición).

### 5. Recovering the proceeds (overview)

- **Confiscation orders** (Part 2) – after **conviction**, the Crown Court can order the defendant to pay the value of their **benefit**; "**criminal lifestyle**" rules allow assumptions about assets.
- **Restraint orders** – freeze assets to stop them being dissipated before confiscation.
- **Cash seizure and forfeiture** (Part 5, Chapter 3): officers can **search for and seize cash** (including some **portable valuables**) of at least the **minimum amount** (⚠ **£1,000**) if they have **reasonable grounds to suspect** it is **recoverable property** (obtained through unlawful conduct) or **intended for use in unlawful conduct**. Initial detention **48 hours**, then a **magistrates' court** can order continued detention (⚠ VERIFICAR periodos máximos), and later **forfeiture** – a **civil** process (balance of probabilities), no conviction needed.
- **Account freezing and forfeiture orders**, **unexplained wealth orders** (Criminal Finances Act 2017) (⚠).

:::es
**Recuperación**: **orden de decomiso** (tras **condena**, con reglas de "**estilo de vida delictivo**"); **orden de congelación** de bienes (*restraint*); **incautación de efectivo** (Parte 5): con **sospecha razonable**, desde el **importe mínimo** (⚠ £1.000), **48 h** y luego el **juzgado** decide prolongar y **decomisar** (proceso **civil**, sin condena). Además, congelación de **cuentas** y **unexplained wealth orders** (⚠).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The girlfriend's car.** A drug dealer buys his girlfriend a £30,000 car with drug money; she suspects where the money came from. → She **possesses/uses** criminal property → **s. 329**.
2. **The money mule.** A student lets a stranger pay £8,000 into his account and transfers it on for a £500 fee, suspecting it's dodgy. → **s. 327** (transferring) / **s. 328** (arrangement).
3. **Cash in the car.** A stop finds £15,000 in vacuum-packed bundles with traces of cocaine and no explanation. → **Cash seizure** under POCA (reasonable grounds to suspect recoverable property / intended for unlawful use).
4. **The jeweller.** A jeweller sells a watch at full price to a known burglar. → **Adequate consideration** may be a defence to s. 329 for the money he receives (⚠ check).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Criminal property** needs **knowledge or suspicion** by the alleged offender.
- **s. 328** = **arrangement** that facilitates **another's** acquisition/retention/use/control.
- **Adequate consideration** defence is for **s. 329**.
- **Cash forfeiture** is **civil** – no conviction needed; minimum amount applies (⚠ £1,000).
- **SAR/DAML** = authorised disclosure to the **NCA**.

## Summary

1. Criminal property = benefit from criminal conduct + offender knows or suspects (s. 340).
2. s. 327 conceal/disguise/convert/transfer/remove; s. 328 arrangements; s. 329 acquire/use/possess.
3. Defences: authorised disclosure (SAR/DAML to NCA); adequate consideration (s. 329); reasonable excuse.
4. Regulated sector: failure to disclose, tipping off.
5. Recovery: confiscation (after conviction), restraint, civil cash seizure/forfeiture (⚠ £1,000, 48 h), account freezing, UWOs.
