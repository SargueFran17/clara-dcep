## Objectives

- Break theft (Theft Act 1968, **s. 1**) into its **five elements** and know the **definitions in ss. 2–6**.
- Apply the key cases on **appropriation** (*Morris, Lawrence, Gomez, Hinks*) and **intention to permanently deprive** (*Lloyd*).
- Know the related offences: **TWOC** (s. 12), **aggravated vehicle-taking** (s. 12A), **removal of articles from public places** (s. 11), **abstracting electricity** (s. 13), **making off without payment** (TA 1978, s. 3) and **going equipped** (s. 25).

## Explanation

### 1. The definition (s. 1)

> A person is guilty of theft if he **dishonestly appropriates property belonging to another** with the **intention of permanently depriving** the other of it.

| Element | Section | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Dishonestly** | s. 2 (+ *Ivey*) | mens rea |
| **Appropriates** | s. 3 | actus reus |
| **Property** | s. 4 | actus reus |
| **Belonging to another** | s. 5 | actus reus |
| **Intention of permanently depriving** | s. 6 | mens rea |

Theft is **either-way**; maximum **7 years** (⚠ VERIFICAR).

:::es
**Hurto (s. 1)**: apropiarse **deshonestamente** de **bienes ajenos** con **intención de privar permanentemente** al otro. Cinco elementos: **deshonestidad** (s. 2), **apropiación** (s. 3), **bienes** (s. 4), **que pertenezcan a otro** (s. 5) e **intención de privar permanentemente** (s. 6). Delito **mixto**.
:::

### 2. Dishonestly (s. 2)

A person is **not** dishonest (s. 2(1)) if they appropriate believing:
- (a) they have **in law the right** to deprive the other of it;
- (b) they would have the **other's consent** if the other knew of the appropriation and the circumstances;
- (c) the person to whom the property belongs **cannot be discovered by taking reasonable steps** (not if the property came to them as trustee or personal representative).

These are **honest beliefs** – they don't need to be reasonable. **s. 2(2)**: an appropriation **may be dishonest even if the person is willing to pay**. Outside s. 2(1), use the ***Ivey*** test (1.2): actual knowledge/belief, then standards of ordinary decent people.

:::es
**No** hay deshonestidad si cree: a) que tiene **derecho legal**; b) que el dueño **consentiría**; c) que el dueño **no puede encontrarse** con pasos razonables. Basta que lo **crea de verdad**. **s. 2(2)**: puede ser deshonesto **aunque quiera pagar**. Si no aplica s. 2(1) → test **Ivey**.
:::

### 3. Appropriates (s. 3)

- **Any assumption of the rights of an owner** – and **one** right is enough (*R v Morris* (1984): swapping price labels in a shop).
- Appropriation can happen **with the owner's consent** (*Lawrence v MPC* (1972)), including consent obtained by **deception** (*DPP v Gomez* (1993)), and even through a **valid gift** (*R v Hinks* (2000)) – dishonesty then does the work.
- It includes a **later assumption** of rights over property first got innocently, by **keeping or dealing** with it as owner.
- **s. 3(2)**: a person who buys property **in good faith** and **for value** doesn't steal it by later keeping it if it turns out the seller had no title.

:::es
**Apropiación** = asumir **cualquier** derecho del propietario (*Morris*: cambiar etiquetas). Puede existir **con consentimiento** del dueño (*Lawrence*), incluso si se obtuvo con **engaño** (*Gomez*) o es un **regalo válido** (*Hinks*): lo decisivo será la deshonestidad. Incluye quedarse después algo obtenido inocentemente. **s. 3(2)**: el **comprador de buena fe y a título oneroso** no hurta al quedárselo.
:::

### 4. Property (s. 4)

Includes **money**, **real and personal property**, **things in action** (e.g. a bank credit balance) and **other intangible property**.

Limits:
- **Land** generally can't be stolen, except in specific cases (e.g. trustees; a person not in possession who **severs** something from land; tenants and **fixtures**).
- **Wild plants**: picking **mushrooms, flowers, fruit or foliage growing wild** is **not theft** unless done for **reward, sale or other commercial purpose** (s. 4(3)).
- **Wild creatures**: not theft unless **tamed/ordinarily kept in captivity**, or **reduced into possession** by someone (and not lost/abandoned) (s. 4(4)).
- **Confidential information** is **not** property (*Oxford v Moss* (1979)); **electricity** is not property – use **s. 13** (abstracting electricity).

:::es
**Bienes**: dinero, muebles e inmuebles, **derechos** (*things in action*, p. ej. saldo bancario) y otros intangibles. **Límites**: el **suelo** en general no; **plantas silvestres** (setas, flores, fruta, hojas) solo si es para **vender/lucro**; **animales salvajes** solo si están **domesticados/en cautividad** o alguien **los ha capturado**. La **información confidencial** no es "property" (*Oxford v Moss*); la **electricidad** tiene su delito propio (s. 13).
:::

### 5. Belonging to another (s. 5)

- Property belongs to anyone having **possession or control** of it, or **any proprietary right or interest** (s. 5(1)). So you can steal **your own** property from someone who has possession of it (e.g. taking your car from a garage that has a lien over it).
- **s. 5(3)**: property received under an **obligation to deal with it in a particular way** (e.g. money collected for a charity) is treated as belonging to the other.
- **s. 5(4)**: property got by **another's mistake**, where there is a **legal obligation to restore** it (e.g. an overpaid salary), is treated as belonging to the other.

:::es
**Pertenece a otro** quien tenga **posesión, control o algún derecho** sobre ella: puedes hurtar **tu propio** bien a quien lo tiene en posesión. **s. 5(3)**: recibido con **obligación de destinarlo** a algo (dinero de una colecta). **s. 5(4)**: recibido **por error** con obligación legal de **devolverlo** (sueldo pagado de más).
:::

### 6. Intention of permanently depriving (s. 6)

- Treating the thing **as your own to dispose of regardless** of the other's rights (s. 6(1)).
- **Borrowing or lending** counts only if it's **equivalent to an outright taking** – e.g. returning it when its **"goodness and virtue"** is gone (*R v Lloyd* (1985): copying films and returning the reels = no intention to permanently deprive; but using up a season ticket and returning it would be).
- **Conditional intention** (to take only if worth taking) – charge carefully (attempt/specific items).

:::es
**Intención de privar permanentemente**: tratarlo **como propio para disponer** de ello (s. 6). **Tomar prestado** solo cuenta si equivale a quitarlo del todo, p. ej. devolverlo **sin su valor** (*Lloyd*: copiar películas y devolverlas = no). Ej.: devolver un abono **ya gastado** sí.
:::

### 7. Related offences (overview ⚠ VERIFICAR qué entra)

- **s. 11** – removal of **articles displayed** to the public in buildings/grounds open to the public (e.g. a painting from a gallery), even without intention to permanently deprive.
- **s. 12** – **taking a conveyance without consent** (TWOC) or driving/being carried in one knowing it was taken – no need to intend to keep it. **Summary**.
- **s. 12A** – **aggravated vehicle-taking**: after a s. 12 offence, the vehicle is driven dangerously, or causes injury, damage to property, or damage to the vehicle.
- **s. 13** – **abstracting electricity** dishonestly.
- **Making off without payment** (Theft Act 1978, s. 3): knowing that **payment on the spot** is required, **dishonestly makes off** without paying, **intending to avoid payment**.
- **s. 25** – **going equipped**: when **not at his place of abode**, has with him any article for use in the course of or in connection with any **burglary or theft**.

## Police scenarios

1. **Price labels.** RAY swaps the price label on a TV for a cheaper one but is stopped before paying. → **Appropriation** already (*Morris*) – theft if dishonest with ITPD.
2. **The salary error.** The payroll pays MEG £2,000 too much; she knows and spends it. → **s. 5(4)** – belongs to the employer; theft if dishonest.
3. **Mushrooms.** LEO picks wild mushrooms to eat at home. → **Not theft** (s. 4(3)). If he picks them **to sell to a restaurant** – could be theft.
4. **Joyride.** Teenagers take a car, drive it around and leave it. → **TWOC (s. 12)**; if they crash it → **s. 12A**.
5. **The restaurant.** A diner leaves without paying after eating, intending never to pay. → **Making off without payment** (TA 1978, s. 3).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Appropriation **with consent** can still be theft (*Gomez*, *Hinks*).
- **Willingness to pay** doesn't stop dishonesty (s. 2(2)).
- **s. 2(1)** beliefs only need to be **honest**, not reasonable.
- **Wild plants** – only theft if for **commercial** purposes.
- **Information** and **electricity** are not "property" for s. 1.
- **TWOC** doesn't need intention to permanently deprive; **theft** does.

## Summary

1. Theft = dishonestly + appropriates + property + belonging to another + ITPD (s. 1).
2. s. 2: no dishonesty if belief in legal right, consent, owner untraceable; else *Ivey*.
3. s. 3: any assumption of an owner's rights; consent irrelevant (*Lawrence, Gomez, Hinks*).
4. s. 4–5: property limits (land, wild plants/creatures, information); possession/control; s. 5(3)/(4).
5. s. 6: treat as own to dispose of; borrowing = only if goodness/virtue gone (*Lloyd*); related offences s. 11, 12, 12A, 13, 25, TA 1978 s. 3.
