## Objectives

- Know the **forms of handling** in Theft Act 1968, **s. 22**: receiving, arranging to receive, and undertaking/assisting in **retention, removal, disposal or realisation**.
- Understand **"stolen goods"** (s. 24) and when goods **stop being stolen**.
- Apply the **knowing or believing** test and the special evidence rule in **s. 27(3)**.

## Explanation

### 1. The offence (s. 22(1))

> A person handles stolen goods if (**otherwise than in the course of the stealing**) **knowing or believing them to be stolen goods** he **dishonestly**:
> - **receives** the goods, or
> - **undertakes or assists** in their **retention, removal, disposal or realisation** **by or for the benefit of another person**, or
> - **arranges** to do so.

**Either-way**; maximum **14 years** – **higher than theft** (7 years) because handlers create the **market** for thieves.

:::es
**Receptación (s. 22)**: **fuera del momento del hurto**, **sabiendo o creyendo** que son robados, **deshonestamente**: **recibir** los bienes, o **realizar o ayudar** en su **retención, traslado, venta o realización** **por o en beneficio de otra persona**, o **acordar** hacerlo. Pena máxima **14 años** (más que el hurto: el receptador crea el **mercado**).
:::

### 2. Key elements

- **"Otherwise than in the course of the stealing"** – the **thief** is not normally a handler while still stealing; after the theft is complete, the thief can later handle.
- **Receiving** = taking **possession or control** (no need for "for another").
- **Undertakes or assists**… **by or for the benefit of another** – e.g. **hiding** goods for a burglar (retention), **driving** them away (removal), **selling** them (disposal/realisation). Doing these acts **only for yourself** (and not receiving) is **not** this form (*R v Bloxham* (1983) – a purchaser selling on for his own benefit was not acting "for another").
- **Arranges** – agreeing to do any of the above (the arrangement itself completes the offence).
- **Mere use** of stolen goods or **failing to tell police** is not "assisting in retention" by itself, but **lying to police** to hide them can be.

:::es
- **"Fuera del momento del hurto"**: el ladrón no es receptador mientras roba.
- **Recibir** = tomar **posesión o control**.
- **Ayudar** a **guardar, mover, vender o realizar** **para otro** (esconder lo de un ladrón, llevarlo en coche, venderlo). Hacerlo solo **para sí mismo** no entra en esta forma (*Bloxham*).
- **Acordar** hacerlo ya consuma el delito.
- **Usar** algo robado o **no decirlo** no basta por sí solo; **mentir a la policía** para ocultarlo puede bastar.
:::

### 3. Stolen goods (s. 24)

- Goods obtained by **theft**, **blackmail** or **fraud** (Fraud Act 2006) – in England and Wales or elsewhere (if criminal there too).
- **Goods representing** the stolen goods – e.g. **money** from selling a stolen car, in the hands of the thief or a handler.
- Goods **stop being stolen** once **restored** to the person they were stolen from or to **lawful possession or custody** (e.g. seized by police), or once the owner loses the right to restitution (s. 24(3)). So if police **recover** goods and then let a "controlled delivery" go ahead, the receiver may only be guilty of an **attempt** (⚠ VERIFICAR).
- **s. 24A**: dishonestly **retaining a wrongful credit** (e.g. money credited from a fraud).

:::es
**Bienes robados (s. 24)**: los obtenidos por **hurto, chantaje o fraude**, y los que los **representan** (p. ej. el dinero de vender un coche robado). **Dejan de ser robados** cuando vuelven al dueño o quedan bajo **custodia legal** (p. ej. incautados por la policía) → en una "entrega controlada" puede quedar solo en **tentativa**. **s. 24A**: retener un **abono indebido**.
:::

### 4. Knowing or believing

- **Knowing** – actual knowledge (e.g. told by the thief).
- **Believing** – the state of mind of someone who says *"I can't say for certain they're stolen, but there can be no other reasonable conclusion"* (*R v Hall* (1985)).
- **Suspicion is not enough**, even strong suspicion; but **deliberately shutting your eyes** may support belief. The test is **subjective** – what this defendant knew/believed (*R v Grainge* (1974)).
- **Dishonestly** – *Ivey* test.

:::es
**Saber** (conocimiento) o **creer** ("no puedo estar segura, pero no hay otra conclusión razonable" – *Hall*). La **sospecha no basta**, aunque sea fuerte; el test es **subjetivo** (*Grainge*). Deshonestidad: test **Ivey**.
:::

### 5. Special evidence rule – s. 27(3)

To prove **knowledge or belief**, the prosecution may (with conditions) adduce evidence that the defendant:
- (a) **handled** (or was involved with) **other stolen goods** stolen **within the 12 months** before the offence; and/or
- (b) has a **conviction for theft or handling** within the **previous 5 years**,

provided **7 days' notice** is given (for (b)) and the goods' handling is part of the charge being tried (⚠ VERIFICAR condiciones y relación con bad character).

:::es
**s. 27(3)**: para probar que **sabía o creía**, se puede usar que **manejó otros bienes robados en los 12 meses anteriores** o que tiene **condena por hurto o receptación en los últimos 5 años** (con aviso de 7 días) (⚠ verifica).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The pub sale.** RAY buys a £1,000 laptop for £50 in a pub from a man who says "don't ask". RAY says he thought it was "probably dodgy". → Is that **belief** or only **suspicion**? Consider *Hall*: no other reasonable conclusion? Price and words are evidence.
2. **The garage.** SUE lets her brother store stolen bikes in her garage, knowing they're stolen. → **Assisting in retention for the benefit of another**.
3. **Controlled delivery.** Police seize a stolen parcel, then allow it to be delivered to the suspect. → Goods were in **lawful custody** – consider **attempted** handling.
4. **The cash.** A burglar sells stolen jewellery and gives the cash to his girlfriend, who knows where it came from. → The cash **represents** stolen goods → she can **receive** it.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Suspicion ≠ belief** (*Hall*, *Grainge*).
- The **thief** isn't a handler "in the course of the stealing".
- **For the benefit of another** – not for yourself only (*Bloxham*).
- Goods recovered by police are **no longer stolen** (s. 24(3)).
- **Maximum 14 years** – higher than theft.
- **Arranging** completes the offence.

## Summary

1. s. 22: receive / arrange to receive / undertake or assist in retention, removal, disposal, realisation for another / arrange to do so.
2. Must be otherwise than in the course of stealing; knowing or believing; dishonestly.
3. Stolen goods: theft, blackmail, fraud + goods representing them; stop being stolen when restored/in lawful custody (s. 24).
4. Belief ≠ suspicion (*Hall*); subjective (*Grainge*).
5. s. 27(3) evidence (12 months handling / 5 years conviction); max 14 years.
