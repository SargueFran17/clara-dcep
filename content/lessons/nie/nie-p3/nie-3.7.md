## Objectives

- Know the offence of **fraud** (Fraud Act 2006, s. 1) and its three ways: **false representation** (s. 2), **failing to disclose information** (s. 3) and **abuse of position** (s. 4).
- Apply **gain and loss** (s. 5) and the test for **dishonesty** (*Ivey v Genting*; *R v Barton and Booth*).
- Know **articles for use in frauds** (ss. 6–8) and **obtaining services dishonestly** (s. 11).

## Explanation

### 1. Fraud (s. 1) – a conduct crime

A person is guilty of **fraud** if he commits it in any of three ways (ss. 2–4). Max **10 years**, either-way. **Nobody needs to be deceived** and **no gain or loss needs to happen** – what matters is the **dishonest conduct** with the **intention** to make a gain or cause a loss.

### 2. False representation (s. 2)

A **dishonestly makes a false representation**, intending, by making it, **to make a gain** for himself or another, **or to cause loss** to another or **expose another to a risk of loss**.

- A representation is **false** if it is **untrue or misleading** **and** the person making it **knows that it is, or might be**, untrue or misleading.
- **Representation** = any representation as to **fact or law**, including as to the **state of mind** of the person making it or any other person. It can be **express or implied** (e.g. using a card implies authority to use it – *R v Lambie* principle; ordering a meal implies intent to pay).
- A representation can be made to a **machine** or system (s. 2(5)) – e.g. entering stolen card details online.

:::es
**Fraude (s. 1)**: delito de **conducta** – no hace falta que nadie sea engañado ni que haya ganancia real. Máx. **10 años**. **Falsa representación (s. 2)**: hacer **deshonestamente** una afirmación **falsa** (falsa o engañosa, sabiendo que lo es **o puede serlo**) con intención de **ganar** o causar **pérdida o riesgo de pérdida**. Puede ser **expresa o implícita** (usar una tarjeta implica que puedes usarla) y hecha a una **máquina**.
:::

### 3. Failing to disclose (s. 3) and abuse of position (s. 4)

- **s. 3**: A **dishonestly fails to disclose** to another information which he is under a **legal duty** to disclose (statute, contract, custom, fiduciary relationship…), intending gain/loss. Example: not telling an insurer about a previous claim when the form requires it; not telling the benefits office about a change ⚠.
- **s. 4**: A **occupies a position** in which he is **expected to safeguard, or not to act against, the financial interests of another person**, and **dishonestly abuses** that position, intending gain/loss. It can be an **omission**. Examples: a carer taking money from an elderly person's account; an employee diverting company orders to his own business.

:::es
**s. 3**: no revelar información que hay **obligación legal** de revelar. **s. 4**: **abuso de posición** – alguien que debe **proteger los intereses económicos** de otro (cuidador, empleado, apoderado) abusa de ello, incluso por **omisión**.
:::

### 4. Gain and loss (s. 5)

Only **money or other property** (real or personal, incl. things in action and intangible property), whether **temporary or permanent**. **Gain** includes **keeping what one has**; **loss** includes **not getting what one might get**.

### 5. Dishonesty

*Ivey v Genting Casinos* (2017, SC), confirmed for criminal cases by *R v Barton and Booth* (2020, CA):
1. What was the defendant's **actual state of knowledge or belief** as to the facts? (the belief need not be reasonable, but reasonableness helps show it was genuine);
2. Given that belief, was the conduct **dishonest by the standards of ordinary decent people**? – **objective**. The old second limb of *Ghosh* (did D realise ordinary people would think it dishonest?) **no longer applies**.

:::es
**Ganancia/pérdida (s. 5)**: solo **dinero u otros bienes**, temporal o permanente; ganar incluye **quedarse lo que se tiene**; perder incluye **no recibir lo que se podría**. **Deshonestidad (Ivey / Barton)**: (1) qué **sabía o creía** realmente el acusado; (2) con esa creencia, ¿es deshonesto según **personas normales y decentes**? (objetivo). Ya **no** se pregunta si él se daba cuenta (fin de *Ghosh*).
:::

### 6. Articles for use in frauds (ss. 6–8)

- **s. 6**: **possession or control** of any **article** for use **in the course of or in connection with any fraud**. Max **5 years**. (Can be at home – no "away from abode" rule.) A general intention that it be used for fraud is required (⚠ *R v Sakalauskas*).
- **s. 7**: **making, adapting, supplying or offering to supply** any article, knowing it is designed or adapted for use in fraud, or intending it to be used to commit/assist fraud. Max **10 years**.
- **s. 8**: "article" includes **programs or data held in electronic form** (e.g. phishing kits, lists of stolen card details).

### 7. Obtaining services dishonestly (s. 11)

A **obtains services** for himself or another **by a dishonest act**, the services are made available **on the basis that payment has been, is being or will be made**, A obtains them **without payment in full**, **knowing** they are being made available on that basis (or might be), and **intending** that payment will not be made in full. Max **5 years**.

- Requires an **act** (not omission) and the services must actually be **obtained** – unlike s. 2. Example: sneaking into a cinema/football match; using false card details to stream films.

:::es
**s. 6** tener un **artículo** para cometer fraudes (máx. **5 años**, incluso en casa); **s. 7** fabricarlo o suministrarlo (máx. **10 años**); **s. 8** incluye **programas y datos** electrónicos. **s. 11 obtener servicios deshonestamente**: hace falta un **acto** y **obtener** de verdad el servicio sin pagar (colarse en un partido). Máx. **5 años**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The phishing email.** A sends emails pretending to be a bank asking for passwords; nobody replies. → **s. 2** complete (no one needs to be deceived); the email template = **article** (ss. 6–7).
2. **The stolen card at the till.** A pays with a found card. → **s. 2** – implied representation of authority to use it.
3. **The carer.** A carer uses her client's bank card for her own shopping. → **s. 4** abuse of position (and s. 2 at the till).
4. **The insurance form.** A doesn't declare his previous convictions where the form requires it. → **s. 3** (legal duty from contract) ⚠.
5. **Over the fence.** A climbs into a festival without a ticket. → **s. 11** obtaining services dishonestly (services obtained, by an act).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- s. 2 is complete when the representation is **made** – no need for anyone to believe it or for any gain.
- "False" = untrue or misleading **and** A knows it **is or might be**.
- Gain/loss is about **money or property** only – **not** e.g. sexual gratification.
- s. 11 needs services **actually obtained** and a **dishonest act**.
- Dishonesty test after *Barton*: no subjective second limb.

## Summary

1. Fraud (s. 1) – 10 yrs: false representation (s. 2), failing to disclose (s. 3), abuse of position (s. 4).
2. Representation: express/implied, fact/law/state of mind, to machines.
3. Gain/loss: money or property, temporary or permanent (s. 5).
4. Dishonesty: Ivey/Barton – actual belief + objective standard.
5. s. 6 possession of articles (5 yrs), s. 7 making/supplying (10 yrs), s. 8 includes data; s. 11 obtaining services (5 yrs).
