## Objectives

- Know the elements of **blackmail** (Theft Act 1968, **s. 21**): **demand**, **menaces**, **unwarranted**, **view to gain / intent to cause loss**.
- Understand when a demand is **warranted** (the two-part belief test).
- Distinguish blackmail from **robbery** and other threats.

## Explanation

### 1. The offence (s. 21(1))

> A person is guilty of blackmail if, **with a view to gain** for himself or another **or with intent to cause loss** to another, he makes any **unwarranted demand with menaces**.

**Indictable only**; maximum **14 years** (⚠ VERIFICAR).

:::es
**Chantaje (s. 21)**: con **ánimo de lucro** (para sí o para otro) **o intención de causar pérdida** a otro, hacer una **exigencia injustificada** **con amenazas** (*menaces*). Solo **Crown Court**; máx. 14 años (⚠).
:::

### 2. Demand

- Can be **express or implied**, in any form: words, conduct, letter, text, email.
- The demand is **made** when it is **sent** (e.g. when a letter is **posted**) – it doesn't matter if it never arrives (*Treacy v DPP* (1971)).
- The **nature of the act or omission demanded** is **immaterial** (s. 21(2)) – e.g. demanding money, or demanding that someone does something.

### 3. Menaces

- Wider than threats of violence: threats of **any action detrimental to or unpleasant** to the person addressed (*Thorne v Motor Trade Association* (1937)) – e.g. threatening to **reveal secrets**, report to employers, damage reputation.
- The menaces must be of such a nature that an **ordinary person of normal stability and courage** might be influenced to accede (*R v Clear* (1968)). If the victim is **unusually timid** and the defendant **knows** it, that can still be menaces (⚠ VERIFICAR – *R v Garwood* (1987)).

:::es
**Menaces** (amenazas): no solo violencia, sino **cualquier acción perjudicial o desagradable** (*Thorne*): revelar secretos, contarlo al jefe… Deben poder **influir a una persona normal** (*Clear*); si la víctima es especialmente **asustadiza** y el acusado **lo sabe**, también cuenta (⚠ *Garwood*).
:::

### 4. Unwarranted

A demand with menaces is **unwarranted unless** the person making it **does so in the belief**:
- (a) that he has **reasonable grounds for making the demand**; **and**
- (b) that the **use of the menaces is a proper means** of reinforcing the demand (s. 21(1)).

Both beliefs are **subjective** (what *he* believed) – but a person can hardly believe it is "proper" to threaten something he knows is **criminal** (e.g. violence).

:::es
La exigencia es **injustificada salvo** que el autor **crea**: a) que tiene **motivos razonables para exigir**, **y** b) que **amenazar es un medio adecuado** para reforzarla. Creencias **subjetivas**, pero es difícil creer "adecuado" amenazar con algo **delictivo**.
:::

### 5. Gain and loss (s. 34(2))

**"Gain"** and **"loss"** relate only to **money or other property** – temporary or permanent. Gain includes **keeping what one has**; loss includes **not getting what one might get**. Demands for **sex** or other non-property advantages are **not** blackmail (other offences may apply). A demand for money **actually owed** can still be blackmail if the menaces make it unwarranted.

:::es
**Lucro/pérdida (s. 34)**: solo **dinero u otros bienes** (temporal o permanente). Incluye **conservar** lo que tienes y **no obtener** lo que podrías. Exigir **sexo** no es chantaje (pero hay otros delitos). Reclamar una **deuda real** puede ser chantaje si la amenaza no es adecuada.
:::

### 6. Blackmail vs robbery

| | Blackmail | Robbery |
|---|---|---|
| Threat | menaces of **anything** detrimental | force **then and there** |
| Timing | future is fine | immediate |
| Completion | when the **demand is made** | when the **theft** is complete |

## Police scenarios

1. **The secret.** "Pay me £5,000 or I'll tell your wife about the affair." → **Blackmail** (menace = revealing a secret; view to gain).
2. **The posted letter.** A demand letter is posted in Portsmouth but lost in the post. → Blackmail **complete** when posted (*Treacy*).
3. **The debt.** A builder owed £2,000 texts: "Pay me by Friday or I'll break your legs." → He may believe he has reasonable grounds for the **demand**, but not that **violence** is a **proper** means → **unwarranted** → blackmail.
4. **Sexual demand.** "Sleep with me or I'll post your photos." → **Not blackmail** (no gain/loss in property) – consider **other offences** (e.g. sexual offences, disclosing private sexual images).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Blackmail is **complete on making the demand** – nothing has to be handed over.
- **Menaces** ≠ only violence.
- **Unwarranted**: both beliefs needed – reasonable grounds **for the demand** AND menaces a **proper means**.
- Gain/loss = **money or property** only.
- A **future** threat of violence → blackmail, not robbery.

## Summary

1. Blackmail: unwarranted demand with menaces, with a view to gain or intent to cause loss (s. 21).
2. Demand made when sent (*Treacy*); any form.
3. Menaces: anything detrimental/unpleasant (*Thorne*); ordinary person test (*Clear*).
4. Warranted only if belief in reasonable grounds for demand AND menaces proper means.
5. Gain/loss = money/property (s. 34); indictable only.
