## Objectives

- Know the elements of **robbery** (Theft Act 1968, **s. 8**): a **theft** + **force or fear of force** + **timing** + **purpose**.
- Apply the key cases: *Robinson*, *Dawson and James*, *Clouden*, *Hale*.
- Know **assault with intent to rob** (s. 8(2)).

## Explanation

### 1. The definition (s. 8(1))

> A person is guilty of robbery if he **steals**, and **immediately before or at the time of doing so**, and **in order to do so**, he **uses force on any person** or **puts or seeks to put any person in fear of being then and there subjected to force**.

Robbery is **indictable only**; maximum **life imprisonment**.

:::es
**Robo con violencia o intimidación (s. 8)**: **hurtar** y, **inmediatamente antes o en el momento**, y **para hacerlo**, **usar fuerza** contra **cualquier persona** o hacer (o intentar hacer) que alguien **tema** que se usará fuerza **en ese mismo momento**. Solo **Crown Court**; pena máxima **cadena perpetua**.
:::

### 2. There must be a theft

All elements of **theft** must be proved. If there is **no theft**, there is **no robbery**: in ***R v Robinson* (1977)** the defendant used force to get money he honestly believed he was owed – **not dishonest** (s. 2(1)(a)) → no theft → no robbery (though assault offences may apply).

:::es
Tiene que haber **hurto completo**. En *Robinson*, usó fuerza para cobrar un dinero que creía honestamente que le debían → sin deshonestidad no hay hurto → **no hay robo** (aunque sí puede haber agresión).
:::

### 3. Force

- **Force** is an ordinary word for the jury; **little force** can be enough – e.g. **jostling** someone to make them lose balance (*R v Dawson and James* (1976)).
- Force on the **property** can be force on the **person** if it's transmitted to them – **wrenching a bag** from someone's hand (*R v Clouden* (1987)). Simply **snatching** something without any force on the person may be only theft (⚠ hechos concretos).
- Force can be used on **any person** – not only the owner (e.g. a security guard).
- **Fear**: putting or **seeking to put** someone in fear of force **then and there** – a future threat is **not** enough (that may be blackmail). The victim doesn't actually have to be afraid if the defendant **sought** to put them in fear.

:::es
**Fuerza**: basta **poca** (*Dawson and James*: empujones); arrancar un **bolso** de la mano puede serlo (*Clouden*). Sobre **cualquier persona** (p. ej. un vigilante). **Miedo**: a fuerza **en ese momento** (una amenaza futura no vale → quizá chantaje). Basta con **intentar** causar miedo.
:::

### 4. Timing and purpose

- Force must be **immediately before or at the time** of stealing. Appropriation can be a **continuing act**: in ***R v Hale* (1978)** force used to tie up the householder **after** the jewellery was taken but while the defendants were still in the house was "at the time of" stealing.
- Force must be used **in order to steal**. Force used **after** the theft is complete only to **escape**, or for another reason (e.g. revenge), is **not** robbery (it may be theft + assault).

:::es
**Momento**: **inmediatamente antes o durante** el hurto; la apropiación puede ser **continuada** (*Hale*: atar a la víctima después de coger las joyas, aún dentro de la casa, sigue siendo "durante"). **Finalidad**: la fuerza debe ser **para robar**; si se usa solo para **huir** cuando el hurto ya terminó, no es robo.
:::

### 5. Assault with intent to rob (s. 8(2))

An **assault** (or battery) **with intent to rob** – useful when the theft isn't completed. Also **indictable only**, maximum life. **Attempted robbery** is another option.

## Police scenarios

1. **The jostle.** Two youths push a man so he loses balance and take his wallet. → Minimal **force** to steal → **robbery** (*Dawson and James*).
2. **The bag wrench.** A woman holds her handbag tightly; the suspect yanks it out of her hand. → **Robbery** (*Clouden*).
3. **The debt.** LEE punches a man and takes £50 he honestly believes the man owes him. → **No theft** (s. 2(1)(a)) → **no robbery** (*Robinson*), but an assault offence.
4. **The escape.** A shoplifter leaves the shop with goods; outside, a guard grabs him and he pushes the guard away to escape. → Theft complete before force; force to **escape** → usually **not robbery** (consider when the appropriation ended ⚠).
5. **The threat by phone.** "Give me £100 tomorrow or I'll beat you up next week." → Not fear of force **then and there** → not robbery (consider **blackmail**).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **No theft = no robbery** (*Robinson*).
- **Minimal force** is enough; force on **any person**.
- Fear of force **then and there**, not in the future.
- Force must be **in order to** steal, **immediately before or at the time** (continuing appropriation – *Hale*).
- Robbery is **indictable only** (life).

## Summary

1. Robbery = theft + force/fear + immediately before or at the time + in order to steal (s. 8).
2. Must be a completed theft (*Robinson*).
3. Little force is enough (*Dawson and James*); bag wrenching (*Clouden*); any person.
4. Appropriation can continue (*Hale*); force only to escape after theft is not robbery.
5. s. 8(2) assault with intent to rob; indictable only; life.
