## Objectives

- Know the elements of **aggravated burglary** (Theft Act 1968, **s. 10**): a **burglary** + **"at the time"** + **has with him** a **WIFE** article.
- Understand the definitions: **firearm/imitation firearm, weapon of offence, explosive**.
- Apply the **timing** rule for s. 9(1)(a) and s. 9(1)(b) burglaries (*R v O'Leary*).

## Explanation

### 1. The offence (s. 10(1))

> A person is guilty of aggravated burglary if he **commits any burglary** and **at the time** has **with him** any **firearm or imitation firearm**, any **weapon of offence**, or any **explosive**.

Memory aid: **WIFE** – **W**eapon of offence, **I**mitation firearm, **F**irearm, **E**xplosive.

**Indictable only**; maximum **life imprisonment**.

:::es
**Burglary agravado (s. 10)**: cometer un **burglary** y **en ese momento** llevar consigo un **arma de fuego o imitación**, un **arma ofensiva** o un **explosivo** (**WIFE**). Solo Crown Court; **cadena perpetua**.
:::

### 2. The articles

- **Firearm** – includes an airgun or air pistol (s. 10(1)(a)); **imitation firearm** – anything that has the **appearance** of a firearm, whether or not capable of being discharged.
- **Weapon of offence** – any article **made or adapted** for use for **causing injury to or incapacitating** a person, **or intended** by the person having it with him for such use (s. 10(1)(b)). So **incapacitating** items (e.g. **rope or handcuffs to tie someone up**, sprays) can be weapons of offence – wider than the "offensive weapon" definition elsewhere.
- **Explosive** – any article manufactured to produce a practical effect by explosion, or intended for that purpose.

:::es
- **Arma de fuego** (incluye de aire comprimido) o **imitación** (lo que **parece** un arma).
- **Arma ofensiva**: fabricada o **adaptada** para **herir o inmovilizar**, o que el autor **pretende** usar así → incluye **cuerdas o esposas para atar** a alguien.
- **Explosivo**: fabricado para producir un efecto explosivo, o destinado a ello.
:::

### 3. "At the time" – the key timing rule

| Type of burglary | The article must be with him… |
|---|---|
| **s. 9(1)(a)** (entry with intent) | at the time of **entry** |
| **s. 9(1)(b)** (steals/inflicts GBH after entry) | at the time of the **stealing / GBH** (or attempt) |

In ***R v O'Leary* (1986)** the defendant entered unarmed, **picked up a knife inside**, and used it while stealing → **aggravated burglary** (s. 9(1)(b): he had the knife at the time he stole).

:::es
**Momento**: en 9(1)(a), al **entrar**; en 9(1)(b), al **hurtar o causar GBH**. En *O'Leary* cogió un cuchillo **dentro** de la casa y lo usó al robar → **agravado**.
:::

### 4. "Has with him"

- Means **knowingly** having the article **readily available** – not necessarily in hand (⚠ VERIFICAR jurisprudencia sobre conocimiento y disponibilidad).
- If **one of a group** has a weapon, the others only commit aggravated burglary if they **knew** about it (secondary liability).
- There is **no need** to **use** or **intend to use** a firearm, imitation firearm or explosive – just **have it with him**. For items **not** made/adapted as weapons, the **intention** to use them to injure/incapacitate is what makes them weapons of offence.

:::es
**"Llevar consigo"**: tenerlo **a sabiendas** y **disponible**. Si en un grupo solo uno lleva arma, los demás solo responden si **lo sabían**. No hace falta **usar** el arma de fuego, la imitación o el explosivo; para objetos corrientes, lo que los convierte en arma ofensiva es la **intención** de usarlos para herir o inmovilizar.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The screwdriver.** A burglar carries a screwdriver only to force windows. → Not a weapon of offence **unless** he **intended** to use it to injure/incapacitate (then it is).
2. **Cable ties.** Burglars bring cable ties to tie up occupants. → **Weapon of offence** (incapacitating) → aggravated burglary.
3. **The kitchen knife.** A man enters unarmed to steal (s. 9(1)(a)), takes a kitchen knife from the drawer and threatens the owner while taking cash. → s. 9(1)(b) theft committed while having the knife → **aggravated** (*O'Leary*).
4. **Toy gun.** A burglar carries a realistic plastic pistol. → **Imitation firearm** → aggravated.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Timing**: (a) = at **entry**; (b) = at **stealing/GBH**.
- **Weapon of offence** includes items for **incapacitating** (ropes, ties, sprays).
- A tool for **breaking in** is not a weapon unless **intended** for injury/incapacitation.
- **Imitation firearm** = appearance is enough.
- Aggravated burglary needs a **completed burglary** first.

## Summary

1. s. 10: burglary + at the time + has with him a WIFE article.
2. Weapon of offence: made/adapted/intended to injure **or incapacitate**.
3. Firearm includes airguns; imitation = appearance.
4. Timing: entry for (a); stealing/GBH for (b) (*O'Leary*).
5. Indictable only; life.
