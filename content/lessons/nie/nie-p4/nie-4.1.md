## Objectives

- Understand the structure of the **Sexual Offences Act 2003** and the investigative approach to sexual offences.
- Know the **lifetime anonymity** of complainants (Sexual Offences (Amendment) Act 1992) and its exceptions.
- Know the protections for complainants at trial: **special measures**, restrictions on **cross-examination by the accused** and on **sexual history evidence** (YJCEA 1999, s. 41).

## Explanation

### 1. The framework

- The **SOA 2003** replaced most earlier law (e.g. SOA 1956). Core offences: **rape** (4.2), **assault by penetration**, **sexual assault**, **causing sexual activity** (4.3), offences against **children** (4.4) and people with a **mental disorder** (4.5), **preparatory** offences (4.6), **prostitution/exploitation** (4.7) and **other** offences (4.8).
- **Gender-neutral** (except rape, which requires a penis).
- Offences committed **abroad** by UK nationals/residents against children can be prosecuted here (s. 72 ⚠).

:::es
La **SOA 2003** sustituyó casi toda la ley anterior. Delitos **neutros en cuanto al género** (salvo la violación). Ciertos delitos contra menores cometidos **en el extranjero** por británicos/residentes se pueden juzgar aquí (s. 72 ⚠).
:::

### 2. Investigating sexual offences – first steps

- **Victim first**: safety, medical needs, **believe and record** the account; avoid victim-blaming.
- **Forensic opportunities**: **early evidence kit** (mouth swab / urine – do not eat, drink, wash or go to the toilet if possible); **SARC** (Sexual Assault Referral Centre) for forensic medical exam; **forensic window** (time limits ⚠).
- **Initial account**: brief first account; a full account later by **ABE/VRI** (video-recorded interview) – avoid a detailed interview by first responders.
- **Support**: **ISVA** (Independent Sexual Violence Adviser).
- **Scene and suspect**: preserve scenes; seize clothing/bedding; suspect's clothing, intimate/non-intimate samples (PACE ss. 62–63).

:::es
**Primeros pasos**: seguridad y atención médica; **creer y registrar**; sin culpar. **Kit de pruebas tempranas** (frotis bucal/orina; no comer, beber, lavarse ni ir al baño si es posible); **SARC** para el examen forense; **ventana forense** (⚠). Un **relato inicial breve**; el completo en **ABE/VRI**. Apoyo de **ISVA**. Preservar escenas y muestras del sospechoso.
:::

### 3. Lifetime anonymity (Sexual Offences (Amendment) Act 1992)

- Once an **allegation** of a relevant sexual offence has been made, **no matter likely to lead members of the public to identify** the complainant may be **included in any publication** during that person's **lifetime** (s. 1).
- Applies **from the moment the allegation is made** – even if no one is charged, or the accused is acquitted.
- Covers most SOA 2003 offences, plus **attempts, conspiracy, incitement** and others (e.g. **FGM**, **trafficking** ⚠).
- **Exceptions** (s. 3 and s. 5): the complainant, aged **16 or over**, gives **written consent** freely; or a **court direction** (e.g. to induce witnesses to come forward, or where the restriction substantially and unreasonably restricts reporting) ⚠.
- Breach: **summary offence** by the publisher/editor (s. 5); defences (e.g. didn't know an allegation had been made ⚠).
- The **suspect** has **no** statutory anonymity (but police policy not to name before charge ⚠).

:::es
**Anonimato de por vida (SO(A)A 1992)**: desde que se hace la **denuncia**, nada que pueda **identificar** a la víctima puede **publicarse** mientras viva, aunque no se acuse a nadie o haya absolución. Incluye tentativa, conspiración, MGF, trata (⚠). **Excepciones**: **consentimiento escrito** de la víctima **mayor de 16** dado libremente, o **orden judicial**. Infracción: delito **sumario** del editor. El **sospechoso no** tiene anonimato legal.
:::

### 4. Protection at trial

- **Special measures** (YJCEA 1999): complainants of sexual offences are **automatically eligible** as intimidated witnesses unless they decline (s. 17(4)); screens, live link, evidence in private, **video-recorded evidence-in-chief** and **pre-recorded cross-examination (s. 28)**.
- **No cross-examination in person** by the accused of a sexual offence complainant (s. 34).
- **Sexual history** (s. 41): no evidence/questions about the complainant's **sexual behaviour** without the court's **leave**, given only in limited circumstances.

:::es
**En el juicio**: **medidas especiales** automáticas para víctimas sexuales (pantallas, videoconferencia, declaración grabada, **contrainterrogatorio pregrabado s. 28**). El acusado **no puede interrogarlas personalmente** (s. 34). **Historial sexual** de la víctima solo con **autorización del juez** (s. 41).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The local news.** A newspaper reports "a 34-year-old nurse living in X Street" was raped. → Likely to **identify**: breach of the 1992 Act.
2. **Victim wants to speak.** A 25-year-old complainant wants to tell her story to a magazine. → Lawful with her **written consent**, freely given.
3. **No charge.** The suspect is never charged. → Anonymity **still applies** for life.
4. **Two hours after.** Victim calls after an oral rape. → **Early evidence kit**: ask not to eat/drink; mouth swab.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Anonymity starts when the **allegation is made**, not at charge.
- Consent to waive must be **written** and the complainant **16+**.
- The 1992 Act protects the **complainant**, not the suspect.
- Sexual history evidence needs the **court's leave** (s. 41).

## Summary

1. SOA 2003: gender-neutral (except rape), structured by victim and conduct.
2. First steps: safety, early evidence kit, SARC, ISVA, brief initial account then ABE.
3. Lifetime anonymity from allegation; exceptions: written consent 16+, court direction.
4. Special measures, no cross-examination in person, s. 41 sexual history restriction.
