## Objectives

- Know the elements of **rape** (Sexual Offences Act 2003, s. 1) and of **rape of a child under 13** (s. 5).
- Understand **consent** (s. 74), the **evidential presumptions** (s. 75) and the **conclusive presumptions** (s. 76).
- Apply the test of **reasonable belief** in consent and the interpretation rules in s. 79.

## Explanation

### 1. The offence (s. 1 SOA 2003)

A person (A) commits rape if:
- he **intentionally penetrates** the **vagina, anus or mouth** of another person (B) **with his penis**;
- B **does not consent** to the penetration; and
- A **does not reasonably believe** that B consents.

Only a person with a **penis** can commit rape as the principal. A woman can be guilty as an **accessory** (aiding, abetting, counselling or procuring). Indictable only; maximum **life imprisonment**.

:::es
**Violación (s. 1)**: A **penetra intencionadamente** la **vagina, el ano o la boca** de B **con su pene**; B **no consiente**; y A **no cree razonablemente** que B consienta. Solo quien tiene **pene** puede ser autor material; una mujer puede ser **cómplice**. Solo por *indictment*; pena máxima **cadena perpetua**.
:::

### 2. Interpretation (s. 79)

- **Penetration is a continuing act** from entry to withdrawal (s. 79(2)) – if consent is withdrawn and A doesn't withdraw, it becomes rape.
- References to a part of the body include **surgically constructed** parts (s. 79(3)).
- **Vagina** includes the **vulva** (s. 79(9)).
- Any penetration, however slight, is enough.

:::es
- La **penetración es un acto continuo** hasta la retirada: si B retira el consentimiento y A no se retira → violación.
- Incluye partes del cuerpo **reconstruidas quirúrgicamente**.
- **Vagina** incluye la **vulva**. Basta cualquier penetración, por leve que sea.
:::

### 3. Consent (s. 74)

A person **consents** if they **agree by choice**, and have the **freedom and capacity** to make that choice.

- **Capacity**: e.g. heavy intoxication can remove capacity (*R v Bree* 2007 – a drunken consent is still consent if capacity remains ⚠).
- **Freedom**: conditions can matter – deception about a condition can vitiate consent in some cases (*Assange*, *R (F) v DPP* – withdrawal method; *R v McNally* – deception about gender ⚠ VERIFICAR alcance).
- Submission is not the same as consent.

:::es
**Consentimiento (s. 74)**: **acordar por elección** teniendo **libertad y capacidad** para elegir. La embriaguez puede eliminar la capacidad (si sigue habiendo capacidad, el consentimiento ebrio es válido – *Bree* ⚠). Ciertos engaños sobre condiciones pueden anular el consentimiento (*Assange*, *F v DPP*, *McNally* ⚠). **Someterse no es consentir**.
:::

### 4. Evidential presumptions (s. 75)

If A did the act, **one of these circumstances existed** and **A knew** it existed, B is **presumed not to have consented** and A **not to have reasonably believed** in consent – **unless sufficient evidence is adduced to raise an issue**:

1. **Violence** used against B **or fear** of immediate violence against B, at the time or immediately before;
2. **Fear** that immediate violence was being used / would be used **against another person**;
3. B was **unlawfully detained** (and A was not);
4. B was **asleep or otherwise unconscious**;
5. B could not communicate consent because of **physical disability**;
6. a **substance** capable of **stupefying or overpowering** B was **administered or caused to be taken** without B's consent (by anyone).

:::es
**Presunciones probatorias (s. 75)** – se presume que no hubo consentimiento ni creencia razonable, **salvo prueba suficiente en contrario**, si A sabía que: había **violencia o miedo** a violencia inmediata (contra B u **otra persona**); B estaba **detenida ilegalmente**; **dormida o inconsciente**; no podía comunicarse por **discapacidad física**; o se le **administró una sustancia** capaz de aturdirla sin su consentimiento.
:::

### 5. Conclusive presumptions (s. 76)

If A **intentionally deceived** B as to the **nature or purpose** of the act, **or** induced B to consent by **impersonating a person known personally** to B, it is **conclusively presumed** that B did not consent and A did not believe B consented. **No rebuttal** is possible.

- *R v Williams* (1923) – singing teacher told a pupil intercourse would improve her breathing → deception as to **nature/purpose**.
- *R v Jheeta* (2007) – deceptions about other matters (not nature/purpose) fall under **s. 74**, not s. 76.

:::es
**Presunciones concluyentes (s. 76)** – **no admiten prueba en contrario**: engaño intencionado sobre la **naturaleza o finalidad** del acto, o **suplantar a una persona conocida personalmente** por B. Otros engaños se analizan con la **s. 74** (*Jheeta*).
:::

### 6. Reasonable belief

Whether a belief is **reasonable** is determined having regard to **all the circumstances**, including **any steps A has taken to ascertain** whether B consents (s. 1(2)). Voluntary intoxication of A is **not** a basis for a reasonable belief.

### 7. Rape of a child under 13 (s. 5)

A **intentionally penetrates** the vagina, anus or mouth of a child **under 13** with his penis. **Consent is irrelevant**; liability as to age is **strict** (*R v G* 2008). Maximum **life**.

:::es
**Creencia razonable**: según **todas las circunstancias**, incluidas las **medidas que tomó A** para comprobar el consentimiento; la embriaguez voluntaria de A no sirve. **Violación de menor de 13 (s. 5)**: el **consentimiento es irrelevante** y la edad es de **responsabilidad objetiva** (*R v G*).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The party.** B was asleep on a sofa when A penetrated her. A says she had flirted earlier. → **s. 75** (asleep) applies: presumption of no consent; A must raise sufficient evidence to the contrary.
2. **The "medical" treatment.** A fake therapist tells B penetration is a medical procedure. → **s. 76** – deception as to **nature/purpose**: conclusive.
3. **The twin.** A pretends to be B's partner (his twin brother) in a dark room. → **s. 76** – impersonating a person **known personally** to B.
4. **Changed mind.** B agrees, then during intercourse says "stop"; A carries on. → Penetration is a **continuing act**: rape from the moment consent is withdrawn.
5. **Age 12.** A says the girl told him she was 16 and agreed. → **s. 5**: consent and belief about age are **irrelevant**.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Mouth** is included in rape (s. 1) – but **not** in assault by penetration (s. 2).
- s. 75 presumptions are **rebuttable**; s. 76 are **conclusive**.
- s. 75 requires **A to know** the circumstance existed.
- The substance in s. 75 may be administered **by anyone**, not only A.
- A woman can't be the principal in rape but **can** be an accessory.

## Summary

1. s. 1: penile penetration of vagina/anus/mouth, no consent, no reasonable belief – life.
2. s. 74: agreement by choice with freedom and capacity.
3. s. 75: six evidential presumptions (rebuttable); s. 76: two conclusive presumptions.
4. Penetration is continuing; vagina includes vulva; surgically constructed parts count.
5. s. 5: child under 13 – consent irrelevant, strict liability on age.
