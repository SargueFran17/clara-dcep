## Objectives

- Know the three groups of offences protecting people with a **mental disorder** (SOA 2003, ss. 30–44).
- Understand **"unable to refuse"** and **lack of capacity to choose** (s. 30(2); *R v C* 2009).
- Know the offences by **care workers** (ss. 38–41).

## Explanation

### 1. Mental disorder

**Mental disorder** = **any disorder or disability of the mind** (Mental Health Act 1983, s. 1). It includes learning disabilities, dementia, serious mental illness…

### 2. Group 1 – mental disorder impeding choice (ss. 30–33)

A intentionally engages in sexual activity with B (s. 30), causes/incites (s. 31), in B's presence (s. 32), or causes B to watch (s. 33), where:
- B is **unable to refuse** because of or for a reason related to a **mental disorder**; **and**
- A **knows or could reasonably be expected to know** that B has a mental disorder and is likely to be unable to refuse.

B is **unable to refuse** if (s. 30(2)):
- (a) B **lacks the capacity to choose** whether to agree (whether because B lacks sufficient **understanding of the nature or reasonably foreseeable consequences** of what is being done, **or for any other reason**); **or**
- (b) B is **unable to communicate** such a choice to A.

*R v C* (2009, HL): capacity is **specific to the person, situation and time**; an **irrational fear** arising from the mental disorder that prevents B from exercising choice can mean B **lacks capacity**.

Max: **life** if penetration involved; otherwise **14 years** (⚠).

:::es
**Trastorno mental** = **cualquier trastorno o discapacidad mental** (MHA 1983). **Grupo 1 (ss. 30–33)**: B **no puede negarse** por su trastorno – **carece de capacidad para elegir** (no entiende la naturaleza o consecuencias, **u otro motivo**) o **no puede comunicarlo** – y A **sabe o debería saber**. *R v C*: la capacidad depende de **persona, situación y momento**; un **miedo irracional** derivado del trastorno puede anularla. Perpetua si hay penetración; si no 14 años (⚠).
:::

### 3. Group 2 – inducements, threats or deception (ss. 34–37)

B **has capacity** but agrees because of an **inducement, threat or deception** offered/made by A **for that purpose**, and A knows or could reasonably be expected to know of B's mental disorder. Example: "If you have sex with me, I'll take you to the cinema."

### 4. Group 3 – care workers (ss. 38–41)

A is involved in B's **care** (e.g. in a **care home**, community care, NHS services) and in the course of that is in **regular face-to-face contact**; B has a mental disorder; A knows or could reasonably be expected to know. **Consent and capacity are irrelevant** – the relationship is the issue. Exceptions: A and B are **married/civil partners** (and B 16+), or had a sexual relationship **before** the care relationship began (ss. 43–44).

:::es
**Grupo 2 (ss. 34–37)**: B **sí tiene capacidad** pero acepta por **incentivo, amenaza o engaño** de A (p. ej. "te llevo al cine si…"). **Grupo 3 (ss. 38–41) – cuidadores**: A cuida a B con **contacto regular cara a cara**; el **consentimiento y la capacidad son irrelevantes**. Excepciones: **matrimonio** o relación sexual **previa** al cuidado.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The day centre.** A man has sex with a woman with severe learning disabilities who can't understand what sex is or its consequences. → **s. 30** (lacks capacity – penetration: life ⚠).
2. **The fear.** B, with schizophrenia, submits because of an irrational fear of harm if she refuses. → Possible **lack of capacity** (*R v C*).
3. **The bribe.** A tells a man with a learning disability he'll buy him trainers if he performs a sex act. → **s. 34** (inducement).
4. **The care assistant.** A care-home worker has a "consensual" relationship with a resident with dementia. → **s. 38** – consent irrelevant.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Group 1: B **lacks capacity** or **can't communicate**; Group 2: B **has capacity** but is induced/threatened/deceived.
- "**For any other reason**" in s. 30(2)(a) – capacity isn't just about understanding.
- A must **know or could reasonably be expected to know** of the disorder.
- Care workers: **consent irrelevant**; exception for prior relationships/marriage.

## Summary

1. Mental disorder = any disorder or disability of the mind.
2. ss. 30–33: unable to refuse (lacks capacity or can't communicate); A knows/ought to know.
3. *R v C*: capacity is situation-specific; irrational fear can remove it.
4. ss. 34–37: inducement, threat, deception.
5. ss. 38–41: care workers – consent irrelevant; ss. 43–44 exceptions.
