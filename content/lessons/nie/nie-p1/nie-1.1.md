## Objectives

- Know the **nine protected characteristics** and the forms of **prohibited conduct** under the **Equality Act 2010**.
- Know the **Public Sector Equality Duty** (s. 149) and the difference between **positive action** and **positive discrimination**.
- Understand **hate crime/hate incidents**, the link with **legitimacy** and **trust**, and the **Code of Ethics**.

## Explanation

### 1. Protected characteristics (Equality Act 2010)

**Age · Disability · Gender reassignment · Marriage and civil partnership · Pregnancy and maternity · Race · Religion or belief · Sex · Sexual orientation.**

- **Race** includes **colour, nationality, ethnic or national origins**.
- **Religion or belief** includes **lack** of religion or belief.
- **Disability**: a physical or mental impairment with a **substantial and long-term** (12 months+) adverse effect on the ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities (cancer, HIV and MS count from diagnosis).
- **Marriage/civil partnership** is protected only in **employment** (direct/indirect discrimination).

:::es
**9 características protegidas (Equality Act 2010)**: **edad, discapacidad, reasignación de género, matrimonio/pareja civil, embarazo/maternidad, raza, religión o creencias, sexo y orientación sexual**. Raza incluye **color, nacionalidad y origen étnico o nacional**; religión incluye **no tener** religión; discapacidad = deficiencia con efecto **sustancial y duradero** (12 meses+).
:::

### 2. Prohibited conduct

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Direct discrimination** (s. 13) | treating someone **less favourably because of** a protected characteristic (incl. by **association** or **perception**). Cannot be justified (except **age**). |
| **Indirect discrimination** (s. 19) | applying a **provision, criterion or practice (PCP)** to everyone that puts people sharing a characteristic at a **particular disadvantage** – unlawful **unless** it's a **proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim** |
| **Discrimination arising from disability** (s. 15) | unfavourable treatment because of something arising from disability, not justified |
| **Failure to make reasonable adjustments** (ss. 20–21) | for disabled people |
| **Harassment** (s. 26) | **unwanted conduct** related to a protected characteristic (or of a sexual nature) with the **purpose or effect** of violating dignity or creating an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment |
| **Victimisation** (s. 27) | subjecting a person to a detriment because they **made a complaint**, gave evidence, or did any other "protected act" under the Act |

### 3. Public Sector Equality Duty (s. 149)

Public authorities (incl. police) must, in exercising their functions, have **due regard** to the need to:
1. **eliminate** discrimination, harassment, victimisation;
2. **advance equality of opportunity**;
3. **foster good relations** between people who share a characteristic and those who don't.

### 4. Positive action vs positive discrimination

- **Positive action** (ss. 158–159) is **lawful**: e.g. targeted outreach, mentoring for under-represented groups; in recruitment, a **"tie-break"** in favour of an under-represented group between candidates **as qualified as each other**.
- **Positive discrimination** (e.g. appointing someone **less qualified** because of a characteristic, quotas) is **unlawful**.
- **Occupational requirement**: a characteristic can be required where it's a proportionate means of a legitimate aim (e.g. a female officer for an intimate search).

:::es
**Discriminación directa** (trato menos favorable, también por **asociación o percepción**); **indirecta** (norma o práctica general que perjudica a un grupo; legal solo si es **medio proporcionado para un fin legítimo**); **acoso** (conducta no deseada que atenta contra la dignidad); **represalias** (por haber denunciado). **Deber de igualdad del sector público (s. 149)**: tener **en cuenta** eliminar la discriminación, **promover la igualdad de oportunidades** y **fomentar buenas relaciones**. **Acción positiva** legal (desempate entre candidatos **igual de cualificados**); **discriminación positiva** (cuotas, elegir al menos cualificado) **ilegal**.
:::

### 5. Hate crime and hate incidents

- **Hate crime**: **any criminal offence** which is **perceived, by the victim or any other person**, to be motivated by **hostility or prejudice** based on a personal characteristic – nationally monitored strands: **race, religion/faith, sexual orientation, disability, transgender identity**.
- **Non-crime hate incident**: the same perception, but **not a crime** (recording rules changed in 2023 – must meet a threshold and respect freedom of expression ⚠).
- Recording is based on **perception**; evidence for **aggravation** (CDA 1998) or **sentence uplift** (Sentencing Act 2020, s. 66) must still be proved (see 2.6).

### 6. Legitimacy, trust and ethics

- **Policing by consent** (Peel) depends on **legitimacy** and **procedural justice**: treat people **fairly**, give them a **voice**, be **neutral** and **transparent**.
- **Macpherson Report (1999)** (Stephen Lawrence inquiry): **institutional racism** = "the collective failure of an organisation to provide an appropriate and professional service to people because of their colour, culture, or ethnic origin".
- **Disproportionality** (e.g. stop and search) must be monitored and explained; **unconscious bias** awareness.
- **Code of Ethics** (College of Policing): principles and **Standards of Professional Behaviour**, including **Equality and Diversity** – act with fairness and impartiality, don't discriminate unlawfully or unfairly; **challenge and report** improper conduct. **Human Rights Act 1998**, Art. 14 (non-discrimination in enjoyment of Convention rights).

:::es
**Delito de odio**: **cualquier delito** que la víctima **o cualquier otra persona perciba** motivado por **hostilidad o prejuicio** por **raza, religión, orientación sexual, discapacidad o identidad trans**. **Incidente de odio no delictivo**: misma percepción sin delito (nuevas normas 2023 ⚠). **Legitimidad**: justicia procedimental. **Informe Macpherson (1999)**: **racismo institucional**. **Código Ético** y Estándares de Conducta (igualdad y diversidad; **denunciar** conductas indebidas). **HRA 1998 art. 14**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The height rule.** A force requires all applicants to be 1.75 m tall. → **Indirect discrimination** (disadvantages women and some ethnic groups) unless objectively justified.
2. **The interview.** Two equally qualified candidates; the force picks the one from an under-represented ethnic group. → Lawful **positive action** (tie-break).
3. **The joke.** A sergeant makes repeated jokes about a colleague's sexuality. → **Harassment**; challenge and report (**Code of Ethics**).
4. **The complaint.** An officer who supported a colleague's discrimination claim is moved to night shifts as punishment. → **Victimisation**.
5. **The bus.** A woman is shoved and the witness thinks it was because she wears a hijab. → Record as a **hate crime** (perception of any person); investigate evidence of hostility.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Nine** characteristics; marriage/civil partnership only in **employment**.
- **Direct** discrimination can't be justified (except age); **indirect** can be (**proportionate means of a legitimate aim**).
- Positive **action** lawful; positive **discrimination** unlawful.
- Hate crime is recorded on **perception** by the victim **or any other person**.
- PSED = **due regard** (not a duty to achieve a result).

## Summary

1. Equality Act 2010: 9 protected characteristics.
2. Direct, indirect, harassment, victimisation, disability-related, reasonable adjustments.
3. PSED s. 149: eliminate, advance, foster.
4. Positive action vs positive discrimination; occupational requirement.
5. Hate crime (perception; 5 strands); Macpherson; Code of Ethics; legitimacy.
