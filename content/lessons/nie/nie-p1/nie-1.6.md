## Objectives

- Know the powers of **entry without warrant**: **s. 17** (to arrest, save life…), **s. 18** (after arrest) and **s. 32** (on arrest).
- Know **search warrants** (s. 8 and safeguards in ss. 15–16), **excluded** and **special procedure** material and **legal privilege**.
- Know the **seizure** powers (s. 19, s. 50 CJPA 2001) and **Code B** rules (consent searches, conduct, records).

## Explanation

### 1. Entry to arrest or save life (s. 17)

A constable may **enter and search any premises** for the purpose of:
- executing a **warrant of arrest**;
- **arresting a person for an indictable offence**;
- arresting for certain **specified offences** (e.g. some public order offences);
- **recapturing** a person **unlawfully at large** whom he is pursuing;
- **saving life or limb** or **preventing serious damage to property**.

Except for saving life/limb, the officer must have **reasonable grounds for believing** the person sought is **on the premises**. The search is limited to what's reasonably required for the purpose. (Common-law power to enter to **deal with or prevent a breach of the peace** is preserved.)

:::es
**s. 17 – entrar para**: ejecutar una orden de detención, **detener por delito *indictable***, detener por ciertos delitos, **recapturar** a un fugado al que persigues, **salvar la vida o integridad** o evitar **daños graves**. Salvo para salvar vidas, hace falta **creer razonablemente** que la persona está dentro. Se mantiene la potestad de *common law* para evitar un **quebrantamiento de la paz**.
:::

### 2. Search after arrest (s. 18)

After arresting a person for an **indictable offence**, a constable may enter and search **premises occupied or controlled** by that person if he has **reasonable grounds for suspecting** there is **evidence** (other than items subject to legal privilege) relating to **that offence** or to **some other indictable offence connected with or similar to** it.

- Needs **written authorisation** from an **inspector** (or above) **before** the search, **unless** the person's presence at the premises is **necessary for the effective investigation** before he's taken to a police station (s. 18(5)) – then inform an inspector **as soon as practicable afterwards**.
- The inspector records the grounds and the nature of the evidence sought.

### 3. Search on arrest (s. 32)

When a person is arrested **elsewhere than at a police station**:
- **Search the person** if there are reasonable grounds for believing he may present a **danger** to himself or others, or has anything he might use to **escape**, or **evidence** relating to an offence. Only outer coat, jacket and gloves in public (plus mouth).
- **Enter and search premises** **in which he was when arrested or immediately before** he was arrested, for **evidence relating to the offence** for which he was arrested (**indictable** offence), if there are reasonable grounds for believing such evidence is there.
- The search must be **at the time** of arrest – not hours later (*R v Badham* 1987).

:::es
**s. 18**: tras detener por delito *indictable*, registrar locales **ocupados o controlados** por el detenido buscando pruebas de **ese delito** o de otro **relacionado o similar**; autorización **escrita de un inspector** antes (o después, si su presencia era necesaria – s. 18(5)). **s. 32**: al detener fuera de comisaría, registrar a la **persona** (peligro, fuga, pruebas) y los **locales donde estaba al detenerla o justo antes**, buscando pruebas **de ese delito**; tiene que ser **en el momento** (*Badham*).
:::

### 4. Search warrants (s. 8) and safeguards (ss. 15–16)

A **justice of the peace** may issue a warrant if there are **reasonable grounds for believing**:
- an **indictable offence** has been committed;
- there is material on the premises **likely to be of substantial value** to the investigation;
- it is likely to be **relevant evidence**;
- it does **not** consist of or include **items subject to legal privilege**, **excluded material** or **special procedure material**; and
- one of the **access conditions**: not practicable to communicate with anyone entitled to grant entry/access; entry won't be granted without a warrant; or the purpose of the search may be frustrated/seriously prejudiced unless an officer can secure immediate entry.

Warrants can be **specific premises** or **all premises** occupied/controlled by a person, and authorise **multiple entries** (if specified). **Safeguards**:
- Application in writing, on oath, with grounds; one warrant = one application.
- **Execute within 3 months** of issue (s. 16(3)); within **a reasonable hour** unless that would frustrate the search.
- Identify yourself, **show the warrant** and give a **copy** to the occupier; endorse the warrant afterwards; return it.
- Search only to the extent required for the purpose.

### 5. Protected material

- **Legal privilege** (s. 10): communications between a lawyer and client for legal advice or for legal proceedings – **cannot be seized** (unless held to further a criminal purpose).
- **Excluded material** (s. 11): **personal records** held in confidence (medical, welfare), **human tissue or tissue fluid** for diagnosis/treatment, **journalistic material** held in confidence.
- **Special procedure material** (s. 14): e.g. other confidential business/professional material and non-confidential journalistic material.
- Access to excluded/special procedure material: **production order or warrant** from a **circuit judge** under **Schedule 1**.

:::es
**Orden s. 8 (magistrado)**: delito *indictable*, material de **valor sustancial**, prueba **relevante**, que **no** sea material **privilegiado, excluido o de procedimiento especial**, y una condición de acceso. **Plazo: 3 meses**; a **hora razonable**; **identificarse, enseñar la orden y dar copia**. **Privilegio legal** (abogado–cliente): no se puede incautar. **Material excluido** (historias médicas/personales, tejidos humanos, material periodístico confidencial) y **procedimiento especial** → **juez de circuito** (Anexo 1).
:::

### 6. Seizure (s. 19) and Code B

- **s. 19**: a constable **lawfully on any premises** may seize anything he has reasonable grounds for believing has been **obtained in consequence of an offence** or is **evidence** of an offence, **and** that it's **necessary** to seize it to prevent it being **concealed, lost, damaged, altered or destroyed**. Includes computer information in a form that can be taken away.
- **CJPA 2001, s. 50 "seize and sift"**: where it's not reasonably practicable to separate material on the premises, seize it all and examine elsewhere (with safeguards).
- **Retention** (s. 22): as long as necessary in all the circumstances.
- **Code B**:
  - **Consent searches**: consent must be **given in writing** on the Notice of Powers and Rights where practicable; the person must be told they're **not obliged** to consent and anything seized may be used in evidence; consent can be withdrawn.
  - **Reasonable force** only if necessary; minimise damage; leave premises **secure**.
  - Give a **Notice of Powers and Rights**; keep a **search register/record**.

:::es
**Incautación s. 19**: estando **legalmente** en el lugar, incautar lo **obtenido por un delito** o que sea **prueba**, si es **necesario** para que no se oculte, pierda o destruya. **s. 50 CJPA 2001**: llevarse todo y **cribarlo** fuera. **Code B**: registro **con consentimiento por escrito** (y avisar de que no está obligado), **fuerza razonable**, dejar el lugar **seguro**, **Notice of Powers and Rights** y registro escrito.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **Screams.** You hear screaming inside a flat and no one answers. → **s. 17 – save life or limb** (no need to believe a suspect is inside).
2. **Burglar's home.** Arrest for burglary in the street; you want to search his flat for stolen goods. → **s. 18** with an **inspector's written authority** (or s. 18(5) if his presence is needed first).
3. **The car he just left.** D arrested in the street; he had just got out of a car. → **s. 32** – premises (vehicle) where he was **immediately before** arrest, at the time of arrest.
4. **Two days later.** Officers go back to the arrest address two days later to search under s. 32. → **Unlawful** (*Badham*) – use s. 18 or a warrant.
5. **The doctor's notes.** Victim's medical records needed. → **Excluded material** – Schedule 1 (circuit judge), or the victim's consent.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- s. 18: premises **occupied or controlled** by the arrested person; s. 32: premises where he **was** at/immediately before arrest (even if not his).
- s. 18: **inspector, in writing**; s. 32: no authorisation, but **immediate**.
- Both s. 18 and s. 32 need an **indictable** offence.
- Warrant execution: within **3 months**.
- Legal privilege can **never** be the subject of a s. 8 warrant.

## Summary

1. s. 17 entry to arrest (indictable offence), recapture, save life/limb, prevent serious damage.
2. s. 18 search after arrest (occupied/controlled; inspector's written authority; s. 18(5)).
3. s. 32 search of person and premises on arrest – immediate.
4. s. 8 warrants (JP; conditions; 3 months; safeguards ss. 15–16); privileged, excluded, special procedure material (Sch. 1).
5. s. 19 seizure; s. 50 CJPA seize and sift; Code B consent searches and conduct.
