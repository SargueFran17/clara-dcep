## Objectives

- Know the **ways to start a prosecution**: charge, written charge and requisition, single justice procedure notice, summons/warrant.
- Know the **time limit for summary offences** (6 months) and when it doesn't apply.
- Understand **bail**: pre-charge bail, post-charge police bail (s. 38 PACE), conditions, the **Bail Act 1976** and the consequences of breach or failing to surrender.

## Explanation

### 1. Starting criminal proceedings

| Method | How | When used |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge** | the suspect is charged at the police station (after a charging decision by police or CPS) | suspect in custody / attending |
| **Written charge + requisition** | a public prosecutor (e.g. the police/CPS) issues a **written charge** and a **requisition** requiring the person to appear at court (Criminal Justice Act 2003, s. 29) | the common way to start proceedings when the suspect is **not** charged in custody |
| **Written charge + single justice procedure notice** | for certain **summary-only, non-imprisonable** offences (often motoring) dealt with on the papers by a single magistrate | minor cases |
| **Summons or warrant** from magistrates (Magistrates' Courts Act 1980, s. 1) | an **information** is laid before a magistrate | private prosecutions and some other prosecutors |

:::es
**Cómo empieza un proceso penal**: **acusación en comisaría** (charge); **written charge + requisition** (CJA 2003 s. 29: escrito de acusación y requerimiento de comparecencia, el método habitual si no se acusa en comisaría); **single justice procedure notice** (infracciones leves no castigadas con prisión, resueltas por un solo magistrado por escrito); **summons o warrant** del juzgado tras una *information* (MCA 1980 s. 1).
:::

### 2. Time limit for summary offences

A magistrates' court generally **cannot try a summary offence** unless the information was laid / the written charge issued **within 6 months** of the offence (Magistrates' Courts Act 1980, **s. 127**), unless a specific statute gives a different limit. **Either-way** and **indictable** offences have **no** general time limit.

:::es
**Delitos summary**: en general hay que iniciar el proceso **en 6 meses** desde el hecho (MCA 1980 s. 127), salvo que una ley diga otra cosa. Los **either-way** e **indictable** **no** tienen plazo general.
:::

### 3. Pre-charge bail (PACE Part IV, as amended)

- A person can be **released on bail** while the investigation continues (with or without conditions), or **released under investigation (RUI)** without bail.
- Since the 2022 reforms, bail should be used where **necessary and proportionate**, considering things like the **risk to victims and witnesses** (⚠ VERIFICAR la redacción).
- **Applicable bail periods** (⚠ VERIFICAR): **initial period 3 months** (authorised by an **inspector**), extendable to **6 months** by a **superintendent**, to **9 months** by an officer of **assistant chief constable/commander** rank, and beyond that by a **magistrates' court**.
- **Conditions** can be imposed (e.g. not to contact the victim, to live at an address).

:::es
**Fianza antes de acusar**: libertad **con bail** (con o sin condiciones) o **RUI**. Desde 2022 se usa cuando es **necesaria y proporcionada** (p. ej. riesgo para víctimas). **Plazos** (⚠ verifica): **3 meses** (inspector) → **6** (superintendente) → **9** (ACC/commander) → después, **juzgado**.
:::

### 4. Bail after charge – PACE s. 38

After charge, the **custody officer** must release the person (on bail or without), **unless** one of the s. 38 grounds applies, e.g.:

- the person's **name or address** can't be ascertained or is believed false;
- reasonable grounds for believing they will **fail to appear** in court;
- (imprisonable offence) to prevent them **committing an offence**;
- to prevent them causing **physical injury** or **loss/damage to property**;
- to prevent them **interfering with the administration of justice** or the investigation;
- for their **own protection**;
- (juveniles) in their **own interests**.

If refused bail, they must be brought before a **magistrates' court** as soon as practicable (normally the next sitting). **Juveniles** refused bail should normally be moved to **local authority accommodation** (s. 38(6)) unless impracticable or (for 12+) secure accommodation is needed and not available (⚠ VERIFICAR).

**Police bail conditions** after charge (s. 47(1A) PACE; Bail Act 1976, s. 3(6)): e.g. residence, curfew, reporting, no contact – to make sure the person **surrenders**, doesn't **offend**, doesn't **interfere with witnesses**, or for their **own protection**. Police **cannot** impose some conditions a court can (e.g. residence in a bail hostel, reports) (⚠ VERIFICAR lista).

:::es
**Tras acusar (s. 38 PACE)**: el custody officer debe **liberar** (con o sin bail) **salvo** que: no se conozca **nombre/dirección**; riesgo de **no comparecer**; de **cometer delitos**; de causar **lesiones o daños**; de **interferir** con la justicia; por su **propia protección**; o (menores) **por su interés**. Si se deniega, va al **juzgado** lo antes posible. **Menores**: normalmente a **alojamiento de la autoridad local** (s. 38(6)). **Condiciones** posibles: residencia, toque de queda, presentarse en comisaría, no contactar…
:::

### 5. The Bail Act 1976 – key points

- **Right to bail** (s. 4): a defendant **charged** with an offence generally has a **right to bail** at court, subject to the exceptions in **Schedule 1** (e.g. substantial grounds to believe they'd **fail to surrender**, **commit an offence on bail**, **interfere with witnesses**).
- **Failure to surrender** without reasonable cause (s. 6) is an **offence**.
- **Breach of conditions**: a person on **court bail** can be **arrested** if an officer has reasonable grounds to believe they are likely to break/have broken conditions (s. 7) – breach is not itself an offence, but they're taken before the court. For **police bail**, arrest powers are in **s. 46A PACE** (failure to answer bail / breach of conditions).

:::es
**Bail Act 1976**: **derecho a la libertad bajo fianza** (s. 4) salvo excepciones del **Anexo 1** (riesgo de fuga, de delinquir, de interferir con testigos). **No comparecer** sin causa razonable es **delito** (s. 6). **Incumplir condiciones**: en bail **judicial**, detención (s. 7) y comparecencia ante el juez (no es delito en sí); en bail **policial**, detención bajo **s. 46A PACE**.
:::

### 6. Out-of-court disposals (overview)

Instead of prosecution, some cases can be dealt with by **out-of-court disposals** (e.g. **community resolution**, **cautions**, **penalty notices**). The PCSC Act 2022 introduced a **two-tier framework** (**diversionary caution** and **community caution**) replacing older options – ⚠ VERIFICAR qué está en vigor en 2027 y cómo aparece en tu edición.

## Police scenarios

1. **The shoplifter.** Charged with theft; no address; previously failed to appear. → **s. 38** grounds: name/address and risk of failing to appear → refuse bail, court next sitting.
2. **The old summary offence.** A minor **summary** offence was committed **8 months** ago. → Out of time under **s. 127 MCA 1980** (unless a statute says otherwise).
3. **Domestic abuse investigation.** Suspect released pending forensic results. Victim at risk. → **Pre-charge bail with conditions** (no contact) is likely **necessary and proportionate**, rather than RUI.
4. **Missed court date.** A defendant on court bail doesn't turn up without reasonable cause. → **s. 6 Bail Act** offence; a warrant may be issued.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **6-month limit** only for **summary** offences.
- **s. 38** grounds are **after charge**; pre-charge bail has its own rules and **time periods**.
- **Breach of bail conditions** is **not** itself an offence; **failure to surrender** **is** (s. 6).
- Police vs court bail conditions – police can't impose all conditions courts can.
- **Juveniles** refused bail → **local authority accommodation** is the default.

## Summary

1. Start proceedings: charge; written charge + requisition (s. 29 CJA 2003); SJP notice; summons/warrant.
2. Summary offences: 6-month limit (s. 127 MCA 1980).
3. Pre-charge bail: necessary & proportionate; 3 → 6 → 9 months → court (⚠).
4. Post-charge: release unless s. 38 grounds; conditions possible; juveniles to LA accommodation.
5. Bail Act: right to bail (s. 4, Sch. 1 exceptions); failing to surrender = offence (s. 6); breach → arrest (s. 7 / s. 46A PACE).
