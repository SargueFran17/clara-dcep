## Objectives

- Know who is **competent** and **compellable** as a witness, including **spouses/civil partners** (PACE s. 80).
- Understand **oath/affirmation**, **unsworn evidence**, **refreshing memory** and **special measures**.
- Get the basics of **hearsay** (CJA 2003, ss. 114–116) and **bad character** (CJA 2003, s. 101 gateways).

## Explanation

### 1. Competence and compellability

- **Competent** = **can** lawfully give evidence. **Compellable** = **can be forced** to give evidence.
- **General rule** (Youth Justice and Criminal Evidence Act 1999, **s. 53**): **everyone is competent**, whatever their age, **unless** they can't **understand questions** put to them as a witness **and give answers that can be understood**.
- The **defendant** is **not competent for the prosecution** in their own trial (s. 53(4)) and is **not compellable** for the defence, but **may** give evidence in their own defence.
- **Sworn evidence**: a witness may give evidence **on oath/affirmation** only if aged **14 or over** and they understand the solemnity of the occasion and the responsibility to tell the truth (s. 55). Otherwise **unsworn** evidence (s. 56).

:::es
- **Competente** = puede declarar; **compelible** = se le puede **obligar**.
- **YJCEA 1999 s. 53**: **todos son competentes**, salvo que no entiendan las preguntas ni den respuestas comprensibles.
- El **acusado** no es competente para la **acusación** y no se le puede obligar a declarar en su defensa (pero puede hacerlo).
- **Juramento**: desde **14 años** y si entiende la solemnidad (s. 55); si no, declaración **sin juramento** (s. 56).
:::

### 2. Spouses and civil partners (PACE, s. 80)

- A spouse/civil partner of the accused is **competent** for the prosecution (unless jointly charged).
- They are **compellable for the prosecution only** in **specified offences** (s. 80(3)):
  - an offence involving an **assault on, or injury or threat of injury to**, the **spouse/civil partner** or a **person under 16**;
  - a **sexual offence** against a **person under 16**;
  - **attempting, conspiring, aiding** etc. any of these.
- They are **compellable for the defence** of their spouse (unless jointly charged).
- **Former** spouses (after divorce) are treated like any other witness.

:::es
**Cónyuge o pareja civil (PACE s. 80)**: competente para la acusación (salvo coacusado). **Compelible para la acusación solo** si el delito implica **agresión, lesión o amenaza de lesión** al **propio cónyuge** o a un **menor de 16**, o un **delito sexual contra un menor de 16** (y tentativas/participación). Compelible **para la defensa** del cónyuge. **Ex-cónyuges**: testigos normales.
:::

### 3. Refreshing memory (CJA 2003, s. 139)

A witness can **refresh their memory** from a **document** (e.g. a **pocket notebook** or statement) if they say it records their recollection **at the time it was made** and that recollection was likely to be **significantly better** then than now. (⚠ VERIFICAR la redacción.) This is how officers use notebooks in court (CO3).

### 4. Special measures (YJCEA 1999)

- **Vulnerable** witnesses (s. 16): **under 18**, or whose evidence may be diminished by **mental disorder**, **significant impairment of intelligence and social functioning**, or **physical disability/disorder**.
- **Intimidated** witnesses (s. 17): **fear or distress** about testifying; **complainants in sexual offences** and witnesses in certain **weapons** offences are **automatically eligible** unless they opt out.
- Measures: **screens**, **live link**, **evidence in private**, **removal of wigs and gowns**, **video-recorded evidence-in-chief** (ABE), **pre-recorded cross-examination** (s. 28), **intermediaries**, **communication aids**.

:::es
**Medidas especiales**: testigos **vulnerables** (s. 16: **menores de 18**, trastorno mental, discapacidad) e **intimidados** (s. 17: miedo o angustia; **víctimas de delitos sexuales**, automáticamente). Medidas: **mamparas**, **videoconferencia**, **sesión privada**, sin pelucas/togas, **declaración grabada (ABE)**, **contrainterrogatorio pregrabado (s. 28)**, **intermediarios**.
:::

### 5. Hearsay (CJA 2003, Part 11, Chapter 2)

**Hearsay** = a **statement not made in oral evidence** in the proceedings, relied on as evidence of the **matter stated** (s. 114). It is **admissible only if** (s. 114(1)):

- (a) a **statute** makes it admissible (e.g. **s. 116** unavailable witness, **s. 117** business documents);
- (b) a **preserved common law rule** applies (e.g. **res gestae**, confessions);
- (c) **all parties agree**; or
- (d) the court is satisfied it's in the **interests of justice**.

**s. 116 – witness unavailable**: the maker is **dead**; **unfit** because of bodily or mental condition; **outside the UK** and it's not reasonably practicable to secure attendance; **cannot be found** despite reasonable steps; or does not give evidence through **fear** (which needs the **court's leave**).

:::es
**Hearsay** = declaración **fuera del juicio** usada para probar **lo que dice** (s. 114). Solo admisible si: **ley** lo permite (s. 116 testigo no disponible; s. 117 documentos mercantiles), **regla de common law** conservada (res gestae, confesiones), **acuerdo de las partes** o **interés de la justicia**. **s. 116**: el testigo ha **muerto**, no está **en condiciones**, está **fuera del Reino Unido**, **no se le encuentra** o **tiene miedo** (con **permiso del tribunal**).
:::

### 6. Bad character (CJA 2003, s. 101 – the gateways)

Evidence of a **defendant's bad character** is admissible only through one of **seven gateways** (s. 101(1)):

- (a) all parties **agree**;
- (b) the defendant **introduces** it;
- (c) **important explanatory evidence**;
- (d) relevant to an **important matter in issue** between defendant and prosecution (e.g. **propensity**);
- (e) **substantial probative value** on an important matter in issue between **co-defendants**;
- (f) to **correct a false impression** given by the defendant;
- (g) the defendant has **attacked another person's character**.

For (d) and (g), the court **must not admit** it if it would have such an **adverse effect on fairness** that it ought not to be admitted (s. 101(3)).

:::es
**Mal carácter del acusado**: solo por **7 puertas** (s. 101): a) **acuerdo**; b) lo **introduce el acusado**; c) prueba **explicativa importante**; d) relevante para un **asunto importante** entre acusado y acusación (p. ej. **propensión**); e) **valor probatorio sustancial** entre **coacusados**; f) **corregir una impresión falsa**; g) el acusado **atacó el carácter de otro**. En (d) y (g) el juez lo excluye si afecta a la **equidad** del juicio.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **Domestic assault.** A husband is charged with assaulting his wife; she doesn't want to give evidence. → She is **compellable** for the prosecution (assault on the spouse, s. 80).
2. **Burglary at a neighbour's.** A husband is charged with burglary of a neighbour's house. → The wife is **competent but not compellable** for the prosecution (not a specified offence).
3. **Dead witness.** A key witness died before trial; her signed statement exists. → Possible **s. 116** hearsay (witness dead).
4. **The officer in court.** You want to use your notebook. → **Refresh memory** under s. 139 (notes made at the time).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **s. 80**: compellable for prosecution only for **assault/injury/threat** to **spouse** or **under 16**, or **sexual** offences against **under 16** – theft, fraud or burglary **don't** qualify.
- **Sworn evidence** needs **14+** (not 16 or 18).
- **Competence** test is about **understanding questions and giving intelligible answers**, not age.
- **s. 116 fear**: needs the **court's leave**.
- **Bad character**: 7 gateways; **(d) and (g)** subject to the **fairness** exclusion in s. 101(3).

## Summary

1. Everyone is competent (s. 53 YJCEA) unless they can't understand/give intelligible answers.
2. Oath from 14 (s. 55); otherwise unsworn.
3. Spouse compellable for prosecution only in specified offences (s. 80 PACE).
4. Hearsay admissible via statute (s. 116/117), common law, agreement or interests of justice.
5. Bad character: 7 gateways (s. 101 CJA 2003); fairness check for (d) and (g).
