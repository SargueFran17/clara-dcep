## Objectives

- Know the **roles** under the CPIA Code of Practice: **investigator**, **officer in charge (OIC)**, **disclosure officer**, **prosecutor**.
- Apply the duties to **pursue reasonable lines of enquiry**, **record**, **retain**, **schedule** and **reveal** material.
- Understand the **disclosure test** (s. 3 CPIA), **defence statements**, the **continuing duty** and **sensitive material/PII**.

## Explanation

### 1. Why disclosure matters

A fair trial requires the defence to see material that could **help** them or **undermine** the prosecution. Failures cause **miscarriages of justice** and collapsed trials. The regime is in the **Criminal Procedure and Investigations Act 1996 (CPIA)**, its **Code of Practice** and the **Attorney General's Guidelines**.

:::es
Un juicio justo exige que la defensa vea el material que podría **ayudarla** o **debilitar** a la acusación. Los fallos causan **condenas injustas** y juicios que se caen. Regulado en la **CPIA 1996**, su **Código de Práctica** y las **directrices del Attorney General**.
:::

### 2. Roles

| Role | Main duties |
|---|---|
| **Investigator** | any officer involved in the investigation – must **record and retain** relevant material |
| **Officer in charge (OIC)** | directs the investigation; ensures **all reasonable lines of enquiry** are pursued, **whether they point towards or away from the suspect** |
| **Disclosure officer** | examines material, **creates schedules**, **reveals** material to the prosecutor, certifies compliance |
| **Prosecutor** | decides what meets the **disclosure test** and discloses it to the defence |

(One person can hold more than one role in smaller cases.)

:::es
**Roles**: **investigador** (registrar y conservar); **OIC** (dirige; asegura que se siguen **todas las líneas razonables, a favor o en contra** del sospechoso); **disclosure officer** (examina, hace los **listados** y los **revela** al fiscal); **fiscal** (decide qué pasa el test y lo entrega a la defensa).
:::

### 3. Relevant material – record and retain

- **Relevant material** = material that appears to have **some bearing on any offence under investigation, any person being investigated, or the surrounding circumstances**, unless it's incapable of having any impact on the case.
- It must be **recorded** in a durable or retrievable form and **retained** (e.g. notebooks, CCTV, 999 recordings, first descriptions, messages, crime reports, custody records, witness statements and their **drafts** if they differ).
- **Retention** (general rule – ⚠ VERIFICAR): at least until a decision is taken whether to prosecute; if there is a prosecution, until the accused is **acquitted** or **convicted** or the prosecution decides not to proceed; after conviction, until the person is **released from custody** or **6 months** from conviction, whichever is later (longer if an appeal is pending).

:::es
**Material relevante**: lo que parezca tener **alguna relación** con el delito, la persona investigada o las circunstancias. Hay que **registrarlo** y **conservarlo** (libretas, CCTV, grabaciones del 999, primeras descripciones, mensajes, borradores de declaraciones si difieren…). **Conservación** (⚠): hasta decidir si se acusa; si hay juicio, hasta absolución o condena; tras condena, hasta que salga de prisión o **6 meses** desde la condena (lo más tarde), más si hay recurso.
:::

### 4. Schedules (Manual of Guidance forms)

- **MG6C** – schedule of **non-sensitive unused material**.
- **MG6D** – schedule of **sensitive** unused material (e.g. informant details, covert techniques, third-party private information) – not given to the defence.
- **MG6E** – disclosure officer's report: draws the prosecutor's attention to material that **might meet the test**.
- Descriptions must be **clear and detailed** enough for the prosecutor to judge – "miscellaneous papers" is not acceptable.

:::es
**Listados**: **MG6C** (material no utilizado **no sensible**), **MG6D** (**sensible**: informantes, técnicas encubiertas…; no se entrega a la defensa), **MG6E** (informe del disclosure officer sobre lo que **podría** pasar el test). Descripciones **claras y concretas**.
:::

### 5. The disclosure test (s. 3 CPIA)

The prosecutor must disclose any **prosecution material not previously disclosed** which **might reasonably be considered capable of undermining the case for the prosecution** against the accused **or of assisting the case for the accused**. Examples: a witness who couldn't identify the suspect, a different first description, previous convictions of a key witness, material pointing to another suspect.

:::es
**Test (s. 3)**: se revela el material **no revelado** que **razonablemente pueda debilitar el caso de la acusación o ayudar al acusado** (p. ej. un testigo que no reconoció al sospechoso, una primera descripción distinta, antecedentes de un testigo clave, indicios de otro autor).
:::

### 6. Defence statement and continuing duty

- **Crown Court**: after initial disclosure, the accused **must** serve a **defence statement** (s. 5) setting out the defence, matters of fact in dispute, and why. In the **magistrates' court** it is **voluntary** (s. 6).
- The prosecutor has a **continuing duty** to keep disclosure **under review** (s. 7A) – especially after the defence statement.
- The defence can apply to court for specific disclosure (s. 8).

### 7. Sensitive material and PII

Sensitive material that **meets** the disclosure test can only be withheld with a **court ruling** on **public interest immunity (PII)**. If the court orders disclosure, the prosecution may have to **drop the case** to protect the material.

:::es
**Escrito de defensa**: **obligatorio** en la Crown Court (s. 5), **voluntario** en el juzgado de magistrados (s. 6). Deber **continuo** de revisión (s. 7A). Material **sensible** que pasa el test solo puede retenerse si el **juez** lo autoriza (**PII**); si no, puede que haya que **retirar la acusación**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The first description.** A witness first described the robber as "white, 20s", but the suspect is Black and 40. → The first description is **relevant**, must be **retained/scheduled** and very likely **meets the test**.
2. **The CCTV that shows nothing.** CCTV from a nearby shop doesn't show the suspect. → Still **relevant** (points away?) – record, retain, schedule.
3. **The informant.** An informant's tip led to the arrest. → **Sensitive** – **MG6D**; if it meets the test, prosecutor seeks a **PII** ruling.
4. **The phone.** The victim's phone might contain messages to the suspect. → **Reasonable line of enquiry** – proportionate examination (⚠ guidelines on digital material and third-party/victim privacy).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- Lines of enquiry **towards or away** from the suspect.
- **MG6C** non-sensitive; **MG6D** sensitive; **MG6E** report.
- Defence statement **compulsory in Crown Court**, **voluntary** in magistrates'.
- The prosecutor (not the police) decides what is disclosed – but the police must **reveal** everything relevant to the prosecutor.
- Sensitive material can't be withheld without a **court** (PII) if it meets the test.

## Summary

1. CPIA 1996 + Code + AG's Guidelines.
2. Roles: investigator, OIC, disclosure officer, prosecutor.
3. Reasonable lines of enquiry (towards or away); record and retain relevant material.
4. Schedules MG6C/D/E; test: undermine prosecution or assist accused (s. 3).
5. Defence statement (Crown Court compulsory); continuing duty (s. 7A); PII for sensitive material.
