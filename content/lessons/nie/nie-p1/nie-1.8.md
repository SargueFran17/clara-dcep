## Objectives

- Know **when an identification procedure must be held** and who runs it (the **identification officer**).
- Compare the **four eyewitness procedures**: video identification, identification parade, group identification and confrontation.
- Understand the rules for **fingerprints, footwear impressions, samples and photographs** under PACE and Code D, and the **Turnbull** warning.

## Explanation

### 1. What Code D covers

Code D sets the rules for **identifying people** in criminal investigations:

1. **Eyewitness identification** – witnesses identifying a suspect (or not).
2. **Recognition** from **CCTV / images** (e.g. officers who know the person).
3. **Fingerprints and footwear impressions**.
4. **Samples** (intimate and non-intimate) and **DNA**.
5. **Photographs**, marks and features.

Why so strict? Mistaken eyewitness identification is a classic cause of **wrongful convictions**. Breaches can lead to **exclusion** of evidence (s. 78 PACE) and to a **judicial warning**.

:::es
**Code D** regula cómo se **identifica** a una persona: **testigos oculares**, **reconocimiento** por imágenes (CCTV), **huellas y marcas de calzado**, **muestras/ADN** y **fotografías**. Es muy estricto porque los errores de identificación causan **condenas injustas**; incumplirlo puede llevar a **excluir la prueba** (s. 78).
:::

### 2. First description

Before any procedure, record the witness's **first description** of the suspect, and make it available to the suspect/solicitor before the procedure (⚠ VERIFICAR momento exacto). The description is later compared with the suspect's appearance.

### 3. When must an identification procedure be held?

When:
- a **suspect is known and available**, **and**
- the suspect **disputes** being the person seen, **and**
- a witness **has identified**, or there is a **reasonable chance** the witness can identify, them,

an identification procedure **shall be held**, **unless** it is **not practicable** or would **serve no useful purpose** (e.g. the suspect is **well known** to the witness, or the suspect admits being present and the dispute is about what happened) – Code D, para. 3.12 (⚠ VERIFICAR el texto).

- **Known** = there's enough information to justify **arresting** them for suspected involvement.
- **Available** = they are **immediately available**, or will be within a reasonably short time, **and willing** to take part.

:::es
Si el sospechoso es **conocido y está disponible**, **niega** ser la persona vista y un testigo **le ha identificado o puede identificarle**, **hay que hacer** un procedimiento de identificación, **salvo** que no sea factible o **no sirva para nada** (p. ej. el testigo le **conoce bien**). **Known** = hay base para detenerle; **available** = disponible pronto **y dispuesto**.
:::

### 4. When the suspect is NOT known – street identification

If the suspect is **not known**, a witness may be taken around the **neighbourhood** to see if they can identify the person, but:
- record the **first description** before;
- **do not direct** the witness's attention to any individual;
- if there are several witnesses, keep them **separate**; once one identifies someone, others should go through a **formal** procedure where possible.

Showing **photographs** (albums, mugshots) is allowed only when the suspect's identity is **not known**, following Annex E (⚠ VERIFICAR número de fotos y reglas).

:::es
**Sospechoso desconocido**: puedes llevar al testigo por la zona para ver si reconoce a alguien, **sin dirigir su atención** y con los testigos **separados**. Enseñar **fotos** (álbumes) solo si la identidad **no se conoce** (Annex E ⚠).
:::

### 5. The identification officer

The procedures are run by an **identification officer**: an officer **not below the rank of inspector** who is **not involved** with the investigation (some tasks can be delegated to other officers/staff, but not to anyone involved in the investigation). Officers involved must **not** take part.

:::es
**Identification officer**: rango **mínimo inspector** y **no involucrado** en la investigación. Los investigadores no participan en el procedimiento.
:::

### 6. The four eyewitness procedures – in order of preference

| Procedure | How it works | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Video identification** | the witness views **moving images** of the suspect and **at least eight** others who resemble the suspect | the **normal** first choice; the suspect/solicitor can see the images and object |
| **Identification parade** | the witness views a **line of people** including the suspect and **at least eight** others (⚠ más si hay dos sospechosos) | used if video ID is not practicable or a parade is more suitable |
| **Group identification** | the witness sees the suspect in an **informal group** (e.g. at a station concourse or shopping centre) | can be **covert** if the suspect refuses; others should resemble the suspect where practicable |
| **Confrontation** | the witness is shown **only the suspect** | **last resort** when no other procedure is practicable |

Before the procedure the suspect is given a **notice** explaining it, the right to **free legal advice**, that a **refusal** may be given in evidence, and that **covert** procedures may be used. The procedure is recorded and the witness's words are noted.

:::es
**Orden de preferencia**: 1) **video identification** (imágenes en movimiento del sospechoso y **al menos 8** personas parecidas); 2) **rueda (ID parade)** con **al menos 8** más; 3) **group identification** (en un grupo informal, puede ser **encubierta**); 4) **confrontation** (solo el sospechoso) = **último recurso**. Antes, se entrega al sospechoso una **notificación** (derecho a abogado, que negarse puede usarse como prueba, posible procedimiento encubierto).
:::

### 7. Recognition from images (CCTV)

When officers or others are shown **CCTV/images** to see if they **recognise** someone, Code D has rules too (e.g. record **how** they recognise the person, show images to people **individually**, don't suggest names) (⚠ VERIFICAR Part B de Code D).

### 8. Fingerprints, footwear, samples and photographs (PACE ss. 61–64A)

- **Fingerprints (s. 61)** and **footwear impressions (s. 61A)**: can be taken **with consent**, or **without consent** in situations such as after **arrest for a recordable offence** (if not already taken in the investigation), or after **charge/conviction** for a recordable offence. Reasonable force may be used where taken without consent (⚠ VERIFICAR condiciones exactas).
- **Intimate samples (s. 62)**: e.g. **blood, semen, urine, pubic hair, dental impressions**, swabs from **genitals or body orifices other than the mouth**. Need **authorisation by an inspector** (or above) **and** the person's **written consent**; refusal without good cause may allow the court to draw **inferences**. Taken by a **registered medical practitioner/health care professional** (dental impressions by a dentist).
- **Non-intimate samples (s. 63)**: e.g. **hair (not pubic)**, **nails**, swab from the **mouth**, **saliva**, **footprint**, skin impression. Can be taken **without consent** in certain circumstances (e.g. after arrest for a recordable offence).
- **Photographs (s. 64A)**: can be taken of a detained person, with or without consent (force cannot be used to take a photo – ⚠ VERIFICAR).
- **Speculative searches** (s. 63A): checking fingerprints/samples against national databases (IDENT1, NDNAD).

:::es
- **Huellas (s. 61)** y **calzado (s. 61A)**: con consentimiento o, en ciertos casos, **sin** él (p. ej. tras **detención por delito registrable**).
- **Muestras íntimas (s. 62)**: sangre, semen, orina, vello púbico, **impresiones dentales**, hisopos de genitales u orificios **salvo la boca**: **autorización de inspector + consentimiento por escrito**; negarse sin motivo puede dar **inferencias**.
- **Muestras no íntimas (s. 63)**: pelo (no púbico), uñas, **hisopo bucal**, saliva, huella del pie: pueden tomarse **sin consentimiento** en ciertos casos.
- **Fotos (s. 64A)** y **búsquedas especulativas** en bases de datos (s. 63A).
:::

### 9. The Turnbull warning

Where a case depends **wholly or substantially** on disputed **eyewitness identification**, the judge must warn the jury of the **special need for caution** and tell them to examine the **circumstances** of the identification (*R v Turnbull* (1977)) – the **ADVOKATE** points: **A**mount of time, **D**istance, **V**isibility, **O**bstruction, **K**nown or seen before, **A**ny reason to remember, **T**ime lapse, **E**rrors in the description.

:::es
Si el caso depende sobre todo de una **identificación ocular discutida**, el juez debe **advertir al jurado** que tenga especial **cautela** (*Turnbull*) y que valore las circunstancias (**ADVOKATE**).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **Robbery in a shop.** The cashier gives a description; two days later CID arrests SAM, who says "it wasn't me". → Suspect **known and available**, **disputes** identity, witness may identify → hold a **video identification** (unless not practicable/no useful purpose).
2. **The ex-boyfriend.** A victim says her **ex-partner of three years** assaulted her; he denies being there. → An ID procedure would likely **serve no useful purpose** – she **knows** him well (it's a **recognition** case).
3. **Street ID.** Five minutes after a street robbery, you drive the victim around and she points at a man. → Allowed if the suspect was **not known**; don't direct her attention; record her **first description** first.
4. **Mouth swab.** You want a DNA sample from a man arrested for burglary who refuses. → A **mouth swab** is **non-intimate** – may be taken **without consent** in the right circumstances.

:::es
1. Robo en tienda y sospechoso que lo niega → **video ID**.
2. Ex-pareja de 3 años → probablemente **no sirve para nada** (reconocimiento).
3. Identificación en la calle → solo si el sospechoso **no era conocido**, sin dirigir la atención.
4. **Hisopo bucal** = muestra **no íntima** → puede tomarse sin consentimiento en ciertos casos.
:::

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Mouth swab = non-intimate**; **dental impression = intimate**. Swabs from **other orifices** = intimate.
- **Intimate sample**: **inspector** authorisation **and written consent** – both.
- The **identification officer** is at least an **inspector** and **not involved** in the investigation.
- **Video ID** is normally first; **confrontation** is the **last resort**.
- An ID procedure is **not required** where it would serve **no useful purpose** (e.g. well known to the witness).
- **Street identification** only when the suspect is **not known**.
- **Turnbull** applies when ID is **disputed** and the case depends **wholly or substantially** on it.

## Summary

1. Code D: eyewitness ID, recognition from images, fingerprints/footwear, samples, photos.
2. Known + available + disputes + witness may identify → procedure shall be held (unless impracticable/no useful purpose).
3. Identification officer: inspector+, not involved; video ID (8+ others) → parade → group → confrontation.
4. Intimate samples: inspector authority + written consent; mouth swab is non-intimate.
5. Turnbull warning and ADVOKATE for disputed eyewitness evidence.
