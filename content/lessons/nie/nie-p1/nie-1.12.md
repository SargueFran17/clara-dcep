## Objectives

- Distinguish **directed surveillance**, **intrusive surveillance** and **covert human intelligence sources (CHIS)** under **Part II of RIPA 2000**.
- Know the **grounds**, the tests of **necessity and proportionality**, and **collateral intrusion**.
- Remember **who authorises** each and for **how long** (in general terms).

## Explanation

### 1. Why RIPA exists

Covert techniques interfere with **privacy** (Article 8 ECHR). RIPA Part II provides a **lawful framework**: if surveillance or a CHIS is **properly authorised**, the conduct is **lawful for all purposes** (s. 27). Without authorisation, evidence may be challenged (s. 78 PACE) and the force may breach the Human Rights Act.

:::es
Las técnicas encubiertas afectan a la **intimidad** (art. 8 CEDH). La **Parte II de RIPA** da un marco legal: si están **bien autorizadas**, son **legales a todos los efectos** (s. 27). Sin autorización, la prueba puede impugnarse y se puede vulnerar la HRA.
:::

### 2. Directed surveillance (s. 26(2))

Surveillance that is:
1. **covert** (carried out so the person is **unaware** of it),
2. **not intrusive**,
3. for a **specific investigation or operation**,
4. **likely to result in obtaining private information** about a person,
5. **not** an **immediate response** to events where it would not be reasonably practicable to get an authorisation.

Examples: following a suspect in public over days; covertly watching a house from the street; repeated covert viewing of someone's social media profile as part of an investigation (⚠ VERIFICAR guía sobre internet).

**Not** directed surveillance: **overt** CCTV in town centres; an officer **reacting immediately** to something they see on patrol.

:::es
**Vigilancia dirigida**: **encubierta**, **no intrusiva**, para una **investigación concreta**, que **probablemente obtenga información privada**, y **no** como **reacción inmediata** a hechos. Ej.: seguir a alguien varios días por la calle, vigilar una casa desde fuera. **No** lo es: CCTV **visible** general, o reaccionar en el momento durante una patrulla.
:::

### 3. Intrusive surveillance (s. 26(3))

Covert surveillance carried out in relation to anything taking place **on residential premises** or in a **private vehicle**, and involving the **presence of an individual** on the premises/in the vehicle, **or** a **surveillance device** there. A device **outside** that gives information of the **same quality and detail** as if it were inside is also intrusive.

- Authorised (for police) by the **chief officer** (Chief Constable or designated deputy) and normally needs **prior approval** by a **Judicial Commissioner** (Investigatory Powers Commissioner's Office), except in urgent cases.
- Grounds are **narrower**: national security, **preventing or detecting serious crime**, economic well-being (national security).

:::es
**Vigilancia intrusiva**: en **viviendas** o **vehículos privados**, con una **persona dentro** o un **dispositivo** dentro (o fuera, si da la misma calidad que dentro). La autoriza el **jefe de la fuerza** con **aprobación previa de un Judicial Commissioner** (salvo urgencia), y solo para **delitos graves**, seguridad nacional o bienestar económico.
:::

### 4. Covert human intelligence source – CHIS (s. 26(8))

A person who **establishes or maintains a personal or other relationship** with someone for the **covert purpose** of obtaining information or providing access to it, or **covertly disclosing** information obtained through that relationship. Includes **informants** and **undercover officers**.

- Every CHIS must have a **handler** (day-to-day contact) and a **controller** (oversight), and proper **records** (s. 29(5)).
- **Juvenile** (under 18) and **vulnerable** CHIS have extra safeguards, higher authorisation and shorter durations; an under-16 cannot be used to give information about their **parents/carers** (⚠ VERIFICAR).
- **CHIS (Criminal Conduct) Act 2021**: allows **criminal conduct authorisations** for a CHIS in limited circumstances (s. 29B) (⚠ VERIFICAR alcance).
- A member of the public who **volunteers** information without a relationship being used covertly is **not** necessarily a CHIS – but watch for "**status drift**".

:::es
**CHIS** = persona que **establece o mantiene una relación** con alguien con el **propósito encubierto** de obtener información o revelarla (informantes y agentes infiltrados). Necesita **handler** y **controller** y **registros**. Menores y vulnerables: más garantías. **CHIS Criminal Conduct Act 2021**: autorizaciones para conducta delictiva limitada. Un ciudadano que informa **sin** relación encubierta no es CHIS, pero ojo al **"status drift"**.
:::

### 5. Grounds, necessity, proportionality, collateral intrusion

An authorising officer must believe the activity is **necessary** on a **ground** in **s. 28(3)** (surveillance) / **s. 29(3)** (CHIS), e.g.:
- interests of **national security**;
- **preventing or detecting crime** or **preventing disorder**;
- **economic well-being** of the UK (as relevant to national security);
- **public safety**; **protecting public health**; **tax** collection;

**and** that it is **proportionate** to what it seeks to achieve (could less intrusive methods work?), considering **collateral intrusion** (privacy of people **other than** the target).

:::es
Hay que creer que es **necesaria** por un **motivo legal** (seguridad nacional, **prevenir o detectar delitos o desórdenes**, bienestar económico, seguridad pública, salud pública, impuestos) **y proporcionada**, valorando la **intrusión colateral** (terceros afectados).
:::

### 6. Authorisation and duration (general picture ⚠ VERIFICAR)

| Activity | Police authorising officer (general) | Duration (written) |
|---|---|---|
| Directed surveillance | **Superintendent** (urgent: **Inspector**) | **3 months** (urgent oral: **72 hours**) |
| CHIS | **Superintendent** (urgent: **Inspector**) | **12 months** (juvenile CHIS: **4 months**) |
| Intrusive surveillance | **Chief Constable** + **Judicial Commissioner** approval | **3 months** |
| Likely to obtain **confidential/legally privileged** information | **higher** rank (e.g. ACC/Chief Constable) | as per Code |

Authorisations must be **reviewed** regularly and **cancelled** as soon as they are no longer needed; **renewals** need fresh justification.

:::es
**Visión general** (⚠ verifica): **vigilancia dirigida** → **superintendente** (urgencia: **inspector**), **3 meses** (urgente oral: **72 h**). **CHIS** → superintendente, **12 meses** (menores: **4 meses**). **Intrusiva** → **Chief Constable + Judicial Commissioner**, **3 meses**. Información **confidencial o con secreto profesional** → rango **superior**. Revisar, renovar con justificación y **cancelar** cuando ya no haga falta.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. **The drug dealer's routine.** CID want to follow a suspected dealer for two weeks in public to see who he meets. → **Directed surveillance** – needs authorisation.
2. **The bug in the car.** A device placed **inside** the suspect's **private car** to record conversations. → **Intrusive surveillance**.
3. **The patrol officer.** On patrol, you see a man acting suspiciously and watch him from a doorway for 5 minutes. → **Immediate response** – not directed surveillance.
4. **The neighbour.** A neighbour starts befriending a suspect **at police request** to find out about his plans. → She has become a **CHIS** – authorisation, handler and controller needed.

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- **Directed** vs **intrusive**: intrusive = **residential premises or private vehicle** + person/device **inside** (or equivalent quality). A **hotel bedroom** or prison cell can be residential (⚠ VERIFICAR).
- **Immediate response** is **not** directed surveillance.
- **CHIS** needs a **relationship** used **covertly** – an anonymous caller is not automatically a CHIS.
- **Intrusive** grounds are **narrower** (serious crime) than directed.
- **Necessity** AND **proportionality** – both, plus collateral intrusion.

## Summary

1. RIPA Part II: properly authorised conduct is lawful (s. 27); Art. 8 ECHR.
2. Directed: covert, not intrusive, specific operation, private information, not immediate response.
3. Intrusive: residential premises / private vehicle, person or device inside; chief officer + Judicial Commissioner.
4. CHIS: covert use of a relationship; handler + controller; juveniles extra safeguards.
5. Necessity + proportionality + collateral intrusion; superintendent / 3 months (DS), 12 months (CHIS) ⚠.
