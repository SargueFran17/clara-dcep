## Objectives

- Know what the **Crown Prosecution Service (CPS)** does and its relationship with the police.
- Understand the **Full Code Test** (evidential + public interest) and the **Threshold Test**.
- Know, in general terms, **who decides to charge**: CPS or police.

## Explanation

### The CPS

The **CPS** is the main **public prosecutor** for criminal cases investigated by the police in **England and Wales**. It is **independent** of the police. It is led by the **Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP)**. It:

- advises the police during investigations;
- **decides whether to charge** in more serious or complex cases;
- prepares and presents cases in court;
- deals with **disclosure** and supports victims and witnesses with the police.

:::es
La **CPS** (Fiscalía de la Corona) es **independiente** de la policía y la dirige el **DPP**. Asesora en las investigaciones, **decide si se acusa** en los casos más graves o complejos, lleva los casos a juicio y se ocupa del **disclosure** junto con la policía.
:::

### The Code for Crown Prosecutors – the Full Code Test

Prosecutors must apply the **Full Code Test**, which has **two stages**:

1. **Evidential stage** – is there **sufficient evidence** to provide a **realistic prospect of conviction** against each suspect on each charge? (An objective test: is a conviction **more likely than not**, considering whether evidence is **admissible**, **reliable** and **credible**, and what the defence might say.)
2. **Public interest stage** – if the evidential stage is passed: is a **prosecution required in the public interest**? Factors include the **seriousness** of the offence, the suspect's **culpability**, **harm to the victim**, the suspect's **age/maturity**, the **impact on the community**, whether prosecution is **proportionate**, and whether sources of information need **protecting**.

If the evidential stage is **not** met, the case must not go ahead, however serious it is.

:::es
**Full Code Test** (dos fases):
1. **Fase probatoria**: ¿hay pruebas suficientes para una **perspectiva realista de condena** (más probable que no)? Pruebas **admisibles, fiables y creíbles**.
2. **Interés público**: ¿hace falta acusar? Gravedad, culpabilidad, daño a la víctima, edad, impacto en la comunidad, proporcionalidad…
Si no se pasa la fase 1, **no se acusa**, por grave que sea.
:::

### The Threshold Test

Used **only in limited cases**: when the suspect presents a **substantial bail risk** (so they should be **remanded in custody**) and **not all the evidence is available yet**, but there are reasonable grounds to believe further evidence will become available. The prosecutor must be satisfied there are **reasonable grounds to suspect** the person committed the offence, among other conditions. It must be **reviewed** once the further evidence arrives (⚠ VERIFICAR los requisitos exactos en el Code).

:::es
**Threshold Test**: solo en casos limitados, cuando hay un **riesgo serio** si se le deja en libertad y **aún no están todas las pruebas**, pero se espera que lleguen. Debe revisarse con el Full Code Test cuando lleguen.
:::

### Who charges?

Under the **Director's Guidance on Charging** (issued by the DPP), the **police can decide to charge** some offences (generally **less serious**, e.g. many **summary-only** offences and some **either-way** offences), while the **CPS decides** in other cases (e.g. **indictable-only** offences and other specified categories). The detail is technical and changes with new editions of the guidance (⚠ VERIFICAR la edición vigente y la tabla de competencias en la academia).

:::es
Según la **Director's Guidance on Charging**, la **policía** puede acusar en delitos **menos graves** (muchos *summary-only* y algunos *either-way*) y la **CPS** decide en el resto (p. ej. *indictable-only*). Es técnico y cambia con cada edición: ⚠ verifica la versión vigente.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. You have a suspect for a burglary with CCTV, fingerprints and an admission. → You prepare a **file (MG forms)** and seek a **charging decision** according to the Guidance.
2. A domestic assault suspect is very likely to reoffend tonight, but the forensic results will take days. → The **Threshold Test** may allow a charge and remand while waiting for the evidence.

## Common mistakes & traps

- Thinking the police **never** charge – they do, for some offences.
- Confusing **"realistic prospect of conviction"** (charging test) with **"beyond reasonable doubt"** (the standard at trial).
- Forgetting that the **public interest** stage comes **after** the evidential stage.

## Summary

1. CPS = independent prosecutor led by the DPP.
2. Full Code Test: evidential stage → public interest stage.
3. Evidential = realistic prospect of conviction (more likely than not).
4. Threshold Test = limited, bail risk + evidence still to come; must be reviewed.
5. Director's Guidance on Charging splits police vs CPS decisions.
