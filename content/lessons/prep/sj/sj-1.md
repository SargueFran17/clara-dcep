## Objectives

- Describe how policing in **England and Wales** is organised.
- Know the role of the **Home Office, PCCs, chief constables, College of Policing, IOPC and HMICFRS**.
- Place **Hampshire & Isle of Wight Constabulary** in that map.

## Explanation

### The tripartite (now "quadripartite") idea

- **43 territorial police forces** in England and Wales, each led by a **Chief Constable** (in London: the **Metropolitan Police Commissioner** and the **City of London Police Commissioner**).
- **Home Secretary / Home Office** – national policing policy, funding and legislation.
- **Police and Crime Commissioner (PCC)** – **elected** by the public (since 2012) for each force area. Sets the **Police and Crime Plan**, sets the budget and **holds the Chief Constable to account**; can appoint and dismiss the Chief Constable. (In some areas a **mayor** does this role.)
- **Police and Crime Panel** – scrutinises the PCC.
- The **Chief Constable** keeps **operational independence**: the PCC decides priorities, but not how to run individual investigations.

:::es
- **43 fuerzas territoriales** en Inglaterra y Gales, cada una con un **Chief Constable** (jefe/a).
- **Home Office** (Ministerio del Interior): política nacional, financiación y leyes.
- **PCC** (Comisionado de Policía y Delincuencia): **elegido** por la ciudadanía; fija prioridades (Police and Crime Plan) y presupuesto, y **pide cuentas** al Chief Constable.
- El **Chief Constable** mantiene la **independencia operativa**.
:::

### National bodies you must know

| Body | What it does |
|---|---|
| **College of Policing** (2012) | Professional body: standards, **Code of Ethics**, **APP**, training curriculum, **PIP**; runs the **NIE** |
| **IOPC** – Independent Office for Police Conduct (2018, replaced the IPCC) | Oversees the police **complaints** system and investigates the most serious matters (e.g. deaths after police contact) |
| **HMICFRS** – His Majesty's Inspectorate of Constabulary and Fire & Rescue Services | **Inspects** forces (e.g. PEEL inspections: effectiveness, efficiency, legitimacy) and publishes reports |
| **NPCC** – National Police Chiefs' Council | Coordinates chief officers on national operational issues |
| **NCA** – National Crime Agency | Leads against serious and organised crime |
| **CPS** – Crown Prosecution Service | Prosecutes; decides on charge in most cases (lesson SJ3) |

:::es
- **College of Policing**: estándares, **Código Ético**, **APP** (guías), formación, **PIP**, **NIE**.
- **IOPC**: supervisa las **quejas** y investiga los casos más graves.
- **HMICFRS**: **inspecciona** las fuerzas (informes PEEL).
- **NPCC**: coordina a los jefes de policía. **NCA**: crimen organizado grave. **CPS**: fiscalía.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. A member of the public complains that an officer was rude. → First handled by the **force's Professional Standards Department**; serious cases can be referred to the **IOPC**.
2. A report says your force is "requires improvement" at investigating crime. → That is an **HMICFRS PEEL** inspection.

## Common mistakes & traps

- Confusing **IOPC** (complaints and conduct) with **HMICFRS** (inspection).
- Thinking the PCC is a police officer – the PCC is an **elected politician/official**, not a warranted officer.
- Thinking Scotland and Northern Ireland are part of the 43 forces – they have **Police Scotland** and the **PSNI**.

## Summary

1. 43 territorial forces in England and Wales, each with a Chief Constable.
2. Home Office = national policy and funding.
3. PCC (elected) = priorities, budget, holds the Chief Constable to account.
4. College of Policing = standards, APP, Code of Ethics, PIP/NIE.
5. IOPC = complaints; HMICFRS = inspections.
