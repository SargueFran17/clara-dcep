## Objectives

- Describe the **criminal courts** of England and Wales and what each one does.
- Follow a case from **first appearance** to **appeal**.
- Know where the **youth court** fits.

## Explanation

### The courts

| Court | Who decides | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Magistrates' court** | **3 lay magistrates** (justices of the peace) with a **legal adviser**, or a **district judge** (paid, legally qualified) | **Every** criminal case starts here. Tries **summary** offences and **either-way** offences kept there; bail and remand decisions; sends serious cases to the Crown Court |
| **Youth court** | specially trained magistrates or a district judge | Cases for defendants aged **10–17** (most offences) |
| **Crown Court** | **judge** (+ **jury** for not-guilty pleas) | Trials of **indictable-only** offences and **either-way** offences sent there; **sentencing** cases committed by magistrates; **appeals** from the magistrates' court |
| **High Court** (King's Bench Division / Divisional Court) | judges | Appeals **on points of law** from magistrates ("**case stated**") and **judicial review** |
| **Court of Appeal (Criminal Division)** | senior judges | Appeals against **conviction** and/or **sentence** from the Crown Court |
| **Supreme Court** | Justices of the Supreme Court | Final appeal on **points of law of general public importance** |

:::es
- **Magistrates' court**: **todos** los casos empiezan aquí; juzga los delitos **leves** y los **mixtos** que se queden; decide fianzas. Lo forman **3 magistrados legos** con un asesor legal, o un **juez de distrito**.
- **Youth court**: menores de **10 a 17** años.
- **Crown Court**: juez **+ jurado**; delitos graves, condenas remitidas por magistrados y **recursos** contra sus decisiones.
- **High Court**: recursos **sobre cuestiones de derecho** desde magistrados (*case stated*) y revisión judicial.
- **Court of Appeal**: recursos contra condena o pena de la Crown Court.
- **Supreme Court**: última instancia en cuestiones de derecho de importancia general.
:::

### Sentencing powers

Magistrates' sentencing powers are **limited** (custody for a single either-way offence has been **6 or 12 months**, and the limit has **changed several times recently** ⚠ VERIFICAR el límite vigente). If they think a heavier sentence is needed, they can **commit** the case to the **Crown Court for sentence**.

:::es
Los magistrados tienen **límites** de pena (para un delito mixto, **6 o 12 meses** de prisión; ha cambiado varias veces recientemente ⚠ verifica). Si creen que hace falta más, lo **remiten a la Crown Court** para sentencia.
:::

### Age of criminal responsibility

In England and Wales a child **under 10** cannot be convicted of a criminal offence (**doli incapax** – conclusively presumed incapable). From **10** they can be prosecuted (usually in the youth court).

:::es
**Menores de 10 años**: no pueden ser condenados (*doli incapax*). Desde los **10**, sí (normalmente en el *youth court*).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. Your burglary suspect (either-way offence) pleads not guilty and elects jury trial. → The case is **sent to the Crown Court**.
2. A 15-year-old is charged with robbery. → First to the **youth court** (it may be sent to the Crown Court in grave cases ⚠ VERIFICAR).
3. The defence says the magistrates got the law wrong. → Appeal **by way of case stated** to the **High Court**.

## Common mistakes & traps

- Thinking serious cases start in the Crown Court – **all** start in the magistrates' court.
- Thinking magistrates are always lawyers – most are **volunteers**.
- Mixing appeal routes: magistrates → **Crown Court** (fact/sentence) or **High Court** (law); Crown Court → **Court of Appeal**.

## Summary

1. All criminal cases start in the magistrates' court.
2. Magistrates' court: summary + either-way kept there; lay magistrates or district judge.
3. Crown Court: judge + jury; indictable cases; appeals from magistrates.
4. Appeals: Crown Court → Court of Appeal → Supreme Court; law points from magistrates → High Court.
5. Under 10 = no criminal responsibility; youth court for 10–17.
