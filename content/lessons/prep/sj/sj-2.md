## Objectives

- Know the **police ranks** in order and how to address each one.
- Understand the difference between **uniformed** policing and the **CID** (detectives).
- Know where you, as a **trainee DC**, fit in.

## Explanation

### Ranks (most forces outside London)

| Rank | Abbrev. | How to address (common practice) |
|---|---|---|
| Constable | PC (uniform) / **DC** (detective) | by name |
| Sergeant | PS / **DS** | "Sarge" / "Sergeant" |
| Inspector | Insp / **DI** | "Sir" / "Ma'am" |
| Chief Inspector | Ch Insp / **DCI** | "Sir" / "Ma'am" |
| Superintendent | Supt / **Det Supt** | "Sir" / "Ma'am" |
| Chief Superintendent | Ch Supt / **Det Ch Supt** | "Sir" / "Ma'am" |
| Assistant Chief Constable | ACC | "Sir" / "Ma'am" |
| Deputy Chief Constable | DCC | "Sir" / "Ma'am" |
| Chief Constable | CC | "Sir" / "Ma'am" / "Chief" |

The **Metropolitan Police** and **City of London Police** use different titles above Chief Superintendent (e.g. Commander, Assistant Commissioner, Commissioner). Rank is the **same** for uniformed officers and detectives – a **DS** and a **PS** have the **same rank**; "Detective" shows the **role**.

How you address senior officers can vary between forces and teams – follow local practice.

:::es
Rangos de abajo arriba: **Constable → Sergeant → Inspector → Chief Inspector → Superintendent → Chief Superintendent → ACC → DCC → Chief Constable**. La **D** (Detective) indica la **función**, no un rango distinto: un **DS** y un **PS** tienen el mismo rango. Tratamiento habitual: "Sarge" al sargento y "Sir/Ma'am" de inspector para arriba (depende de cada equipo).
:::

### Uniform vs CID

- **Uniformed policing** (response, neighbourhood, roads policing): first response to calls, patrol, public reassurance, initial investigation.
- **CID** (Criminal Investigation Department) and other detective units (e.g. **Major Crime**, **Public Protection / safeguarding**, **Serious Organised Crime**): investigate more **serious or complex** crime, plain clothes.
- Detectives are accredited under **PIP** (Professionalising Investigation Programme): **PIP 1** (volume crime, usually uniformed), **PIP 2** (serious and complex – **detectives**), **PIP 3** (major crime – SIO), **PIP 4** (strategic oversight). You are training for **PIP 2**, which needs the **NIE**.

:::es
- **Uniformados**: respuesta a llamadas, patrulla, barrio, tráfico; investigación inicial.
- **CID** y otras unidades de detectives: delitos **graves o complejos**, de paisano.
- **PIP**: niveles 1 a 4. Tú vas a por **PIP 2** (detective), que exige aprobar el **NIE**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. In a briefing, "the DI wants an update by 14:00". → Your **Detective Inspector**: two ranks above you.
2. A PC hands over a burglary with a suspect in custody to CID. → Handover: what was done, exhibits, statements, custody times.

## Common mistakes & traps

- Thinking a DC outranks a PC – **same rank**.
- Writing *"Detective Sergent"* → ✅ **Sergeant**.
- Calling a sergeant "Sir" in some teams sounds odd – listen to how colleagues speak.

## Summary

1. Constable → Sergeant → Inspector → Chief Inspector → Superintendent → Chief Superintendent → ACC → DCC → CC.
2. "Detective" = role, not a higher rank.
3. Met/City use different senior titles.
4. CID = serious/complex investigations; uniform = first response.
5. PIP 1–4; DCs = PIP 2 (NIE required).
