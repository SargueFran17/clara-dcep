## Objectives

- Use **respectful, accurate language** about disability and mental health.
- Communicate effectively with **disabled people** and people in **mental health crisis**.
- Know, in general terms, the police powers in the **Mental Health Act 1983** (ss. 135–136).

## Explanation

### Words to use and to avoid

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| *the disabled*, *the handicapped* | **disabled people**, **people with disabilities** |
| *wheelchair-bound*, *confined to a wheelchair* | **wheelchair user** |
| *suffers from / victim of* (a condition) | **has** (a condition), **lives with** |
| *mentally ill*, *a schizophrenic*, *crazy*, *psycho* | **person with a mental health condition**, **person with schizophrenia** |
| *retarded*, *slow* | **person with a learning disability** |
| *deaf and dumb* | **deaf**, **Deaf** (culturally Deaf, BSL users), **hard of hearing** |
| *normal people* | **non-disabled people** |
| *committed suicide* | **died by suicide**, **took their own life** |

In the UK, **"disabled people"** (identity-first) is widely used and accepted, as is **"people with…"**. When in doubt, **ask the person** what they prefer.

:::es
Evita *wheelchair-bound* (usa **wheelchair user**), *suffers from* (usa **has / lives with**), *mentally ill / psycho* (usa **person with a mental health condition**), *retarded* (usa **learning disability**), *committed suicide* (usa **died by suicide**). En el Reino Unido se usa mucho **"disabled people"**. En la duda, **pregunta** a la persona.
:::

### Communicating well

- Speak **to the person**, not to their carer or interpreter.
- **Deaf** people: face them, don't shout; offer a **BSL interpreter** for formal processes.
- **Autism / learning disability**: short clear sentences, one question at a time, extra time, avoid sarcasm and idioms; consider an **appropriate adult** in custody.
- **Mental health crisis**: calm voice, space, simple choices, listen; think about **vulnerability** and **safeguarding**.

:::es
Habla **a la persona**. **Sordos**: de frente, sin gritar, intérprete de **BSL** si es formal. **Autismo / discapacidad intelectual**: frases cortas, una pregunta cada vez, más tiempo, sin ironías; **appropriate adult** en custodia. **Crisis de salud mental**: calma, espacio, opciones sencillas, escuchar.
:::

### Mental Health Act 1983 – police powers (general idea)

- **Section 136**: an officer may remove a person who **appears to be suffering from a mental disorder** and to be **in immediate need of care or control** to a **place of safety** (or keep them at one), if it's necessary in their interests or for the protection of others. It **cannot** be used in a **private dwelling**. A **health professional** should be consulted first where practicable. The place of safety should normally be a **health-based** one, **not** a police station (⚠ VERIFICAR límites y excepciones vigentes).
- **Section 135**: a **warrant** from a magistrate to enter **premises** to remove a person to a place of safety.
- **Right Care, Right Person**: a national approach so that police attend mental-health-related calls **only when there's a policing role** (e.g. risk to life or crime) and health services lead otherwise (⚠ VERIFICAR cómo lo aplica Hampshire).

:::es
- **s. 136**: retirar a alguien que **parece tener un trastorno mental** y **necesita cuidado o control inmediato** a un **lugar seguro**. **No** en un domicilio privado; consultar a un profesional sanitario si es posible; el lugar seguro **no** debería ser una comisaría (⚠ verifica).
- **s. 135**: **orden judicial** para entrar en un domicilio.
- **Right Care, Right Person**: la policía solo acude a llamadas de salud mental cuando hay un **papel policial** (⚠ verifica en Hampshire).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. A man is standing on the wrong side of a bridge railing, distressed. → Risk to life: police role. Calm communication; s. 136 may apply in a public place.
2. A deaf victim reports a theft. → Arrange a **BSL interpreter** for the statement.

## Common mistakes

- Talking to the carer instead of the person.
- Using "schizophrenic" or "bipolar" as adjectives for moods.
- Thinking s. 136 can be used inside someone's home.

## Summary

1. Disabled people / wheelchair user / has a condition.
2. Person with a mental health condition; died by suicide.
3. Speak to the person; adapt communication; BSL interpreter.
4. s. 136: public place, mental disorder + immediate need → place of safety.
5. s. 135: warrant for premises; Right Care, Right Person.
