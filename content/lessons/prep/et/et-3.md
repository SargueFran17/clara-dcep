## Objectives

- Know the **10 Standards of Professional Behaviour** in the Police (Conduct) Regulations 2020.
- Understand the difference between **practice requiring improvement**, **misconduct** and **gross misconduct**.
- Apply the standards to everyday situations, including **off duty** and **social media**.

## Explanation

### The 10 standards (Schedule 2, Police (Conduct) Regulations 2020)

1. **Honesty and integrity** – be honest, act with integrity, don't abuse your position.
2. **Authority, respect and courtesy** – act with self-control and tolerance; treat people with respect and courtesy; don't abuse powers.
3. **Equality and diversity** – act with fairness and impartiality; don't discriminate unlawfully or unfairly.
4. **Use of force** – only use force to the extent that it is **necessary, proportionate and reasonable** in all the circumstances.
5. **Orders and instructions** – give and carry out **lawful** orders and instructions.
6. **Duties and responsibilities** – be diligent in your duties and responsibilities.
7. **Confidentiality** – treat information with respect; access and disclose it only for a proper policing purpose.
8. **Fitness for work** – when on duty or presenting for duty, be fit to carry out your responsibilities (e.g. not under the influence of alcohol or drugs).
9. **Discreditable conduct** – don't behave in a way that **discredits the police service** or undermines public confidence, **on or off duty**. You must report any **charge, caution or conviction** you receive.
10. **Challenging and reporting improper conduct** – report, challenge or take action against the conduct of colleagues that has fallen below these standards.

These apply to **police officers** (there are equivalent expectations for staff). The **Code of Ethics** (ET2) guides behaviour; the **Standards** are the basis for **misconduct proceedings**.

:::es
**Las 10 normas** (Reglamento de Conducta 2020): 1) honestidad e integridad; 2) autoridad, respeto y cortesía; 3) igualdad y diversidad; 4) uso de la fuerza (necesaria, proporcionada, razonable); 5) órdenes legales; 6) diligencia; 7) confidencialidad; 8) aptitud para el servicio; 9) **conducta que desacredita** (de servicio y fuera) y obligación de comunicar acusaciones o condenas propias; 10) **cuestionar e informar** de la mala conducta de compañeros.
:::

### Levels of seriousness

- **Practice requiring improvement** – underperformance or conduct not amounting to misconduct → usually dealt with through **reflective practice** (the *Reflective Practice Review Process*).
- **Misconduct** – a breach of the Standards that justifies at least a **written warning**.
- **Gross misconduct** – a breach so serious that **dismissal** would be justified.

Complaints and conduct matters are handled by the force's **Professional Standards Department (PSD)**, with the **IOPC** involved in the most serious cases.

:::es
- **Práctica que requiere mejora** → proceso de **práctica reflexiva**.
- **Misconduct** (falta) → al menos **amonestación por escrito**.
- **Gross misconduct** (falta muy grave) → podría justificar el **despido**.
Lo gestiona el **PSD** de la fuerza; los casos más graves, con la **IOPC**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. You check the PNC record of your new neighbour out of curiosity. → Breach of **confidentiality** (no policing purpose) – potentially serious; also data protection offences may apply.
2. Off duty, you post an offensive joke on social media. → **Discreditable conduct** (and possibly equality and diversity).
3. A colleague uses excessive force on a compliant prisoner. → You must **challenge and report** it.

## Common mistakes & traps

- Thinking conduct **off duty** doesn't count.
- Thinking "just looking" at police systems without purpose is harmless.
- Forgetting the duty to **report your own** charges, cautions or convictions.

## Summary

1. 10 standards, Police (Conduct) Regulations 2020, Schedule 2.
2. Force: necessary, proportionate, reasonable.
3. Confidentiality: only for a policing purpose.
4. Discreditable conduct applies on and off duty.
5. Duty to challenge and report improper conduct; practice requiring improvement → misconduct → gross misconduct.
