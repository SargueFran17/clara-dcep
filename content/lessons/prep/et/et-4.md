## Objectives

- Name the **9 protected characteristics** of the Equality Act 2010.
- Understand **direct and indirect discrimination, harassment and victimisation**.
- Explain the **Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED)** and why it matters for police decisions.

## Explanation

### The 9 protected characteristics (s. 4)

1. **Age**
2. **Disability**
3. **Gender reassignment**
4. **Marriage and civil partnership**
5. **Pregnancy and maternity**
6. **Race** (including colour, nationality, ethnic or national origins)
7. **Religion or belief** (including lack of belief)
8. **Sex**
9. **Sexual orientation**

Memory trick: **"DR MARSGAPS"** → **D**isability, **R**ace, **M**arriage, **A**ge, **R**eligion, **S**ex, **G**ender reassignment, **A**nd **P**regnancy, **S**exual orientation.

:::es
**9 características protegidas**: edad, discapacidad, reasignación de género, matrimonio y pareja de hecho, embarazo y maternidad, raza (incluye color, nacionalidad, origen étnico o nacional), religión o creencias (incluida la falta de creencias), sexo y orientación sexual. Truco: **DR MARSGAPS**.
:::

### Types of prohibited conduct

- **Direct discrimination** (s. 13) – treating someone **less favourably because of** a protected characteristic. *Refusing to take a report seriously because the victim is gay.*
- **Indirect discrimination** (s. 19) – a **rule or practice applied to everyone** that puts people with a characteristic at a **particular disadvantage**, and it **can't be justified** as a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim.
- **Harassment** (s. 26) – unwanted conduct related to a characteristic that violates dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment.
- **Victimisation** (s. 27) – treating someone badly because they **made or supported a complaint** under the Act.
- **Disability**: also failure to make **reasonable adjustments** (s. 20) and discrimination arising from disability (s. 15).

:::es
- **Discriminación directa**: trato peor **por** la característica.
- **Indirecta**: una norma igual para todos que **perjudica** a un grupo y **no está justificada**.
- **Acoso**: conducta no deseada relacionada con la característica que atenta contra la dignidad.
- **Victimización**: represalias por **quejarse** o apoyar una queja.
- **Discapacidad**: obligación de **ajustes razonables**.
:::

### The Public Sector Equality Duty (s. 149)

Public authorities, including the **police**, must, in carrying out their functions, have **due regard** to the need to:

1. **eliminate** discrimination, harassment and victimisation;
2. **advance equality of opportunity**;
3. **foster good relations** between people who share a characteristic and those who don't.

In practice: think about how **policies and operations** (e.g. **stop and search**, **dispersal**, **communication with victims**) affect different groups, and record that thinking (e.g. equality impact assessments).

:::es
**PSED (s. 149)**: la policía debe tener **en cuenta** la necesidad de **eliminar** la discriminación, **promover la igualdad de oportunidades** y **fomentar buenas relaciones** entre grupos. Se aplica a políticas y operaciones (p. ej. *stop and search*).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. A deaf victim attends the front counter. → **Reasonable adjustments**: BSL interpreter, written communication.
2. An operation targets "young men in hoodies" in one area. → Consider the **PSED**: is it justified, based on evidence, and how will it affect relations with the community?

## Common mistakes & traps

- Thinking **nationality** is not protected – it's part of **race**.
- Forgetting **lack of belief** is protected under religion or belief.
- Thinking indirect discrimination needs **intention** – it doesn't.

## Summary

1. 9 protected characteristics (DR MARSGAPS).
2. Direct (because of) vs indirect (neutral rule, unjustified disadvantage).
3. Harassment and victimisation are also prohibited.
4. Reasonable adjustments for disability.
5. PSED: eliminate, advance, foster – "due regard".
