## Objectives

- Use **singular "they/them"** naturally when you don't know someone's gender or when it is their pronoun.
- **Ask respectfully** for a name, title and pronouns, and record them correctly.
- Know the **titles** (Mr, Mrs, Ms, Miss, Mx) and when to use the person's **name** instead.

## Explanation

### Singular "they"

In English, **they / them / their / themselves** can refer to **one person** when:

- you **don't know** their gender: *"The caller said **they** would wait outside."*
- gender is **not relevant**: *"Each officer must complete **their** notebook."*
- it is the **person's own pronoun**: *"Alex uses **they/them**. **They** are the witness."*

The verb stays **plural**: *they **are***, *they **were*** – even for one person. The reflexive is usually **themselves** (some people use *themself*).

:::es
En inglés **they/them/their** sirve también para **una sola persona**: cuando no sabes su género, cuando no importa, o porque es **su pronombre**. El verbo va en **plural**: *they **are***. En español no hay un equivalente directo, por eso conviene practicarlo hasta que salga solo.
:::

### Asking respectfully

You will often need a person's details for a report. Simple, neutral phrases:

- *"What's your full name?"* / *"What name would you like me to use?"*
- *"How would you like me to address you?"* (title: Mr, Ms, Mx…)
- *"What pronouns do you use?"* – ask in a natural, matter-of-fact way, the same for everybody, not only for people who "look" a certain way.
- If you make a mistake: **apologise briefly, correct yourself and move on** (*"Sorry – they said…"*). Don't make a big scene.

Use the **name** when in doubt: *"Sam said Sam's car…"* sounds odd, so mix the name and *they*.

:::es
Frases neutras: *"What name would you like me to use?"*, *"How would you like me to address you?"*, *"What pronouns do you use?"*. Pregúntalo **igual a todo el mundo**, con naturalidad. Si te equivocas: **perdón breve, corriges y sigues**. En la duda, usa el **nombre**.
:::

### Titles

| Title | Use |
|---|---|
| **Mr** | men |
| **Mrs** | married women (traditional) |
| **Miss** | unmarried women/girls (traditional) |
| **Ms** | women, without saying if married – the safest choice |
| **Mx** (pronounced "mix" or "mux") | gender-neutral title |

Use the title the person **chooses**. Never guess based on appearance or voice.

:::es
**Ms** es la opción neutra para mujeres (no indica estado civil). **Mx** es el título neutro de género. Usa siempre el que **elija la persona**.
:::

### Why it matters in policing

- Respect builds **trust and cooperation** – the basis of **policing by consent**.
- The **Equality Act 2010** protects **gender reassignment** (and other characteristics); the **Code of Ethics** and **Standards of Professional Behaviour** require respect and courtesy.
- There are specific rules for **searching** people and recording gender in custody (PACE Code C, **Annex L** – ⚠ VERIFICAR la versión vigente cuando llegues a la academia; sigue siempre la política de tu fuerza).

:::es
El respeto genera **confianza y cooperación** (policía por consentimiento). La **Equality Act 2010** protege la reasignación de género, y el Código Ético exige respeto y cortesía. Para registros y custodia hay normas específicas (PACE Code C, Annex L ⚠ verifica la versión vigente y la política de Hampshire).
:::

## Police scenarios

1. A witness gives the name **"Jordan Lee"** and you can't tell their gender. → *"Thanks, Jordan. And how would you like me to address you in my statement – Mr, Ms, Mx, or just your name?"* In the statement: *"Jordan LEE stated that **they** saw…"*.
2. A caller on 101 doesn't give a name. → In the log: *"The caller stated **they** had seen two males…"*.
3. You call a woman "sir" by mistake. → *"Sorry, madam."* – and continue normally.

:::es
1. Nombre ambiguo → pregunta cómo dirigirte y escribe *"they saw…"*.
2. Persona que llama sin dar nombre → *"the caller stated they…"*.
3. Error al tratar a alguien → disculpa breve y sigue.
:::

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *"He or she is the witness"* repeatedly → ✅ *"They are the witness."*
- ❌ *"They is…"* → ✅ *"They **are**…"*
- ❌ Assuming gender from voice on the phone.
- ❌ Asking about pronouns only to some people → ask everyone in the same way, or use names.

## Summary

1. Singular *they* = one person, plural verb.
2. Ask: name, how to address, pronouns – same way for everyone.
3. Titles: Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms, Mx – the person chooses.
4. Mistake? Apologise briefly, correct, continue.
5. Respect = trust = policing by consent (Equality Act, Code of Ethics).
