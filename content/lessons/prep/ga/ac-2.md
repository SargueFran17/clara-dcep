## Objectives

- Learn the **second block of acronyms**: safeguarding, systems, roles and major incidents.
- Connect each acronym to **when you will meet it** as a detective.
- Pass the **Test Acrónimos II** with at least 80 %.

## Explanation

The second block (positions 41–90 in `content/acronyms.json`) groups acronyms you'll meet once you start working cases. Learn them by **theme**:

- **Safeguarding & vulnerability**: MASH · LADO · CSE · CCE · VAWG · FGM · HBA · MSHT · NRM · ISVA · IDVA · SARC · DAPO / DVPO · VPS · VRR
- **Custody**: AA · HCP · DP · TIC
- **Systems & databases**: NDNAD · IDENT1 · VIPER · HOLMES · MOPI · DPA · IPA
- **Roles & units**: NCA · CSI / SOCO · POLSA · FLO · AFO · ARV · NPT · SPOC · DS / DI / DCI
- **Disposals & ASB**: PND (penalty notice) · FPN · PCN · ASB · CPN
- **Major incidents & problem-solving**: JESIP · METHANE · IIMARCH · SARA
- **Crime groups**: OCG

:::es
El segundo bloque está agrupado por **temas**: protección de personas vulnerables, custodia, sistemas, roles y unidades, sanciones y ASB, incidentes graves y resolución de problemas, crimen organizado.
:::

### Watch out: same letters, different meanings

- **PND** = *Police National Database* **and** *Penalty Notice for Disorder*. Context tells you which.
- **CPS** vs **CPN**: prosecutor vs anti-social behaviour notice.
- **IDVA** (domestic violence) vs **ISVA** (sexual violence).

:::es
Cuidado con las **siglas iguales**: **PND** puede ser la base de datos nacional o la multa por desorden; **IDVA** (violencia doméstica) vs **ISVA** (violencia sexual).
:::

## Police scenarios

> *"Refer the family to the **MASH**; the victim has an **IDVA**; there's a **DAPO** application pending. Check **NDNAD** for the DNA hit and ask the **FLO** to update the family."*

## Common mistakes

- Learning acronyms only as letters, without the **meaning in plain English**.
- Confusing **CSE** (sexual exploitation) and **CCE** (criminal exploitation, e.g. county lines).

## Summary

1. Learn by theme, not alphabetically.
2. Safeguarding acronyms are the most used in CID.
3. Same letters can mean different things (PND).
4. Practise with flashcards daily.
5. Test: "Test Acrónimos II".
