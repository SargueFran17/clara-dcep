## Objectives

- Explain **policing by consent** and why it is the foundation of British policing.
- Know the main ideas of the **Peelian principles** (1829).
- Compare it with the **Spanish model** you know.

## Explanation

### Policing by consent

In Britain, the police's power comes from the **approval, trust and cooperation of the public** – not from fear or force. The police are seen as **citizens in uniform**. This is called **policing by consent**. It explains a lot of what you will see: officers explaining their actions, using persuasion first, being mostly **unarmed**, and being **accountable** to the public (e.g. through **Police and Crime Commissioners** and the **IOPC**).

:::es
**Policing by consent** = la policía tiene poder porque el **público lo aprueba y coopera**, no por miedo. Los agentes son "**ciudadanos de uniforme**". Por eso verás: explicar siempre lo que haces, persuadir antes que usar la fuerza, policía mayoritariamente **sin armas de fuego** y **rendición de cuentas** (comisionados electos, IOPC).
:::

### The Peelian principles

Sir **Robert Peel** created the **Metropolitan Police** in **1829**. The "Peelian principles" (written down later, but linked to that time) include ideas such as:

1. The basic mission of the police is to **prevent crime and disorder**.
2. The police's ability to do their job depends on **public approval**.
3. Securing public cooperation **reduces the need for physical force**.
4. The police should show **impartial service to the law**, not follow public opinion.
5. Use **force only when persuasion, advice and warning are not enough**, and only the minimum necessary.
6. *"The police are the public and the public are the police"* – officers are paid to do full-time what every citizen should do.
7. The test of police efficiency is the **absence of crime and disorder**, not visible police action.

:::es
**Robert Peel** creó la **Policía Metropolitana en 1829**. Ideas clave: **prevenir** el delito; depender de la **aprobación pública**; cooperación = **menos fuerza**; **imparcialidad**; fuerza **solo** cuando falla la persuasión, y la **mínima**; *"la policía es el público y el público es la policía"*; el éxito se mide por la **ausencia de delito**, no por las detenciones.
:::

### Spain vs UK (big picture)

| Spain | England & Wales |
|---|---|
| Several national bodies (Policía Nacional, Guardia Civil – military nature), regional and local police | **43 territorial forces** + national bodies (e.g. NCA, British Transport Police) |
| Officers routinely carry firearms | Most officers are **unarmed**; specialist **firearms officers** |
| National ID card (DNI) widely used | **No** compulsory national ID card |
| Investigating judge (*juez de instrucción*) leads the investigation | **Police investigate**; the **CPS** decides on charge in most cases |

:::es
En España hay varios cuerpos (Policía Nacional, Guardia Civil de naturaleza militar, autonómicas y locales) y el **juez de instrucción** dirige la investigación. En Inglaterra y Gales hay **43 fuerzas territoriales**, la **policía investiga** y la **CPS** (fiscalía) decide la acusación en la mayoría de casos.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. Officers attend a noisy party. Instead of ordering everyone out, they **explain** the complaint, **ask** the host to turn the music down and leave. → Persuasion first (Peel).
2. A member of the public films you during a stop. → Filming in public is generally lawful; stay professional and explain what you are doing. → Accountability and trust.

:::es
1. Fiesta ruidosa → **explicar y pedir** antes de imponer.
2. Te graban en la calle → normalmente es legal; sé profesional y **explica** lo que haces.
:::

## Common mistakes & traps

- Thinking "consent" means **each individual** must agree to be arrested – it means the **public in general** supports the police.
- Thinking unarmed = without any equipment: officers carry **handcuffs, baton, PAVA spray**, and many carry **Taser**.

## Summary

1. Police power comes from public approval and cooperation.
2. Peel, Metropolitan Police, 1829.
3. Prevention, impartiality, minimum force, public cooperation.
4. "The police are the public and the public are the police."
5. Success = absence of crime, not number of arrests.
