## Objectives

- Understand **British politeness**: indirect requests, softeners and "sorry".
- Decode what British people **really mean** (understatement).
- Adjust your **tone** as an officer: polite but clear.

## Explanation

### Indirect requests

Spanish is often more **direct**: *"Dame tu nombre."* In British English, direct imperatives can sound **rude**. Use softeners:

- *"Could you give me your name, please?"*
- *"Would you mind stepping over here for a moment?"*
- *"I'm going to need you to…"* (polite but firm – common in policing)
- *"Can I just ask…?"*

When you **must** give an instruction (safety), be **clear and direct**, then explain: *"Put the knife down. **Now.**"* → later: *"Thank you. I needed you to do that because…"*

:::es
En español somos más **directos**; en inglés británico una orden directa puede sonar **grosera**. Suaviza: *Could you…?*, *Would you mind…?*, *I'm going to need you to…*. Pero en situaciones de **seguridad**: orden **clara y directa**, y luego explicas.
:::

### "Sorry" – many meanings

- **Apology**: *"Sorry, that was my mistake."*
- **"Pardon?"**: *"Sorry?"* (= I didn't hear)
- **Getting attention**: *"Sorry, excuse me…"*
- **Sympathy**: *"I'm sorry for your loss."* (to bereaved families)
- **Softener before bad news/disagreement**: *"Sorry, but I can't let you go in."*

:::es
**Sorry** sirve para disculparse, para "¿perdón?/¿cómo?", para llamar la atención, para dar el pésame (*I'm sorry for your loss*) y para suavizar un "no".
:::

### Understatement – what they say vs what they mean

| They say | They often mean |
|---|---|
| *"Not bad."* | Good / very good |
| *"I'll bear it in mind."* | I probably won't do it |
| *"With the greatest respect…"* | I think you're wrong |
| *"That's an interesting idea."* | I disagree |
| *"I'm a bit upset."* | I'm very upset |
| *"Are you alright?"* | Hello / how are you (not necessarily worried) |

For victims, "**a bit** shaken" may mean **very** distressed – check gently.

:::es
**Atenuación británica**: *not bad* = muy bien; *I'll bear it in mind* = probablemente no; *with the greatest respect* = creo que te equivocas; *a bit upset* = muy disgustado. Con víctimas, "un poco" puede ser "mucho": pregunta con tacto.
:::

### Informal address

You'll hear **mate**, **love**, **pet**, **duck** (regional), **cheers** (thanks/bye). As an officer, use **neutral** forms: *"Sir"*, *"Madam"*, the person's **name**, or nothing. Avoid "love"/"darling" – it can be seen as unprofessional.

:::es
Oirás **mate, love, cheers**. Como agente, usa formas **neutras**: *Sir/Madam*, el **nombre** de la persona. Evita *love/darling*.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. You need a man to move away from a scene. → *"Sir, could you step back behind the tape for me, please? Thank you."*
2. A victim says *"I'm alright, just a bit shaken."* → *"That's completely understandable. Would you like to sit down? Is there anyone I can call for you?"*

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- Sounding rude by using bare imperatives (*"Give me your ID."*).
- Taking *"not bad"* literally.
- Over-using *"please"* at the **end** of urgent safety commands – be clear first.

## Summary

1. Use indirect requests: Could you / Would you mind / I'm going to need you to.
2. Safety first: clear direct commands, then explain.
3. "Sorry" has many uses.
4. Understatement: listen for what people mean.
5. Neutral address: Sir/Madam/name – not "love".
