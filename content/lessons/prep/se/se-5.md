## Objectives

- Know the UK **emergency and non-emergency numbers** and when each is used.
- Convert quickly between **imperial and metric** units (miles, feet, stones, pints).
- Write **dates and times** the UK way.

## Explanation

### 999, 112 and 101

- **999** – emergencies: a crime **in progress**, someone in **immediate danger**, a serious **injury**. (**112** also works in the UK.)
- **101** – **non-emergency** police contact (reporting a crime that already happened with no danger, giving information).
- Deaf or speech-impaired people can register for **emergency SMS** (text 999 after registering) or use **Relay UK** (18000 for emergencies).
- **Silent Solution**: if a caller to 999 can't speak, they can press **55** when prompted to show it's a genuine emergency (⚠ VERIFICAR funcionamiento exacto).
- Online reporting is also common.

:::es
- **999** (o **112**): emergencias – delito en curso, peligro inmediato, heridos graves.
- **101**: policía **no urgente** (denunciar algo ya pasado sin peligro, dar información).
- Personas sordas: **SMS de emergencia** (registro previo) o **Relay UK (18000)**.
- **Silent Solution**: si no puedes hablar en una llamada al 999, pulsa **55** cuando te lo indiquen (⚠ verifica).
:::

### Units you will hear every day

| Imperial | Metric (approx.) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **1 mile** | 1.6 km | distances, **speed (mph)** |
| **1 foot (ft)** = 12 in | 30.5 cm | height of people, distances |
| **1 inch (in)** | 2.54 cm | height, knife blades |
| **1 yard (yd)** = 3 ft | 0.91 m | short distances, road signs |
| **1 stone (st)** = 14 lb | 6.35 kg | **body weight** ("10 stone 4") |
| **1 pound (lb)** | 0.45 kg | weight |
| **1 pint** (UK) | 568 ml | **beer, milk** |

Quick conversions: **mph × 1.6 = km/h** (30 mph ≈ 48 km/h; 70 mph ≈ 113 km/h). **kg ÷ 6.35 = stone** (70 kg ≈ 11 st). Drugs and science use **grams**; road signs use **miles and yards**.

:::es
**1 milla ≈ 1,6 km** (velocidades en **mph**), **1 pie ≈ 30 cm**, **1 pulgada = 2,54 cm**, **1 yarda ≈ 0,9 m**, **1 stone = 14 libras ≈ 6,35 kg** (peso corporal), **1 pinta ≈ 568 ml** (cerveza, leche). Atajo: **mph × 1,6 = km/h**. Las drogas se pesan en **gramos**.
:::

### Dates and times

- UK date order is **day/month/year** – same as Spain: **03/11/2026** = 3 November 2026. (US is month/day!)
- Written: *Tuesday 3 November 2026* or *3rd November 2026*.
- Police use the **24-hour clock** (CO1).
- **Clocks change**: forward 1 hour on the **last Sunday of March** (BST), back on the **last Sunday of October** (GMT). Times near the change can matter in investigations!

:::es
Fecha: **día/mes/año** igual que en España (EE. UU. es al revés). Reloj de **24 h**. **Cambio de hora**: último domingo de marzo (adelanta) y último domingo de octubre (atrasa) – ¡ojo con las horas en las investigaciones!
:::

## Police scenarios

1. A witness says the suspect was "about 6 foot and 13 stone". → ≈ 183 cm and ≈ 83 kg.
2. A caller rings 999 because their bike was stolen last week. → It's a **101** matter (or online report).
3. CCTV time stamp is 01:30 on the night the clocks went back. → Check whether it's **BST or GMT**.

## Common mistakes

- Giving speeds in km/h.
- Confusing **US** dates (11/03 = 3 November in US style) with UK dates.
- Forgetting that a UK **pint** is bigger than a US pint.

## Summary

1. 999/112 emergencies; 101 non-emergency; 18000 Relay UK.
2. Miles and mph; ×1.6 for km.
3. Height in feet/inches; weight in stones/pounds.
4. Dates dd/mm/yyyy; 24-hour clock.
5. Clock changes: last Sunday of March and October.
