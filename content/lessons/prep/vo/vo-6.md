## Objectives

- Rank the **mental thresholds** used in police powers: *suspect < believe < know*.
- Understand **"reasonable grounds"** as an **objective** test.
- Read a power and identify **exactly** which threshold it needs – a classic NIE trap.

## Explanation

### The ladder

| Level | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| lowest | **suspect** | think something **may** be true – a possibility based on some facts |
| higher | **believe** | think something **is** true – stronger, few doubts |
| highest | **know** | be aware of it as a fact |

Laws usually add **"reasonable grounds"**: *"reasonable grounds for **suspecting**"*, *"reasonable grounds for **believing**"*. That means:

1. the officer **actually** suspects/believes it (**subjective**), **and**
2. a **reasonable person** with the same information would also suspect/believe it (**objective**).

:::es
**Escalera**: **suspect** (sospechar: puede ser) < **believe** (creer: es así, casi sin dudas) < **know** (saber). **"Reasonable grounds"** = 1) tú lo sospechas/crees de verdad **y** 2) una **persona razonable** con la misma información también lo haría (parte **objetiva**).
:::

### Examples in PACE (general idea – check the exact wording)

- **Arrest without warrant** (s. 24): reasonable grounds for **suspecting** an offence has been/is being committed **and** that the person is guilty – plus **necessity** (Code G).
- **Stop and search** (s. 1): reasonable grounds for **suspecting** you'll find stolen or prohibited articles.
- **Entry to arrest** (s. 17): reasonable grounds for **believing** the person you're seeking is **on the premises**.
- **Search upon arrest** (s. 32): reasonable grounds for **believing** the person may have evidence/a weapon etc.

### What is NOT reasonable suspicion (Code A)

Reasonable suspicion **cannot** be based on **personal factors alone** – e.g. race, age, appearance, religion, a previous conviction, or **generalisations or stereotypes**. It must be based on **facts, information and/or intelligence** relevant to the likelihood of finding an article (⚠ VERIFICAR texto completo de Code A, para. 2.2).

:::es
**Code A**: la sospecha razonable **no puede** basarse **solo** en factores personales (raza, edad, apariencia, religión, antecedentes) ni en **estereotipos**. Debe basarse en **hechos, información o inteligencia**.
:::

## Police scenarios

1. Intelligence says a red Golf is used for drug supply; you see the car and smell cannabis. → Facts + information → **reasonable grounds for suspecting**.
2. You want to enter a house to arrest a suspect. A neighbour says "he went in 5 minutes ago and hasn't come out". → May give **reasonable grounds for believing** he's inside (higher threshold).
3. You stop a young man because "he looks like a dealer". → **Not** reasonable suspicion (appearance/stereotype).

## Common mistakes & exam traps

- An option says "reasonable grounds to **suspect**" when the power requires "**believe**" (or vice versa) → wrong option.
- Thinking a **hunch** is enough – there must be an objective basis.
- Translating *believe* as "think" (too weak) – in law it's strong.

## Summary

1. suspect < believe < know.
2. Reasonable grounds = genuine + objective.
3. Arrest & stop/search: suspecting; entry to arrest/s. 32: believing.
4. Code A: not personal factors or stereotypes alone.
5. Exam trap: suspect vs believe.
