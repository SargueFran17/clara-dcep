## Objectives

- Use the vocabulary of **victim care** and **safeguarding** correctly.
- Know the basics of the **Victims' Code** and **special measures**.
- Speak to victims and witnesses in a **supportive, professional** way.

## Explanation

### The Victims' Code (Code of Practice for Victims of Crime, 2020)

It gives victims **12 rights**, including (summarised): to **understand and be understood**; to have the crime **recorded** without unjustified delay; to be **provided with information** when reporting; to be **referred to support services**; to be **informed about the investigation and prosecution**; to make a **Victim Personal Statement (VPS)**; to be **given information about the trial**; to be **paid compensation** where applicable; to **make a complaint** (⚠ VERIFICAR lista exacta). Some victims get **enhanced rights** (e.g. victims of the most serious crime, **persistently targeted**, **vulnerable or intimidated**).

:::es
El **Victims' Code (2020)** da **12 derechos** a las víctimas: entender y ser entendidas, que se registre el delito, recibir información, ser derivadas a servicios de apoyo, estar informadas del caso, hacer una **VPS** (declaración de impacto), información del juicio, compensación, presentar quejas… Algunas víctimas tienen **derechos reforzados** (delitos más graves, víctimas reiteradas, vulnerables o intimidadas).
:::

### Vulnerable and intimidated witnesses – special measures

Under the **Youth Justice and Criminal Evidence Act 1999**, the court can allow **special measures** for **vulnerable** (e.g. **under 18**, mental disorder, learning disability, physical disability) and **intimidated** witnesses (fear or distress; **complainants in sexual offences** are eligible automatically): **screens**, **live link**, **video-recorded evidence-in-chief** (**ABE** interview), **removal of wigs and gowns**, **evidence in private**, **intermediaries**, **aids to communication**.

:::es
**Medidas especiales** (YJCEA 1999) para testigos **vulnerables** (menores de 18, trastorno mental, discapacidad) e **intimidados** (miedo/angustia; víctimas de delitos sexuales automáticamente): **mamparas**, **videoconferencia**, **declaración grabada (ABE)**, sin pelucas ni togas, sesión a puerta cerrada, **intermediarios**.
:::

### Safeguarding vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **safeguarding** | protecting people from harm, abuse and neglect |
| **at risk** / **vulnerable** | likely to be harmed or less able to protect themselves |
| **disclose** / **disclosure** (by a victim) | tell someone about abuse |
| **make a referral** | pass a concern to another agency (e.g. MASH, social care) |
| **signpost** | tell someone where to get help |
| **welfare check** | visit to make sure someone is safe |
| **risk assessment** (e.g. **DASH**) | structured questions to assess risk |
| **safety plan** | steps to keep the victim safe |

:::es
**safeguarding** = protección; **at risk** = en riesgo; **disclose** = revelar (abuso); **make a referral** = derivar; **signpost** = orientar hacia recursos; **welfare check** = visita de comprobación; **safety plan** = plan de seguridad.
:::

## Police scenarios

> *The victim **disclosed** ongoing **coercive control**. I completed a **DASH risk assessment** (high risk), **made a referral** to MARAC and the **IDVA** service, explained her rights under the **Victims' Code**, and **signposted** her to local support. She was eligible for **special measures**.*

### Phrases for talking to victims

- *"You're safe now. Take your time."*
- *"I'm going to explain what happens next."*
- *"Is there anything you need right now?"*
- *"Thank you for telling me."* (after a disclosure – avoid "why didn't you report it before?")

## Common mistakes

- Saying *"the injured part"* (Spanish *parte perjudicada*) → ✅ *the victim* / *the injured party* (formal) / *the IP*.
- Asking *"Why didn't you leave?"* to a domestic abuse victim – it sounds like **blame**.
- Promising outcomes you can't guarantee (*"He'll go to prison"*).

## Summary

1. Victims' Code 2020: 12 rights; enhanced rights for some.
2. VPS = how the crime affected the victim.
3. Special measures (YJCEA 1999) for vulnerable/intimidated witnesses.
4. Safeguarding: disclose, referral, signpost, risk assessment, safety plan.
5. Supportive language; no blame; no false promises.
