## Objectives

- Use court vocabulary correctly: **plea, trial, verdict, sentence, acquittal, conviction**.
- Know the people in a criminal court and what they do.
- Prepare to **give evidence** as a police witness.

## Explanation

### From charge to sentence (the vocabulary path)

1. The suspect is **charged** and becomes the **defendant**.
2. **First appearance** at the **magistrates' court** (always). For either-way offences there is **allocation** (which court).
3. The defendant enters a **plea**: **guilty** or **not guilty**.
4. If not guilty → a **trial**. Witnesses **give evidence**: **examination-in-chief** (by the side that called them), **cross-examination** (by the other side), **re-examination**.
5. **Verdict**: **guilty** (→ **conviction**) or **not guilty** (→ **acquittal**).
6. If convicted → **sentence** (e.g. **fine**, **community order**, **suspended sentence**, **custodial sentence**/**imprisonment**). Sentencing follows the **Sentencing Council guidelines**.
7. **Appeal** against conviction and/or sentence.

:::es
Recorrido: **charge** (acusación) → **defendant** (acusado) → primera comparecencia en el **magistrates' court** → **plea** (declaración de culpable/no culpable) → **trial** (juicio): testigos con **examination-in-chief**, **cross-examination**, **re-examination** → **verdict** (veredicto) → **conviction** (condena) o **acquittal** (absolución) → **sentence** (pena: multa, trabajos comunitarios, pena suspendida, prisión) → **appeal** (recurso).
:::

### People

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **judge** (Crown Court) | runs the trial, decides law and sentence |
| **magistrates / district judge** | decide magistrates' court cases |
| **jury** | decides guilty/not guilty in the Crown Court |
| **prosecutor** (CPS lawyer, **barrister** or **solicitor**) | presents the case against the defendant |
| **defence** (solicitor/barrister) | represents the defendant |
| **legal adviser** (clerk) | advises magistrates on law |
| **usher** | calls witnesses, administers the oath |
| **witness** | gives evidence |

### Giving evidence

You will **swear an oath** or **affirm** (promise without religion), give evidence from your **statement** and you may be allowed to **refresh your memory** from your **pocket notebook** if it was made at the time (⚠ VERIFICAR el criterio exacto). Address the judge as **"Your Honour"** (Crown Court; some judges are "My Lord/My Lady"), and magistrates as **"Sir/Madam"** or **"Your Worships"** (⚠ VERIFICAR la forma actual).

:::es
Como testigo **juras** (oath) o **prometes** (affirm), declaras sobre tu **statement** y puedes **refrescar memoria** con tu libreta si la escribiste en el momento (⚠ verificar). Tratamiento: **"Your Honour"** al juez en la Crown Court; a los magistrados **"Sir/Madam"** (⚠ verifica la forma actual).
:::

## Police scenarios

> *The defendant **pleaded not guilty** to robbery. At **trial**, I **gave evidence** about the arrest and was **cross-examined** about the timing in my notebook. The jury **returned a guilty verdict** and the judge **adjourned sentence** for a pre-sentence report. He was later **sentenced to** three years' **imprisonment**.*

:::es
Colocaciones: **plead (not) guilty**, **give evidence**, **be cross-examined**, **return a verdict**, **adjourn sentence**, **be sentenced to**.
:::

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *He was condemned to 3 years* → ✅ *He was **sentenced to** 3 years*.
- ❌ *He was absolved* → ✅ *He was **acquitted***.
- ❌ *The sentence* (= the verdict) → in English **sentence** = **punishment**; the decision guilty/not guilty is the **verdict**.
- ❌ *declare as witness* → ✅ *give evidence*.

## Summary

1. Charge → plea → trial → verdict → sentence → appeal.
2. Conviction (guilty) vs acquittal (not guilty).
3. Sentence = punishment, not verdict.
4. Examination-in-chief, cross-examination, re-examination.
5. Oath or affirmation; "Your Honour".
