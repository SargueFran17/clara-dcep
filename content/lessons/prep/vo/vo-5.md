## Objectives

- Name common **offences** in English and use the right **verbs** with them.
- Describe **offenders** and their history: *previous convictions, prolific, repeat*.
- Avoid Spanish-influenced mistakes (*rob* vs *steal*, *crime* vs *offence*).

## Explanation

### Offence vs crime

- **offence** – the legal word: *the offence of theft*, *a summary offence*.
- **crime** – general word: *crime rates*, *a crime scene*, *serious crime*. Both are often used, but in law and charges use **offence**.
- **commit** an offence/crime; **be charged with**, **be convicted of**, **plead guilty to**.

:::es
**offence** = término legal (delito/infracción); **crime** = más general. Verbos: **commit** (cometer), **be charged with** (ser acusado de), **be convicted of** (ser condenado por), **plead guilty to** (declararse culpable de).
:::

### Common offences and verbs

| Offence | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **theft** | **steal** (something) | *He **stole** a phone.* |
| **robbery** | **rob** (a person/place) | *She was **robbed**. They **robbed** a bank.* |
| **burglary** | **burgle** / **break into** | *The house was **burgled**. They **broke into** the shed.* |
| **shoplifting** | **shoplift** | *She was caught **shoplifting**.* |
| **criminal damage** | **damage**, **smash**, **vandalise** | *He **smashed** the window.* |
| **assault** | **assault**, **attack**, **punch**, **kick** | *He **assaulted** a door supervisor.* |
| **fraud** | **defraud**, **scam** | *Elderly victims were **defrauded**.* |
| **drug offences** | **possess**, **supply**, **deal** | *possession with intent to supply* |
| **harassment** | **harass** | *He **harassed** his ex-partner.* |
| **drink driving** | **drive over the limit** | *She was **over the limit**.* |

**steal vs rob:** you **steal** a *thing*; you **rob** a *person or place*. ❌ *He robbed my phone* → ✅ *He **stole** my phone* / *He **robbed me***.

:::es
**steal** + cosa (*stole a phone*); **rob** + persona o lugar (*robbed me / a bank*). **burgle / break into** = robar en una casa/edificio entrando. **smash** = romper a golpes.
:::

### Offenders

- **suspect** → **defendant** (in court) → **offender** (after conviction).
- **accomplice**, **co-accused**, **gang**, **organised crime group (OCG)**.
- **previous convictions** ("**previous**" or "**pre-cons**" informally), **criminal record**.
- **first-time offender** vs **repeat offender** / **prolific offender**.
- **modus operandi (MO)** – the way they commit offences.
- **on bail**, **on licence** (released from prison under supervision), **on probation**.

:::es
**suspect** (sospechoso) → **defendant** (acusado) → **offender** (condenado). **previous convictions** = antecedentes; **prolific offender** = delincuente reincidente muy activo; **on licence** = en libertad condicional tras prisión; **MO** = modus operandi.
:::

## Police scenarios

> *The suspect, a **prolific offender** with 23 **previous convictions** for **theft** and **burglary**, was **on licence** at the time. His **MO** was to **break into** sheds and **steal** bicycles. He was **charged with** three counts of burglary.*

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *He robbed a bike* → ✅ *He **stole** a bike*.
- ❌ *He was accused of* (formal charge) → ✅ *He was **charged with***.
- ❌ *antecedents* → ✅ *previous convictions / criminal record*.
- ❌ *delinquent* (for adults) → ✅ *offender* (*delinquent* is old-fashioned, mostly for youths).

## Summary

1. Offence (legal) vs crime (general).
2. Commit; be charged with; be convicted of.
3. Steal + thing; rob + person/place.
4. Suspect → defendant → offender.
5. Previous convictions, prolific offender, on licence, MO.
