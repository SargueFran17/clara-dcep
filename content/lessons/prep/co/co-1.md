## Objectives

- Spell names, registrations and postcodes using the **phonetic alphabet** without thinking.
- Say and write times using the **24-hour clock** as UK police do.
- Read **numbers** clearly over the radio.

## Explanation

### The phonetic alphabet (NATO / ICAO)

| | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A **Alpha** | B **Bravo** | C **Charlie** | D **Delta** | E **Echo** |
| F **Foxtrot** | G **Golf** | H **Hotel** | I **India** | J **Juliet** |
| K **Kilo** | L **Lima** | M **Mike** | N **November** | O **Oscar** |
| P **Papa** | Q **Quebec** | R **Romeo** | S **Sierra** | T **Tango** |
| U **Uniform** | V **Victor** | W **Whiskey** | X **X-ray** | Y **Yankee** |
| Z **Zulu** | | | | |

Use it for **surnames, vehicle registrations, postcodes and email addresses**: *"Surname GARCÍA: Golf, Alpha, Romeo, Charlie, India, Alpha."* For accents, say *"with an accent on the India"* or just spell without it (as the system records).

:::es
Úsalo para **apellidos, matrículas, códigos postales y correos**. Tu apellido o el de un testigo: letra a letra con la palabra. Las tildes normalmente no se registran (o dices "with an accent on…").
:::

### Numbers over the radio

- Say numbers **digit by digit**: 457 → *"four, five, seven"*.
- **0** = *"zero"* (on the radio; in everyday UK speech people also say "oh").
- **9** is often said *"niner"* in some services to avoid confusion – follow your force practice.
- Registrations: *"HN68 KXT"* → *"Hotel November six eight, Kilo X-ray Tango."*

:::es
Números **cifra a cifra**: 457 = "four, five, seven". **0 = zero**. Matrícula *HN68 KXT* = "Hotel November six eight, Kilo X-ray Tango".
:::

### 24-hour clock

| Time | Written | Spoken (common police style) |
|---|---|---|
| 9:00 am | 09:00 | "zero nine hundred" / "oh-nine-hundred hours" |
| 2:30 pm | 14:30 | "fourteen thirty" |
| 6:05 pm | 18:05 | "eighteen oh five" |
| 11:45 pm | 23:45 | "twenty-three forty-five" |
| midnight | 00:00 | "midnight" / "zero hundred hours" (avoid 24:00 in reports) |

In **pocket notebooks and statements**, use the 24-hour clock (often without a colon: **1430**, depending on force style).

:::es
En la policía se usa el **reloj de 24 horas**: 14:30 = "fourteen thirty". En libreta y declaraciones, siempre 24 h (algunas fuerzas sin dos puntos: **1430**). Medianoche: **00:00** (evita 24:00).
:::

## Police scenarios

> "Control from 2-1-4, can you run a PNC check on a vehicle registration: **Whiskey Romeo one nine, Alpha Bravo Charlie**. Silver Vauxhall Corsa, stationary at the junction of Elm Road and High Street, **fourteen thirty-two**."

:::es
Observa: tu **indicativo** (call sign) cifra a cifra, la matrícula en fonético, el vehículo y la hora en 24 h.
:::

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- Mixing Spanish letter names (*"jota"*, *"i griega"*) under pressure – practise until it's automatic.
- Saying *"Juliet"* as "Hulieta" – it's **"JOO-lee-et"**.
- *"Fourteen and thirty"* ❌ → ✅ *"fourteen thirty"*.
- Writing *2:30 pm* in a statement ❌ → ✅ *14:30*.

## Summary

1. Phonetic alphabet for names, regs, postcodes, emails.
2. Numbers digit by digit; 0 = zero.
3. 24-hour clock in all police records.
4. Spoken: "fourteen thirty", "zero nine hundred".
5. Midnight = 00:00.
