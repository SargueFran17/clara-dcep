## Objectives

- Report what someone said using **reported speech** (tense backshift, pronouns, time words).
- Choose precise **reporting verbs**: *stated, explained, admitted, denied, claimed, alleged*.
- Know when to use **direct speech** instead (suspects' significant statements).

## Explanation

### Basic changes

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| "I **am** scared." | She said (that) she **was** scared. |
| "He **hit** me." | She said he **had hit** her. |
| "I **have lost** my keys." | He said he **had lost** his keys. |
| "I **will** call you." | He said he **would** call me. |
| "I **can't** remember." | She said she **couldn't** remember. |
| "I **saw** him **yesterday**." | She said she **had seen** him **the day before / the previous day**. |
| "**here**", "**now**", "**today**", "**tomorrow**" | "**there**", "**then**", "**that day**", "**the next/following day**" |

**say** vs **tell**: *say* (something) / *tell* **someone** (something). ✅ *She **told me** that…* / *She **said** that…* ❌ *She said me…*

:::es
Al pasar a estilo indirecto el verbo "da un paso atrás": *am → was*, *hit → had hit*, *will → would*, *can't → couldn't*. Cambian también pronombres y expresiones de tiempo/lugar (*yesterday → the day before*, *here → there*). **say** sin persona, **tell** + persona: *She **told me**…* (nunca *said me*).
:::

### Questions and orders

- *"Where were you?"* → *I **asked** him **where he had been**.* (no question order)
- *"Did you see him?"* → *I asked her **if/whether** she had seen him.*
- *"Get out of the car!"* → *I **told** him **to get out** of the car.*

### Reporting verbs for police writing

| Verb | Use |
|---|---|
| **stated** | neutral, formal (*The victim stated that…*) |
| **explained** | gave reasons |
| **admitted** | accepted something negative (*He admitted taking the bottle*) |
| **denied** | said it was not true (*She denied hitting him*) |
| **claimed / alleged** | said something that isn't proven (*He claimed he had found it*) |
| **confirmed**, **agreed**, **refused**, **warned** | as their meaning |

:::es
Verbos útiles: **stated** (declaró – neutro), **explained**, **admitted** (reconoció), **denied** (negó), **claimed / alleged** (afirmó – sin probar), **refused** (se negó). Ojo: *admit/deny* + **-ing**: *He admitted **taking** it.*
:::

### When NOT to use reported speech

**Significant statements, unsolicited comments and replies after caution** must be written **word for word** in **direct speech** with quotation marks (CO3). Reported speech is fine for **summaries** of what a witness told you.

:::es
Las **manifestaciones relevantes** y **respuestas tras la caution** van **literales** y entre comillas (estilo directo). El indirecto es para **resumir** lo que te contó un testigo.
:::

## Police scenarios

> *The victim **stated** that she **had been** at home when her ex-partner **had arrived** at about 21:00. She **said** that he **had kicked** the door and **told** her that he **would** "sort her out". She **told me** she **was** frightened. When I **asked** him what **had happened**, he **denied** kicking the door and **claimed** that he **had only knocked**.*

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *She said me that…* → ✅ *She **told me** that…*
- ❌ *I asked him where was he* → ✅ *where **he was***.
- ❌ *He admitted to take* → ✅ *He admitted **taking***.
- Forgetting the backshift in written reports.

## Summary

1. Backshift: present → past, past → past perfect, will → would.
2. Change pronouns and time/place words.
3. say vs tell (+ person).
4. Questions: no inversion; if/whether; orders: told him to…
5. Suspects' significant words: direct speech, word for word.
