## Objectives

- Use the **past perfect** (*had + past participle*) to show an action that happened **before** another past action.
- Order events clearly with **sequencing words**: *first, then, after that, before, after, by the time*.
- Avoid confusion in statements when events are told **out of order**.

## Explanation

### Past perfect

Form: **had + past participle** (*had gone, had seen, had left, had broken*).

Use it when you are already talking about the past and need to go **further back**:

> *When I **arrived**, the suspect **had** already **left**.* (first he left, then I arrived)
> *The victim told me that someone **had broken** into her car during the night.*

Compare:
- *When I arrived, the suspect **left**.* → he left **after** I arrived.
- *When I arrived, the suspect **had left**.* → he left **before** I arrived.

:::es
**Past perfect** = *had + participio*: algo que pasó **antes** de otra acción pasada (el "pluscuamperfecto": *había salido*).
- *When I arrived, the suspect **had left*** = cuando llegué, ya **se había ido**.
- *When I arrived, the suspect **left*** = cuando llegué, **se fue** (después).
¡Cambia totalmente el significado en una declaración!
:::

### Sequencing words

- **First / Then / Next / After that / Finally**.
- **Before** + -ing / clause: *Before leaving, he took the keys.*
- **After** + -ing / clause: *After checking the house, I spoke to the neighbour.*
- **By the time** + past simple → past perfect: ***By the time** the police **arrived**, the fire **had spread**.*
- **Already / just / never** often go with the past perfect: *He **had just** left.*

:::es
Conectores: **first, then, next, after that, finally**; **before/after + -ing**; **by the time** (para cuando) + pasado simple → past perfect.
:::

## Police scenarios

> *I **arrived** at 08:10. The owner **told** me that she **had locked** the shop at 18:00 the previous evening and that, when she **returned** that morning, someone **had forced** the rear door. By the time I **arrived**, she **had** already **touched** the door handle, so I **advised** her not to touch anything else.*

:::es
Observa: *had locked*, *had forced*, *had touched* = acciones **anteriores** a la llegada y al relato.
:::

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- Using **past simple** for everything and confusing the order of events.
- ❌ *When I arrived, he has gone* → ✅ *he **had gone***.
- ❌ *had went* → ✅ *had **gone*** (use the past participle).
- Overusing the past perfect when the order is already clear (*After he left, I called*… is fine).

## Writing exercise ✍️

A victim tells you: "I went to bed at 23:00. At 06:00 I woke up and my car was not outside. My son used it last night but he says he parked it." Write 3 sentences in **reported style** using past perfect.

**Self-check:**
- [ ] *She stated that she **had gone** to bed at 23:00.*
- [ ] Earlier actions → *had + participle*.
- [ ] Times and order are clear.

## Summary

1. Past perfect = had + past participle.
2. An earlier past (before another past action).
3. *When I arrived he had left* ≠ *when I arrived he left*.
4. *By the time* + past simple → past perfect.
5. Use past participles: gone, seen, taken, broken.
