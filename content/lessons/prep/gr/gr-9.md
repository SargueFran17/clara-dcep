## Objectives

- Learn **15 more phrasal verbs** common in investigations and court.
- Understand phrasal verbs with **several meanings** depending on context.
- Use them naturally in conversation with colleagues and witnesses.

## Explanation

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **track down** | find after searching | *We **tracked down** the suspect in Southampton.* |
| **tip off** | give secret information/warning | *Someone **tipped off** the police.* / *a **tip-off*** |
| **turn in** | take someone/something to the police | *His mother **turned him in**.* |
| **hold up** | rob with a weapon; delay | *They **held up** a petrol station.* / *I was **held up** in traffic.* |
| **go through** | search carefully; examine | *I **went through** his phone with consent.* |
| **go off** | (alarm) start ringing; (gun/bomb) fire/explode | *The alarm **went off** at 03:10.* |
| **let off** | not punish / give only a warning | *He was **let off** with a warning.* |
| **make up** | invent (a story) | *She **made up** an alibi.* |
| **cover up** | hide the truth | *He tried to **cover up** the assault.* |
| **own up** | admit (informal) | *He **owned up** to taking the money.* |
| **find out** | discover information | *We need to **find out** who was there.* |
| **come across** | find by chance | *I **came across** a knife in the hedge.* |
| **rule out** | exclude | *CCTV **ruled him out**.* |
| **point out** | show / mention | *The witness **pointed out** the car.* |
| **put up with** | tolerate | *She had **put up with** the abuse for years.* |

:::es
**track down** = localizar; **tip off** = dar el chivatazo; **turn in** = entregar a la policía; **hold up** = atracar / retrasar; **go through** = revisar a fondo; **go off** = sonar (alarma) / dispararse; **let off** = dejar ir (con advertencia); **make up** = inventar; **cover up** = encubrir; **own up** = confesar; **find out** = averiguar; **come across** = encontrar por casualidad; **rule out** = descartar; **point out** = señalar; **put up with** = aguantar.
:::

### One verb, several meanings – use context

- **hold up**: *They held up a bank* (robbed) vs *Roadworks held me up* (delayed).
- **go off**: *The alarm went off* (rang) vs *The milk has gone off* (gone bad).
- **pick up**: *I picked up the exhibit* vs *Officers picked him up in town* (arrested/collected) vs *ANPR picked up the car* (detected).

:::es
Contexto: **hold up** = atracar / retrasar; **go off** = sonar / estropearse (comida); **pick up** = recoger / detener / detectar (ANPR).
:::

## Police scenarios

> *An anonymous **tip-off** led us to the flat. We **went through** the bins and **came across** a bloodstained T-shirt. The suspect initially **made up** a story, but later **owned up**. His friend had tried to **cover up** the offence.*

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *We found out the knife* (by chance) → ✅ *We **came across** the knife* (*find out* = information).
- ❌ *turn in* = *entregarse* only – also *hand over someone else*.
- Mixing *put up with* (tolerate) and *put up* (accommodate/build).

## Summary

1. track down, tip off, turn in, hold up, go through.
2. go off, let off, make up, cover up, own up.
3. find out (information) vs come across (by chance).
4. rule out, point out, put up with.
5. Many phrasal verbs have several meanings – read the context.
