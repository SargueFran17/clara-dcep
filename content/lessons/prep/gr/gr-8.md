## Objectives

- Understand and use **15 phrasal verbs** you'll hear every day in policing.
- Know which are **separable** (*hand it over*) and which aren't (*break into the house*).
- Choose between the **phrasal verb** (spoken) and a **formal verb** (written reports).

## Explanation

### 15 essential phrasal verbs

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example | Formal alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **pull over** | stop a vehicle at the side of the road | *I signalled the driver to **pull over**.* | stop |
| **break into** | enter illegally (by force) | *They **broke into** the shed.* | force entry to |
| **break in** | enter illegally (no object) | *Someone **broke in** last night.* | – |
| **hand over** | give to someone (with authority) | *He **handed over** the knife.* | surrender |
| **turn up** | arrive / appear | *He **turned up** at 23:00.* | arrive |
| **run off** | escape on foot | *The suspect **ran off** towards the park.* | flee |
| **get away** | escape | *The robbers **got away** in a car.* | escape |
| **carry out** | do (a search, enquiries) | *I **carried out** a search.* | conduct |
| **look into** | investigate | *CID will **look into** it.* | investigate |
| **follow up** | take further action on | *I **followed up** the CCTV enquiry.* | pursue |
| **calm down** | become calm | *I asked him to **calm down**.* | – |
| **take down** | write down | *I **took down** her details.* | record |
| **set off** (an alarm) | activate | *The intruder **set off** the alarm.* | activate |
| **back up** | support (an officer); support (a story) | *Units arrived to **back me up**.* / *CCTV **backs up** her account.* | support / corroborate |
| **come forward** | offer information to police | *Two witnesses **came forward**.* | – |

:::es
**pull over** = parar en el arcén; **break into** = entrar a la fuerza (con objeto); **break in** = (sin objeto); **hand over** = entregar; **turn up** = aparecer; **run off / get away** = huir; **carry out** = realizar; **look into** = investigar; **follow up** = dar seguimiento; **calm down** = calmarse; **take down** = apuntar; **set off** = hacer saltar (alarma); **back up** = apoyar / corroborar; **come forward** = presentarse (testigos).
:::

### Separable or not?

- **Separable** (object can go in the middle): *hand **the knife** over* / *hand **it** over* (with pronouns it **must** go in the middle: ❌ *hand over it*). Also: *take down*, *set off*, *back up*, *calm (someone) down*, *pull (a car) over*.
- **Not separable**: *break into* the house, *look into* the case, *come forward*, *run off*, *turn up*.

:::es
**Separables**: *hand it over* (con pronombre, **siempre en medio**: nunca *hand over it*). **No separables**: *break into the house*, *look into the case*.
:::

### Spoken vs written

Phrasal verbs are normal in **speech and statements** (witnesses use them). In **formal reports** you can use either; formal verbs (*conduct, flee, surrender*) sound more official, but **clarity** is what matters.

## Police scenarios

> *I **pulled** the car **over** on London Road. The driver **turned up** his music and refused to **calm down**. I **carried out** a search under s. 23 Misuse of Drugs Act and he **handed over** a bag of white powder. Two passers-by **came forward** as witnesses and I **took down** their details.*

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *hand over it* → ✅ *hand it over*.
- ❌ *He broke the house* (= destroyed it) → ✅ *He broke **into** the house*.
- ❌ *turn up* vs *turn on/off* (lights) – different meanings.
- Translating literally: *"he escaped running"* → ✅ *he **ran off***.

## Summary

1. 15 key phrasal verbs for daily policing.
2. Separable verbs: pronoun in the middle (hand it over).
3. break into (object) / break in (no object).
4. Spoken and statements: phrasal verbs are natural.
5. Formal alternatives: conduct, flee, surrender, investigate.
