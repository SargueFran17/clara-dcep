---

## Objectives

- Use **at / on / in** with times, days, dates, months and years without hesitating.
- Write the **time and date** of an incident in a police report correctly.
- Avoid the most common Spanish-speaker mistakes ("in Monday", "at the morning").

## Explanation

Think of a pyramid: **at** = a point, **on** = a day, **in** = a longer period.

| Preposition | Use it with | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **AT** | exact times, short points | **at** 14:30 · **at** midnight · **at** noon · **at** the weekend (UK) · **at** night · **at** the time of the incident |
| **ON** | days and dates | **on** Monday · **on** 3 March · **on** Christmas Day · **on** Monday morning · **on** the night of the 4th |
| **IN** | months, years, seasons, parts of the day | **in** March · **in** 2026 · **in** summer · **in** the morning · **in** the afternoon · **in** the evening |

**No preposition** with: *this*, *next*, *last*, *every*, *yesterday*, *today*, *tomorrow* → *I saw him **last** Monday* (not *on last Monday*).

**The police order**: time → day → date (→ place). *At 14:30 **on** Monday 3 March 2026 **in** Southsea…*

:::es
- **AT** = un punto: horas (*at 14:30*), *at night*, *at the weekend* (en Reino Unido).
- **ON** = días y fechas: *on Monday*, *on 3 March*, *on Monday morning* (¡ojo! con un día concreto pasa a **on**).
- **IN** = periodos largos: meses, años, estaciones, *in the morning / afternoon / evening*.
- **Sin preposición** con *this, next, last, every, yesterday, today, tomorrow*.
- En informes: **hora → día → fecha → lugar**.
:::

### Special cases

- **in the morning** but **on Monday morning** (a specific day changes it to *on*).
- **at night** but **in the night** (= during a particular night) – both exist; in reports use **at** 02:15 or **during the night of**…
- **in time** (early enough) vs **on time** (punctual).
- **within** 24 hours (no later than) · **by** 17:00 (not later than) · **until** 17:00 (continuing up to).

:::es
- *in the morning* pero *on Monday morning*.
- **in time** = con tiempo suficiente; **on time** = puntual.
- **within 24 hours** = dentro de 24 h; **by 17:00** = como tarde a las 17:00; **until 17:00** = hasta las 17:00 (algo que dura).
:::

## Police scenarios

> **At** approximately 23:45 **on** Saturday 14 November 2026 I was on mobile patrol. **At** 23:50 I received a call to attend a disturbance. I arrived **at** 23:56. The victim stated that the incident started **at** about 23:30 and that she had seen the suspect earlier **in** the evening and **last** week.

:::es
Fíjate: **at** + hora, **on** + día/fecha, **in** the evening, **last** week (sin preposición).
:::

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- ❌ *in Monday* → ✅ *on Monday*
- ❌ *at the morning* → ✅ *in the morning*
- ❌ *in 3 March* → ✅ *on 3 March*
- ❌ *on last Friday* → ✅ *last Friday*
- ❌ *at 2026* → ✅ *in 2026*
- ❌ *during 10 minutes* → ✅ *for 10 minutes* (duration = **for**)

## Summary

1. AT = point in time (hours, night, weekend).
2. ON = days and dates (also "Monday morning").
3. IN = months, years, seasons, parts of the day.
4. No preposition with last/next/this/every.
5. Reports: at [time] on [day date] in [place].
