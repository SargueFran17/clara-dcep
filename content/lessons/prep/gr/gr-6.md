## Objectives

- Form the **passive** in different tenses (*is/was/has been/had been + participle*).
- Understand why laws and police texts use the passive so much.
- Decide **when to use active or passive** in statements and reports.

## Explanation

### Form

**be** (in the right tense) + **past participle**:

| Tense | Passive example |
|---|---|
| present simple | *Statements **are taken** by officers.* |
| past simple | *He **was arrested** at 14:40.* |
| present perfect | *The vehicle **has been seized**.* |
| past perfect | *The door **had been forced**.* |
| future / modal | *He **will be interviewed**. He **may be detained**.* |
| continuous | *The suspect **is being interviewed**.* |

Add the **agent** with **by** only if it's important: *He was arrested **by PC JONES**.*

:::es
**Pasiva = be (en el tiempo correspondiente) + participio.** *He **was arrested*** (fue detenido), *The door **had been forced*** (la puerta había sido forzada). El agente con **by** solo si importa.
:::

### Why police and legal English use it

- When the **doer is unknown**: *The car **was stolen** during the night.*
- When the **action/result** matters more than the doer: *The victim **was taken** to hospital.*
- In **laws and Codes**: *A person **may be detained**…*, *The detainee **must be told**…*

### Active is often better in statements

In your **own statement**, say clearly **who did what**: ✅ *I **arrested** him* is clearer than *He **was arrested***. Use the passive when the doer is unknown or obvious.

:::es
Úsala cuando **no se sabe quién** lo hizo (*the car was stolen*), cuando importa más el **resultado**, y la verás constantemente en **leyes y Códigos**. Pero en **tu declaración**, mejor activa: ***I** arrested him* deja claro quién hizo qué.
:::

### Useful structure: "have something done"

*She **had her bag stolen**.* (something happened to her) · *I **had the exhibit photographed**.* (I arranged it)

## Police scenarios

> *On arrival I found that the rear door **had been forced** and a TV **had been taken**. The scene **was examined** by a CSI and fingerprints **were recovered**. The suspect **was arrested** the next day and **is being interviewed** today.*

## Common mistakes (Spanish speakers)

- Using **"se"** structures: ❌ *It was stolen the car* → ✅ *The car **was stolen***.
- ❌ *He was arrest* → ✅ *He was **arrested*** (participle!).
- ❌ *The victim was hospitalised by the ambulance* → ✅ *was **taken** to hospital **by** ambulance*.
- Overusing the passive in your own statement so nobody knows who did what.

## Summary

1. Passive = be + past participle.
2. Agent with *by* only when useful.
3. Used for unknown doers, results, and in legal texts.
4. In your own statement, prefer active: I arrested / I seized.
5. *had her bag stolen* = something happened to her.
